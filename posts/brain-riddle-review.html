Brain Riddle Review: Tricky Puzzles (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Brain Riddle: Tricky Box is a genuinely clever doodle puzzle game: guide the little box hero Boxy to the door, but never trust the obvious route — every level hides a trap, a twist, or a joke. Three modes, offline play and a charming hand-drawn world make it easy to recommend, if you can live with heavy ads and a chunky 302 MB download.

Brain Riddle: Tricky Box, from developer Brainteaser Puzzle Game Studio, launched on Google Play in April 2026 and has already passed 500,000+ installs — with around 590,000 installs tracked in total, about 140,000 of them in the last 30 days (roughly 4,500 a day). On Google Play it holds 4.5 stars from about 3,700–3,900 ratings, and AppBrain flags it as both “Growing fast” and “Loved by users”. That combination — a fresh puzzle idea, a fast-growing audience and a rating most puzzle games would envy — is why it earns a review here this week.

The premise is disarmingly simple: Boxy wants to get through the door. The catch is that the door is almost never reached the way you first expect. One level asks “How do I get through that door?” and the answer turns out to be standing on a pressure plate; another freezes the door solid until you melt the ice; a third makes you knock politely first (“Show some respect?”). The developer's own pitch sums it up: “Think you're smart? Think again!” — common sense, the game warns, is your biggest enemy.

How it plays

Tricky Box actually packs three games into one doodle world. In Main Story mode you tap, drag and interact with objects to find the clever way to the door — the classic troll-puzzle format where the solution is lateral, not logical. In Magic Pencil mode you draw lines to dodge traps, block falling objects and protect Boxy from danger: one well-placed line can save the day, and one careless one can doom him. And in Run! Run! mode the game turns into a fast troll platformer, where you run and jump through levels packed with hidden traps and sudden surprises, and the door that looked two steps away turns out to be a trick.

Stuck? A hint system offers clever clues rather than full solutions, so the “aha!” moment stays yours. And because the whole game works offline, it is a natural travel companion — no Wi-Fi needed on a commute, a flight, or anywhere the signal drops. The hand-drawn notebook style ties it all together: sketchy lines, playful colours, funny sound effects and a world that looks like it was doodled in the margins of a school book.

Why it is trending

Tricky Box is trending because it understands what makes troll puzzles shareable: every level is a small joke with a punchline you have to discover yourself. Players praise exactly that. One five-star reviewer says it is the first game they have ever given five stars to, calling the levels beautifully designed; another says the puzzles are challenging yet “so, so simple” once the trick clicks. At roughly 4,500 installs a day over the last 30 days, it is growing the way a game does when players keep telling friends about the level that fooled them.

It also helps that the game respects the format's audience. The puzzles are short, the goal is always visible, and the doodle art makes even a failed attempt feel light rather than punishing. Version 1.16.0 (updated 11 October 2026) keeps the content fresh, and the game runs on Android 7.0 and up, so it reaches older phones as well as new ones.

Key features

  • Three modes in one game — Main Story puzzles, Magic Pencil drawing challenges, and Run! Run! troll platformer levels.
  • Troll-puzzle humour — every level hides a trap or twist, and the obvious answer is usually wrong.
  • Magic Pencil drawing — sketch lines, paths and barriers to protect Boxy from danger.
  • Hand-drawn doodle art — a sketchbook world with playful sounds and witty effects.
  • Hint system — clever clues when you are stuck, without spoiling the solution outright.
  • Fully offline play — no Wi-Fi needed, ideal for travel and commutes.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.
  • Regular updates — version 1.16.0 arrived 11 October 2026, with steady updates since launch.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Three genuinely different modes — story puzzles, drawing challenges and troll platforming — in one free game
  • Strong 4.5 stars from about 3,700 ratings, with 140,000 installs in the last 30 days alone
  • Clever, funny level design where the twist is the reward — players call the puzzles beautifully designed
  • Charming doodle art and a hint system that nudges rather than spoils
  • Full offline play and an Everyone content rating, on Android 7.0 and up
Cons
  • Contains ads, and reviewers say they get heavier as you progress — one reports an ad before every retry after failing a level
  • About 302 MB — a chunky download for a puzzle game, so grab it on Wi-Fi
  • One reviewer found the platformer-mode joystick controls fiddly and hard to press
  • Hint reliance can frustrate: some puzzles only click after a clue, which breaks the flow
  • No in-app purchase price range was shown in the listing data checked, so treat any paid offer inside the app on its own merits

Pricing

Brain Riddle: Tricky Box is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The listing data checked for this review does not show an in-app purchase price range, though the app does request in-app billing permission, so check any paid offer inside the app before accepting it. The current version is 1.16.0, updated 11 October 2026, and the download is about 302 MB — large for a puzzle game, but fair for three modes' worth of content.
