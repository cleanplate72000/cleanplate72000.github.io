Meowdoku Review: Cat Sudoku Puzzle (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Meowdoku is a genuinely clever puzzle: Sudoku-style logic crossed with Minesweeper deduction, wrapped in cats. It earns its #1 free puzzle ranking in Japan, Canada, Italy and more — but go in knowing the ads are heavy, with players reporting a forced ad after almost every level.

Meowdoku: Brain Puzzle Games, from developer Oakever Games (package com.oakever.meowdoku), is not another match-three clone. Its pitch is simple and, once you play it, surprisingly addictive: find the perfect "exclusive territory" for every aloof little cat on the board.

The numbers explain why it is suddenly everywhere. Meowdoku holds 4.7 stars from more than 530,000 ratings on Google Play, shows 28 million total installs, and AppBrain tracks roughly 10 million installs in recent weeks alone. It sits at #1 in the Top Free Puzzle chart in Japan, Brazil, Sweden, Italy, Canada and Poland, and was #1 in US Top Free Games from late September to mid-October 2026. The game was updated on 22 October 2026 (version 1.21.0 on the store listings) and is free to play, with optional Premium subscriptions listed in the store.

How Meowdoku works

The board is divided into coloured sections, and the rules are elegant: place exactly one cat in each section, and cats cannot share the same row or column, or touch each other — not even diagonally. That last rule is the Minesweeper part. You are not just filling a grid; you are eliminating squares, reading the board, and proving where a cat must go. Double-tap a tile to place a cat, and you have only three hearts — three mistakes — per puzzle, so guessing your way through does not work for long.

Meowdoku's board at a glance: one cat per coloured section, with no two cats sharing a row, column or diagonal touch.

Beyond the main puzzles, Meowdoku offers daily puzzles every day, global leaderboards for fastest completion times, and offline play — no internet required once the game is installed. That offline mode is a real plus for commutes, queues and coffee breaks, and the minimalist look keeps the focus on the logic rather than on flashy effects.

Key features

  • Sudoku-style logic crossed with Minesweeper deduction — one cat per coloured section, no row, column or diagonal contact.
  • Three hearts per puzzle, so every placement has to be calculated, not guessed.
  • Daily puzzles and global leaderboards for fastest times.
  • Offline play with no internet required.
  • Minimalist, cat-themed presentation with simple tap controls.

Pros and cons

Pros: genuinely original puzzle idea; easy to learn, hard to master; daily puzzles and leaderboards; offline play; huge, active player base at 4.7 stars.

Cons: ads are the big downside — many players report a forced, unrewarded ad after almost every level, some lasting 30 seconds or more, and no clear ad-free option found by those players; the store page claims "non-intrusive ads", which does not match those reports. One player also reported a misleading security-style ad popup, though the developer replied that Meowdoku itself is safe and ads can be skipped without paying or downloading anything. If an ad ever redirects you elsewhere, close it and return to the game — never enter personal or banking details from an in-game ad.

Pricing

Meowdoku is free to download and play on Google Play, with ads and optional Premium subscriptions available in the store for players who want extra features. There is no cash-payout or "earn money" system in this game — it is a pure puzzle game, and any ad promising otherwise is not part of Meowdoku itself.

Who should download it?

If you like Sudoku, Minesweeper, or quiet logic puzzles, Meowdoku is an easy recommendation — especially offline, in short sessions. If forced ads ruin a game for you, try the free version first and see how the ad frequency feels before committing to a subscription.
