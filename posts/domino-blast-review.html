Domino Blast Review: Chain Reactions (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Domino Blast: Block Puzzle is a bright, easy-to-learn block puzzle with one genuinely clever twist: complete a line that holds a domino and the whole board can erupt in a chain reaction. It is brand new, very well rated so far, and free — but it is also a 127 MB download with plenty of ads, so it is best for puzzle fans who do not mind ad breaks.

Domino Blast: Block Puzzle, from developer Loyalty Gaming, is one of the fastest-rising new strategy games on Google Play in October 2026. It reached number 1 in the Top New Free Strategy chart in Canada, and also ranks in the top few in the United States, France, Germany, Italy, Spain and the United Kingdom. AppBrain puts it at about 80,000 installs, all in the last 30 days — roughly 3,400 a day — after launching in early October 2026. On the live Google Play listing it is rated 4.6 stars from about 4,950 ratings, it is rated Everyone, and it is free to download. The listing says it contains ads, including optional reward ads; no in-app purchase prices were listed on the Play page checked for this review. The latest version is 1.3, updated 14 October 2026, and the app is about 127 MB.

The core idea is a block puzzle crossed with a domino run, and it takes about a minute to understand. You drag block pieces from a tray onto an 8x8 board and try to fill a full row or column. The twist is that the dominoes standing on the board count as filled cells too. Complete a line that contains a domino and that domino tips over along its arrow, smashing every block in its path and knocking over the next domino. Chains can turn corners, and fallen dominoes can complete even more lines, so one well-placed piece can clear far more than the single line you aimed at.

That chain mechanic is what separates Domino Blast from the many plain block puzzles on Play. In a standard block puzzle, the skill is almost entirely spatial — finding a home for awkward shapes before the board fills up. Here, placement has a second layer: you are also setting up dominoes so that a later line clear fires them in a useful direction. A domino pointing into a crowded corner is worth more than the same domino pointing at empty space, and rainbow dominoes, which tip in every direction, are the most satisfying pieces on the board. The listing screenshots show the payoff clearly, with on-screen counters such as “Topple x9” and “Topple x15” as chains roll across the board.

How to play

Each level gives you a goal rather than asking you to survive forever: free every domino on the board, or smash a target number of blocks. Drag a piece from the tray onto the board, fill a full row or column to clear it, and let any dominoes in that line do their work. If no piece fits, the level ends — unless you call in a Domino Drop, the game's rescue move. Later levels add ice blocks, bombs and stone walls, and boss levels promise giant chains, with a fresh world look every 40 levels. Reward chests arrive every few levels, and the whole game plays offline, although ads and ad rewards need a connection.

Key features

  • Domino chain reactions: lines containing a domino fire it along its arrow, smashing blocks and toppling further dominoes.
  • 8x8 block puzzle board: drag pieces from the tray, fill rows and columns, and keep space for the next pieces.
  • Level goals, not endless survival: free every domino or hit a block-smashing target to clear the level.
  • Rainbow dominoes: tip in every direction, making them the best chain starters on the board.
  • Boss levels and new worlds: giant chains on boss levels, with a fresh look every 40 levels.
  • Ice, bombs and stone walls: new obstacles join the board as you progress.
  • Reward chests: a chest every few levels, plus optional reward ads.
  • Offline play: no Wi-Fi needed for the game itself, though ads and rewards need a connection.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely fresh twist on the block puzzle; simple to learn in seconds; short levels that suit quick sessions; bright, easy-to-read colours; offline play; and a strong early rating from thousands of players.

Cons: ads are the biggest complaint in early player reviews, with some players reporting ad breaks that arrive far too often during play; it is a chunky 127 MB download for a puzzle game; and as a game launched in October 2026, it is still early — long-term balance, later levels and update quality are unproven. One early reviewer also reported crashes and slow performance, so older or lower-end phones should try it before committing much time.

Pricing and a note on in-game coins

Domino Blast is free to play, and the Play listing flags ads, including optional reward ads. No in-app purchase prices were listed on the Play page checked for this review. A note worth making plainly: the listing promises in-game coins, chests and rewards — not real money. Some player comments online talk about “earning”, but that refers to in-game currency, and nothing on the official Play listing offers cash payouts. If you play it, play it for the puzzle, not for earnings.

Is it worth downloading?

If you like block puzzles and you want one with a bit more drama than clearing rows, Domino Blast is an easy recommendation — the chain reactions are satisfying, the levels are short, and the early player rating is strong. If you hate ads, though, go in with your eyes open: this is a free, ad-supported game, and several early reviewers say the ad frequency is its weakest point. For a calmer, ad-light-feeling alternative in the same puzzle family, try our Tiledom review.
