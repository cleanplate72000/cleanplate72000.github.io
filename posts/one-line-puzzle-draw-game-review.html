One Line Puzzle Review: Draw It (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. One Line Puzzle: Draw Game is not one puzzle but a whole drawer full of them: more than 20 quick mini-games built around drawing a line, a bridge or a route, plus rescue, sorting, parking and maze rounds, all inside a single free app. It is at its best in short bursts — one clever line, one small win, then move on. It is held back by advertising, a modest rating sample for an app with 830,000 installs, and the fact that no single mode here is deep enough to carry the app on its own.

One Line Puzzle: Draw Game, from developer qdd game, is a casual mini-game collection for Android. Its headline mode is Thief Puzzle, the one featured in the app icon and store artwork: draw a route that helps a cartoon thief reach the target while avoiding the guard, the laser or the obstacle in the way. When that style stops appealing, you go back to the central menu and pick something else entirely — a different rule, a different objective, a different two-minute challenge.

The numbers show a game on the rise. Independent tracker AppBrain lists it on its trending free casual games page for late October 2026, with about 57,000 installs in 7 days and about 76,000 installs in the last 30 days, roughly 2,500 a day, taking it to about 830,000 installs in total. It ranks in the top 100 free casual games in several countries, including Brazil, Mexico, France, Canada and the United States. The live Google Play listing shows about 4.3 stars from 494 ratings, and Play rates it Everyone. The current version is 1.20.23, updated 13 October 2026, the download is about 102 MB, and it has been on Play since February 2026. It is free to download and contains ads.

The pitch in one image: draw a line, outsmart the guard, grab the prize. The small cards along the bottom hint at the wider collection hiding behind the hub.

How it actually plays

Every mode in the collection is built around one simple touch action — tap, drag, draw or aim — and one simple rule you can learn in seconds. In the drawing modes you sketch the missing piece: a line that becomes a ramp for a car, a bridge over a gap, a route past a guard, or a single stroke that completes a shape. In Fill the Glass and Water Sort you guide water or sort colours into the right container. In the rescue and stickman modes you help a character escape trouble, often by drawing the one barrier or path that changes the outcome. Car Parking, Rope Hero, Maze for Cat and Ball Blast round out the set with driving, aiming, maze and arcade-style rounds.

That variety is the whole point, and it is also the trade-off. Because you are never locked into one puzzle style, a mode that frustrates you is never a dead end: back to the hub, pick another card, and you are playing something different within seconds. But it also means the collection is a mile wide and an inch deep. The drawing puzzles are satisfying, but they are short, and the later stages in any one mode matter less than they would in a dedicated puzzle game, because the next mode is always one tap away. If you want one game to master over weeks, this is not it. If you want a pocketful of quick puzzles for a commute or a coffee break, the format fits perfectly.

Draw one line, save the car: the drawn pink line carries the red car over the spikes to the chequered flag. One good stroke is all a level needs.

Why it is trending

One Line Puzzle is trending for the same reason mini-game collections keep climbing the charts: every mode is instantly understandable from a single screenshot. A thief behind bars, a car above spikes, a glass waiting for water — you know what to do before you read a word of the description. That readability travels well in store charts and short clips, and it helps explain the October rise: about 57,000 installs in a week, four times the week before on AppBrain's trending casual list, with top-100 free casual rankings in Brazil, Mexico, France, Canada and the United States.

It also helps that the collection never asks for a big commitment. Sessions are deliberately short, the controls are one finger, and there is no long tutorial standing between you and the first win. Players who like drawing puzzles get their fix immediately, and players who get bored of one style can switch without downloading another app. The trade-off is that the collection leans on quantity: with more than 20 modes sharing one app, polish varies, and a few modes will feel thinner than the headline thief puzzles.

A museum-heist round: the thief ducks the red laser on a drawn pink line, past the framed painting. The cartoon mischief is slapstick, not realistic — worth knowing if younger children will play.

Key features

  • More than 20 mini-games in one free app, from a single hub menu
  • Drawing and one-line puzzles: Thief Puzzle, Draw Bridge, Draw Stickman, One Stroke and One Line Draw
  • Rescue and stickman modes: Stickman Thief, Master Thief, Stickman Rescue, Stickman Ninja and Save the Puppy
  • Sorting, matching and water puzzles: Line Match, Water Sort and Fill the Glass
  • Parking, aiming, maze and action rounds: Car Parking, Rope Hero, Maze for Cat and Ball Blast
  • Short sessions built for quick breaks, with simple tap, drag and draw controls
  • Everyone content rating on Google Play, with no account or sign-up needed to play

Fill the Glass: water follows the drawn pink line down into the waiting glass. The water modes are the calmest corner of the collection.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Real variety: more than 20 modes in one download, so boredom has an easy exit
  • Instantly learnable — every mode is one rule, one finger, one quick round
  • Strong trending momentum: about 830,000 installs and top-100 casual rankings in several countries
  • Free, with an Everyone rating, and short sessions that suit quick breaks
  • The drawing mechanic itself is genuinely satisfying when a single line solves the level
Cons
  • Contains ads, which will interrupt a collection built for quick sessions
  • No single mode is deep — this is a sampler, not a game to master over weeks
  • Small rating sample: about 494 ratings on Play despite 830,000 installs, so the 4.3-star average is less settled than bigger games' scores
  • The thief theme is cartoon mischief, but parents should know it is there before handing it to young children
  • About 102 MB is chunky for a casual collection — use Wi-Fi for the download
  • Store artwork is promotional art, so individual levels will look plainer than the key art suggests

Pricing

One Line Puzzle: Draw Game is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The Play listing shows a price of $0, and no in-app purchase range is advertised on the listing checked for this review — so the cost of playing is your time and the ads between rounds. Given that, the free version is the whole proposition: try the thief puzzles and the water rounds first, and if the ad breaks bother you in short sessions, you will know quickly. The current version is 1.20.23, updated 13 October 2026, and the download is about 102 MB.
