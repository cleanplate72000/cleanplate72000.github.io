Harbor Blast Review: Smash Puzzles (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Harbor Blast is a bright, instantly understandable physics puzzler: aim a harbour cannon, find the weak spot, and watch a whole stack of blocks collapse in one satisfying chain reaction. It loses half a star because it is still a young game — a modest install base, a chunky 103 MB download, and at least one player report that the cannon can feel weak, taking out only one or two items per shot.

Harbor Blast, from developer Flyfox Games, is a casual physics puzzle game set in colourful 3D harbour scenes. The official Play description sums up the loop in one line: load your cannon, choose the right angle, and blast your way through harbour scenes, smashing blocks, breaking objects, knocking down structures and triggering spectacular chain reactions. There is no complicated story to learn and no long tutorial to sit through — you point, you fire, and the physics does the entertaining part.

Why review it now? Because it is growing fast from a small base. AppBrain's tracking puts Harbor Blast at about 29,000 installs in total, with roughly 24,000 of those arriving in the last 30 days — around 810 new players a day — and the live Play listing shows 4.83 stars from 2,246 ratings. That combination, a new game with a very high rating and most of its downloads still to come, is exactly the kind of early breakout worth flagging before it becomes obvious. It launched on Google Play in September 2026, and version 1.0.4, dated 29 September 2026, is the current release checked for this review.

What you actually do

Each level puts a structure on a platform in front of your cannon and gives you a limited number of balls — the screenshots show counts of 20 and 28 balls, so every shot has to earn its place. You adjust your aim, pick your angle, and fire. Blocks break, bottles topple, crates slide, and if you hit the right spot, one collapse drags the rest of the structure down with it. That chain reaction is the game's real reward: a single well-placed ball that clears a whole tower feels far better than three wild shots that chip away at the edges.

The Play description breaks the appeal into five ideas, and they all show up in the screenshots. First, aim, shoot and smash: simple cannon control with no fiddly power meter to master. Second, satisfying physics destruction, with smooth 3D animation as materials break and structures fall. Third, clever physics puzzles, where you study the structure, spot a hidden weak point, and plan a shot that sets off a cascade. Fourth, chain reactions, where knocking down one object hits another and the whole scene comes crashing down. And fifth, boosters and power-ups, usable before or during a level when a setup looks too stubborn to crack on its own.

Progression comes through coins and special coin levels. Complete a level, collect coins, and take on the coin levels that appear as you move through the game. The harbour setting stays consistent throughout — palm trees, lighthouses, boats and snowy rooftops in the background — with what the developer calls subtle pirate-inspired elements, but the focus never drifts from the puzzle in front of you. Levels are quick to pick up and put down, which makes this an easy game for short breaks rather than long sessions.

Key features

  • One-tap cannon aiming: choose your angle, fire, and watch the physics play out — no complicated controls.
  • Physics-based destruction: blocks, bottles, crates and structures all react differently to each cannonball.
  • Weak-spot puzzles: study each structure, find the load-bearing block, and trigger a chain reaction.
  • Boosters and power-ups: helpful tools available before or during a level for the trickier setups.
  • Coins and coin levels: complete levels, collect coins, and take on special coin challenges as you progress.
  • Colourful 3D harbour worlds: bright seaside scenes with pirate-inspired details, built for quick play sessions.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Instantly understandable: aim, fire, watch it collapse
  • Chain reactions are genuinely satisfying when a plan comes together
  • Very high player rating: 4.83 stars from over 2,200 ratings
  • Fast-growing: most of its installs arrived in the last month
  • Rated Everyone, so it is suitable for the whole family
  • Quick levels that work well in short play sessions
Cons
  • Still a young game with a modest total install base
  • 103 MB download is large for a casual puzzle game
  • One player reports the cannon can feel weak, clearing only one or two items per shot
  • In-app purchase details are not shown on the Play listing checked
  • Boosters can make later levels feel less about pure aiming skill

Pricing and data

Harbor Blast is free to download from Google Play, with the Play listing showing a price of $0. The listing checked does not display in-app purchase prices, so check the in-game shop before spending — boosters and power-ups are the obvious place money could go, and it is worth knowing what they cost before you lean on them. On the wider picture, this is a casual game, not a cash-earning app: there is no payout, no withdrawal and no promised reward here, just coins earned by clearing levels. Anyone downloading it expecting to earn money will be disappointed, and that is a good thing — it keeps the game honest about what it is.

One honest warning from the player reviews: a four-star reviewer says the cannon can feel weak, clearing only one or two items per shot, which makes some levels drag. Treat that as a player report rather than a verified fact — most reviewers are enthusiastic, with one five-star player calling out the strategic play on some levels and another praising the colours and music — but it is a useful tempering note for anyone expecting every shot to level a tower. If you like working out the perfect angle, that limitation is part of the puzzle; if you want pure, effortless destruction, it may frustrate you.

Who should download it?

If you like physics puzzlers where one smart shot beats ten careless ones — the same crowd that enjoys Knock Away — Harbor Blast is an easy recommendation: it is bright, quick to learn, rated Everyone, and its chain reactions are the satisfying kind. If your phone is short on storage, remember the 103 MB download first. And if you prefer games with years of levels already built up, this one is still early — but that is also the fun of catching it now, while it is climbing.

Bottom line: a colourful, clever little cannon puzzler with a very high rating and real momentum — aim carefully, find the weak spot, and enjoy watching the whole harbour stack come down.
