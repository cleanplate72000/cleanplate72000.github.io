Miyamoto First Gamer to Win Japan's Honour

Mario creator Shigeru Miyamoto is to receive Japan's Order of Culture — and he is the first person from the video game industry ever to be given the honour.

Why this matters: Japan's highest cultural honour has now gone to a game maker for the first time, putting video games on the same honours list as literature, science and the traditional arts.

Miyamoto Order of Culture: the key facts

  • The honour: Japan's fiscal 2026 Order of Culture, announced by the government on October 23, 2026. Miyamoto, 73, is the first person from the video game industry to receive it.
  • One of eight: Miyamoto is one of eight recipients this year, alongside figures including lighting designer Motoko Ishii and Nobel Prize-winning chemist Kenso Soai.
  • The ceremony: the Order of Culture will be presented by the Emperor at the Imperial Palace in Tokyo on November 3, Japan's Culture Day.
  • The man: Miyamoto is Nintendo's executive fellow and the creator of Mario. He joined Nintendo in 1977 and went on to work on Donkey Kong, Super Mario Bros., The Legend of Zelda and Pikmin.
  • Not his first first: in 2019 he was named a Person of Cultural Merit, also a first for the games industry.
  • His response: at a press conference at Nintendo's Kyoto headquarters, Miyamoto said he accepted the honour on behalf of everyone who helped make the games, stressing that games are made by many people working together, not by one person alone.

Why it matters

For decades, games have argued for a place beside film, music and books as culture. This is that recognition at the very top: the Japanese state itself has now listed a game creator among its most distinguished cultural figures. It also fits Miyamoto's career — his characters, from Mario to Link, have reached far beyond games into films and theme parks, while still starting from the same simple, playful ideas.
