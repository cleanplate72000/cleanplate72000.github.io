Bus Parade Review: Traffic Jam Puzzle (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Bus Parade is a bright, genuinely clever little puzzle: a crowded car park, colour-coded passengers, and one clear job — get the right bus out in the right order. It is at its best in short bursts, it is free, and it carries an Everyone rating. The caveats are real but small: it is brand new with only a few hundred ratings so far, it contains ads, and at around 128 MB it is a chunkier download than most puzzle games.

Bus Parade, from developer Brainbuff Club, is a bus parking puzzle that has climbed fast since its launch in October 2026. On its official Google Play listing it holds 4.8 stars from 250 ratings, with 17,000+ installs — all of them in the last 30 days, at roughly 2,300 installs a day — and an Everyone content rating. It has reached number 19 in India's Top New Free Puzzle chart, and the top 30 for new free puzzle games in Austria, Belgium, Switzerland, the Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, Norway and Slovakia. It is free to download, contains ads, is about 128 MB in size, and its current version is 1.0.0, released on 15 October 2026.

The idea is easy to grasp and harder to master. The parking lot is packed, and passengers are waiting in colour-coded queues. You tap a bus to move it out of the traffic, but only a bus with a clear path can leave — and only a bus that matches the waiting passengers' colour does any good. A bus may be the right colour but stuck behind another vehicle, while a bus with a clear path may not be the one you need next. So every move is a small decision: look at the whole lot, work out what is blocking the bus you want, and move something else first to open the route.

The key art sums up the whole game: colour-matched buses, colour-matched passengers, and a city street to untangle.

The Play Store description breaks the experience into four ideas. First, untangle the parking jam: start with the vehicles that can move, then work out what needs to leave to free the bus you need — removing one obstacle can reveal an opening that was easy to miss. Second, match colours with a purpose: getting a bus out is only part of the job, because the waiting passengers need a ride that matches their colour, so your plan has to connect the buses in the lot with the passengers waiting to board. Third, think a few moves ahead: spot the chain of vehicles blocking your target, then decide how to make space for it, one small decision at a time. Fourth, watch the lot clear, one bus at a time: a blocked bus gets its turn, passengers find their ride, and a tangled lot starts to flow.

Level 8 shows the core loop in action: the passenger queues at the top set the colours you need, and the jammed lot below is the puzzle you have to solve.

Key features

  • Colour-matching bus puzzles: match each bus to passengers of the same colour to clear the board.
  • Parking-jam strategy: work out the right order to move vehicles so blocked buses can escape.
  • Simple tap controls: no complicated gestures — tap a bus, watch it move, plan the next one.
  • Levels that build in difficulty: later levels pack the lot tighter and demand longer chains of moves.
  • Bright, cheerful presentation: colourful buses, chunky passengers and a clean city look.
  • Free to play: free download on Google Play, with ads supporting the game.

Level 4, "Fill the Buses!", is an early example of the planning the game asks for: several buses are boxed in, and the order you free them in is everything.

Who is it for?

This is a game for puzzle fans who like thinking a few moves ahead without a timer breathing down their neck: colour-sorting players, parking-jam fans, and anyone who wants a satisfying five-minute brain teaser. It sits nicely alongside other recent puzzle hits — if you enjoy untangling a board one move at a time, our Maze Out! review covers another fast-rising puzzle game in the same spirit. What it is not is a deep, long-form game: version 1.0.0 is a fresh release, the level count and long-term progression are still unproven, and one early reviewer complained that the ads felt longer than the gameplay itself. If heavy ads ruin a puzzle for you, that is worth knowing before you download.

Pros

  • Colour matching gives every move a clear purpose
  • Everyone rating, free to download
  • Fast-growing: 17,000 installs in its first two weeks, 4.8 stars
  • Cheerful visuals and simple tap controls
Cons
  • Contains ads — one early player found them heavy
  • Still a small rating base (250 ratings), so the score may move
  • Large for a puzzle game at around 128 MB
  • Brand-new release, so long-term content is unproven

Pricing

Bus Parade is free to download on Google Play (listed at 0 USD), and the listing confirms it contains ads. No subscription or paid edition is mentioned on the official listing, so the cost of playing is simply putting up with the occasional advert. At around 128 MB it is a heavier download than many puzzle games, so it is worth grabbing on Wi-Fi if your data is tight.

Download Bus Parade on Google Play

The bottom line: Bus Parade takes two familiar ideas — colour matching and parking-jam puzzles — and combines them into something that genuinely makes you think. The first levels teach the rules in seconds, the later ones demand real planning, and the whole thing is free and family-safe. Expect ads and a new game still finding its feet, and you will enjoy it for exactly what it is: a bright, brain-teasing traffic jam that is well worth clearing, and one of the fastest-growing puzzle games of October 2026.
