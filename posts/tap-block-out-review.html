Tap Block Out Review: Color Logic (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.4 / 5. Tap Block Out: Color Puzzle is a calm, clever logic game with one clear rule: send every coloured block out through the exit of its own colour, in the right order. No timer, no frantic tapping, just a board that rewards thinking one move ahead. It is let down by ads and a chunky download, and a few reviewers report annoying glitches, but the core puzzle is genuinely satisfying.

Tap Block Out: Color Puzzle, from developer funsmith, is a tap-to-slide colour puzzle. Each board has four exits, one in each colour, and a set of interlocking coloured blocks. Tap a block and it slides out through its matching exit — provided nothing is in its way. Tap a blocked block and nothing moves, so the real game is not speed, it is order: which block has to leave first so the rest can follow?

The numbers show a game on a sharp rise. Independent tracker AppBrain counts about 350,000 installs in total, with 230,000 of those in the last 30 days — roughly 7,600 a day — and about 110,000 installs in the last 7 days, about four times the week before, which put it on AppBrain's trending puzzle list on 26 October 2026. The live Google Play listing shows 4.8 stars from over 3,500 ratings, and the game has reached the top 10 free puzzle charts in the Czech Republic and the top 15 in Italy and Slovakia. Google Play rates it Everyone, the current version is 1.9.4, updated 16 October 2026, and the download is about 136 MB.

The pitch in one image: two boards full of interlocking coloured blocks, each with a matching coloured exit. The whole game is reading a board like this before you tap.

How it actually plays

The Play description lays out the loop plainly. You look at the board, work out which blocks are free to move and which are trapped behind others, and tap in an order that clears a path for the rest. A block that is blocked stays put, so a careless first tap can waste a move — and moves are limited, with the listing screenshots showing a 30-move budget on the levels pictured. That limit is what turns a relaxing-looking puzzle into a proper logic test: you cannot just tap everything and hope.

There is no timer. The Play listing stresses this twice, and it matters: you can stare at a Super Hard board for as long as you like, try a sequence in your head, and only tap when you are sure. Daily levels at higher difficulty are also promised for players who want a harder test, and the soft, bright block style with ASMR-like tap sounds is pitched as a stress-relief experience rather than a high-score chase. In practice, that makes it a good short-session game — one board while waiting, or several in a quiet evening — rather than a game that demands long play sessions.

A clearer board with 30 moves on the clock — in moves, not seconds. The two buttons at the bottom are help props, for when the right order is not obvious.

Why it is trending

Tap Block Out is trending because its rule is instantly readable. Anyone who looks at a board understands the goal within seconds — matching colours to exits — yet the boards themselves are not trivial, because a block can only leave when its path is clear. That gap between easy to learn and hard to solve cleanly is exactly what makes a puzzle game shareable: it looks simple in a screenshot, then makes you think once you play it.

Its timing helps too. It arrived on Google Play in June 2026, so it is no longer brand new, but its growth has accelerated sharply in October, with most of its installs arriving in the last month. A 4.8-star average across several thousand ratings is unusually high for a puzzle game at this scale, and its climb into top puzzle charts in several European countries suggests the growth is broad rather than a single-country spike. That said, a few thousand ratings is still a modest sample next to the biggest puzzle games, so the average could move as more players arrive.

A denser board, and the moment of decision: the yellow block is about to slide out, but only if its path is clear. One wrong order here, and the blocks behind it stay trapped.

Key features

  • Tap-to-slide colour matching: send each block to the exit of its own colour
  • Four coloured exits per board, with blocked blocks that stay put until their path clears
  • Limited moves per level, so the order of your taps matters as much as the taps themselves
  • No timer and no time pressure on any level
  • Endless unique levels, plus higher-difficulty daily levels
  • Soft, bright block visuals with ASMR-like tap sounds, pitched as stress relief
  • Help props for stuck boards, including a hint-style button and a hammer-style prop
  • Super Hard boards for players who want a longer, tougher puzzle

A Super Hard board, where the props earn their keep: the hammer smashes a block that is in the way, opening a path the other blocks could not use.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Instantly understandable rule, with real depth in the move order
  • No timer, so it suits relaxed, think-it-through play
  • Strong ratings: 4.8 stars on the live Play listing, from over 3,500 ratings
  • Endless levels plus harder daily boards, so it does not run dry quickly
  • Free to download, with an Everyone content rating
Cons
  • Big download at about 136 MB for a puzzle game — use Wi-Fi
  • Contains ads, which can interrupt a quiet puzzle session
  • One reviewer reports that watching an ad for extra moves restarted their level instead of granting the moves — treat that as one player's report, not a verified finding
  • Another reviewer reports a pop-under ad opening a browser page, and a third reports a paid no-ads purchase not being restored — again, single reports, but worth knowing before you pay for anything inside the app
  • A further reviewer complains about lengthy data-consent screens; check the Play listing's Data safety section before installing

Pricing

Tap Block Out: Color Puzzle is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The Play listing also signals in-app purchases, and one reviewer reports paying about $10 for a no-ads option, so if you are tempted by a paid offer inside the app, check exactly what it includes and whether it restores after reinstalling before you buy. The current version is 1.9.4, updated 16 October 2026, and the download is about 136 MB — chunky for a puzzle game, so download it on Wi-Fi rather than on mobile data.

Download

Download Tap Block Out: Color Puzzle on Google Play

If you like a puzzle that is calm on the surface and quietly demanding underneath, Tap Block Out is an easy one to try: a fast-climbing chart game, a high rating, and a loop — look, think, tap, clear — that rewards patience more than speed.
