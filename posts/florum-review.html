Florum Review: Plant ID & Care (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Florum is the plant app for people who keep killing houseplants and want to know why: snap a photo to identify a plant, get a care plan, and get told when to water it. It loses half a mark because the free version has limits — unlimited AI chat sits behind a subscription, and a few reviewers report subscription recognition problems — so check what is free before you pay.

Florum: AI Plant Identifier, from ANKO Solutions LLC, is a plant identification and care app that has climbed fast in Google Play's Lifestyle charts. On its official Play listing it holds 4.7 stars from more than 2,300 ratings, with 100,000+ downloads and an Everyone content rating, and AppBrain's 26 October 2026 trending list shows it growing about 63% week on week, with around 260,000 installs in total. It was updated on 24 October 2026. That combination — a genuinely useful camera feature, a clear care routine, and a very high rating for a young app — is why it is trending, and it is worth asking what you actually get for free.

All screenshots in this review are the developer's own promotional images from the official Google Play Store listing.

What Florum does

Florum is built around one simple idea: most plant problems start with not knowing what the plant is, or what it needs. Point your phone at a leaf, flower, tree, weed or wildflower and the app identifies it in seconds, then builds a care plan around that plant. That plan covers watering, feeding, lighting, pruning and repotting, so a new purchase or a struggling plant comes with instructions instead of guesswork. For beginners, that is the whole appeal — you do not need to know botanical names to keep a plant alive, you just need a photo and a routine you can follow.

The second half of the app is the care routine itself. Add your indoor and outdoor plants to a collection and Florum sends timely reminders for watering, fertilising, pruning, propagation and repotting. This matters more than it sounds: most houseplants die from too much water, not too little, and a reminder that also tells you how much water a pot needs is far more useful than a vague note to water it every week. The app's watering calculator, shown in its own screenshots, gives an amount for a specific pot — around 240 millilitres in the example shown — rather than leaving you to guess.

Identify, diagnose, and place your plants

Beyond simple identification, Florum works as a plant doctor. Photograph a sick leaf and it offers a diagnosis with a practical treatment plan, as in the screenshot where yellowing leaves are flagged as possible overwatering, with the advice to check the soil first. That kind of specific, checkable advice is what separates a useful plant app from a novelty camera filter: it does not just name the plant, it tells you what might be wrong and what to do next. The listing also promises unlimited disease checks and rich botanical content, alongside personal consultations with gardening experts for subscribers.

Placement is the third genuinely useful feature. Florum includes a built-in light meter: hold your phone near a pot, measure the surrounding light, and the app tells you whether the spot suits the plant. The screenshot shows a parlour palm rated as a good spot in soft, indirect light, with a reading of about 4,500 lux, and the advice to keep it away from harsh, direct sun. For anyone who has watched a plant fade on a sunny windowsill, that is practical, testable information — move the pot, take another reading, and see the difference. It also explains why the same plant thrives in one room and struggles in another.

Pros and cons

What is good: photo identification for plants, flowers, trees, weeds and wildflowers; personalised care plans with watering, feeding, lighting, pruning and repotting steps; reminders for a whole collection; a light meter that gives a real lux reading; disease diagnosis with treatment advice; and an Everyone rating, so it is suitable for family use. The 4.7-star rating across more than 2,300 reviews is strong for an app only a few months into rapid growth, and several reviewers praise it as an innovative way to keep track of a growing plant collection.

What to watch: Florum is free to install with in-app purchases, and the free version has limits. The developer says plant identification, reminders and care tips do not require payment, but unlimited AI chat is subscription-only, with a daily message limit on the free tier that resets every 24 hours. A few reviewers report paying for a yearly subscription and then being told it was not recognised; the developer has replied to those reviews calling it a technical bug and asking users to contact support with their user ID for manual activation. That is reassuring in tone, but it means you should test the free version properly before subscribing, and keep your purchase receipt. Also note the data-safety section on Play: the app may share photos and videos with third parties, and collects financial and app-activity data — worth knowing before you upload lots of personal photos.

Pricing

Florum is free to download on Google Play, with in-app purchases for a subscription. The Play listing does not publish a subscription price on the store page we checked, so we are not quoting one here — check the price shown inside the app before you confirm any payment, and check whether it renews automatically. The core loop — identify a plant, get its care plan, and set reminders — is usable without paying, which is the right way to judge whether the subscription is worth it for you.

Who should download it?

Florum suits new plant owners, people with a growing indoor jungle, and anyone who has inherited a plant with no label. It also suits parents and teachers who want a safe, Everyone-rated way to explore nature with children — with one useful safety touch in the app itself, which flags plants such as hydrangea as harmful to pets and children. If you already keep a detailed plant journal and know your plants by name, the identification feature adds less, but the light meter and watering reminders may still earn their place. If you like hands-on, beginner-friendly guides, our SewPal review covers a similar learn-a-skill app for sewing, with the same friendly, step-by-step approach.

Final verdict

Florum earns its trending spot. Photo identification, a care plan, watering reminders, a light meter and a plant doctor in one free app is a genuinely useful bundle, and the 4.7-star rating and fast weekly growth suggest many new plant owners agree. The subscription limits and the reported recognition bug are real caveats, and the data-sharing disclosure deserves a read — but neither stops the free version from being worth trying. Start free, test it on your own plants for a week, and only subscribe if the unlimited chat and expert consultations matter to you.
