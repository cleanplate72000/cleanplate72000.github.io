Ring Tangle Review: Untangle Rings (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Ring Tangle is a calm, clever little puzzle game: spin colourful rings and linked shapes until every piece sits in the right place and the tangle comes free. It is easy to learn, genuinely relaxing, and already a chart-topper for new puzzle games — just expect ads in a free game, and puzzles that get properly tricky after the first few dozen levels.

Ring Tangle - Circle Puzzle, from developer Echidna Games, launched on Google Play in October 2026 and has already climbed to 50,000+ installs — all of them in its first few weeks, at roughly 2,900 installs a day. On Google Play it holds an outstanding 4.9 stars from over 3,000 ratings, and it has topped the Top New Free Puzzle chart in Germany and Japan, reaching #3 in the United States and #3 in France as well. For a puzzle game barely three weeks old, that is a remarkable debut — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The idea is beautifully simple. Each level starts with a tangled arrangement of colourful rings, loops and linked shapes. You rotate the pieces, line up the gaps and find the open path, unlocking the structure step by step until every ring sits free. Some levels fall apart in a few satisfying moves; others demand careful looking, pattern recognition and a little experimentation. There is no timer breathing down your neck and no frantic tapping — just you, the tangle, and the small pleasure of watching a knotted mess resolve into order.

How it plays

Controls are simple tap-and-drag: grab a ring, spin it, and see how the structure responds. The game teaches itself — the first levels are gentle, single-ring affairs, and new shapes arrive gradually: interlocking loops, frames, bars and connectors that have to be freed in the right order. The promo screenshots show the range well: an early Level 20 puzzle with a few orange and yellow rings and green bars, a busier Level 80 with stacked shapes and metal connectors, and a dense Level 201 grid of multicoloured loops that looks like a bowl of neon spaghetti. The difficulty curve is the honest kind: it grows with you, rather than spiking to force a purchase.

That calm pacing is the game's real selling point. The developer pitches it as a “calm and rewarding brain-training experience”, and the early player reviews back that up: one player calls it a “pleasant diversion” and a “great brain exercise”, while another, 25 levels in, praises the fact that the levels “do make you think” and asks the developer to keep the game as it is rather than bolting on gimmicks. For a free puzzle game, that kind of affection from players is rare — and it shows in the 4.9-star average.

Why it is trending

Ring Tangle is trending because it nails the current puzzle-game formula without the usual baggage: no lives system mentioned on the listing, no forced difficulty wall in the early game, and a visual style — chunky, glossy, colourful rings on a soft cream background — that reads instantly in a store screenshot. Its chart run tells the story: #1 Top New Free Puzzle in Germany and Japan, top three in the US and France, and a top-ten Games showing in several countries too. Word of mouth is clearly doing the heavy lifting here, with nearly 60,000 total installs tracked in under a month.

It also helps that the game respects your time. Levels are quick to play — ideal for a coffee break or a commute — and hints are available when a level truly has you stuck, so a hard puzzle is a pause, not a dead end. At about 72 MB, version 0.5.1 (updated 21 October 2026), it is a modest download that does not demand much from your phone.

Key features

  • Rotate-and-untangle gameplay — spin rings, loops and linked shapes into the right position to free each structure.
  • Spatial logic challenges — every level is a small exercise in looking carefully and finding the open path.
  • Colourful puzzle layouts — from simple rings to complex interlocking frames and shapes.
  • Simple tap-and-drag controls — easy to learn, with no fiddly gestures to master.
  • Gentle difficulty curve — puzzles grow more challenging over time, at a pace that respects new players.
  • Satisfying unlock moments — smooth animations as rings rotate into place and structures come free.
  • Helpful hints — a nudge when a tricky level has you genuinely stuck.
  • Quick levels — short puzzles you can play anytime, in a spare minute or a long session.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Outstanding 4.9 stars from over 3,000 ratings, and #1 new puzzle game in Germany and Japan
  • Calm, timer-free puzzling — relaxing rather than stressful, and genuinely good brain exercise
  • Simple tap-and-drag controls with a difficulty curve that grows fairly
  • Quick levels and hints make it easy to dip in and out without frustration
  • Bright, readable visuals — the puzzle state is always clear at a glance
  • Everyone content rating, and a modest 72 MB download
Cons
  • Contains ads, as expected from a free game — expect interruptions between levels
  • Still a young game: around 3,000 ratings is a small base, so the average may settle over time
  • No in-app purchase prices are shown on the listing, so check any paid offer inside the app before accepting
  • Later levels get genuinely hard — patient players will love it, impatient ones may bounce off
  • One game type done well, rather than a variety of modes — know what you are downloading

Pricing

Ring Tangle is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The Play listing shows a price of $0 and no in-app purchase price range, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 0.5.1, updated 21 October 2026, and the download is about 72 MB — a fair size for a polished puzzle game, and small enough to try on a whim.

Download Ring Tangle on Google Play

If you like a puzzle that rewards patience over speed, Ring Tangle is an easy recommendation: a chart-topping debut, a 4.9-star average, and a loop — spin, align, untangle — that stays satisfying well past level 200.
