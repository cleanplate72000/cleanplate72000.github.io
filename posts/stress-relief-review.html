Stress Relief Review: Calm Mini-Games (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Stress Relief - Relaxing Game is a bumper box of 40+ tiny, satisfying mini-games — wash a car, shape a pot, pop a fidget toy — with no scores to chase and no way to lose. It is free, Everyone-rated, and Google Play lists 500,000+ installs, with AppBrain counting about 130,000 of those in the last 30 days alone. The 4.0-star rating rests on just 451 ratings, so it is a fast-rising newcomer rather than a proven classic — and one mini-game in the box (a gun-sound toy) is worth knowing about before handing it to younger kids.

Stress Relief - Relaxing Game, from developer App Rack Inc., has one of the simplest pitches on Google Play: "Relax your mind & relieve stress." There is no campaign to finish, no lives to lose, and no leaderboard to climb. You open the app, pick a mini-game from the shelf, and unwind at your own pace — a minute of bubble-popping while waiting for a bus, or a longer wind-down session of pottery and sand-cutting before bed. The developer describes it as suitable for kids, teens and adults, and the Everyone content rating backs that up.

The numbers explain why it is trending. AppBrain's 24 October 2026 trending free Casual chart puts Stress Relief at number two, with about 75,000 installs in the last seven days — 27 times the week before, the biggest week-over-week jump on the whole list. Google Play shows 500,000+ installs and a 4.0-star rating from 451 ratings (the live Play fetch at writing time showed 4.04 stars), while AppBrain puts the total nearer 510,000. The app is about 218 MB (AppBrain figure) at version 1.23, and it contains ads — the price of a free collection this size.

What is actually inside the box? The Play listing promises 40+ mini-games, and the range is genuinely wide. The satisfying-cleaning corner has carpet cleaning, car cleaning, sand cutting and crop cutting. The craft corner has wood shaping, pottery shaping on a spinning wheel, and a blackboard for drawing. The fidget-toy shelf has bubble pop, pop-it shapes, a fidget spinner, a squish ball, Newton's cradle, a light switch and a glass-crack toy. There are playful oddities too: egg frying, dalgona candy carving, bees splash, an item crusher, fruit and vegetable slicing, snooker, insect swat and a xylophone for making your own relaxing melodies. A recent update added six more: skin care, arm waxing, leg waxing, armpit waxing and cleaning, lipstick cutting, and a trending keyboard chain toy.

That variety is the whole point. Most relaxing games give you one mechanic and repeat it; here, if one toy stops calming you, another is a tap away. The interactions are deliberately simple — tap, drag, slice, squeeze — and the sounds are a big part of the appeal: the listing leans hard on ASMR-style audio, from the crunch of cutting to the click of switches. Nothing here demands skill or speed, which is exactly why it works as a stress reliever rather than as a game that creates new stress.

Key features

  • 40+ mini-games in one app. Cleaning, crafting, fidget toys, music and slicing games, all from a single shelf — no need to install a dozen separate toys.
  • Zero pressure by design. No complicated rules, no win-or-lose states, no timers pushing you along — pick a toy and stop whenever you like.
  • ASMR-style sounds. Satisfying audio is central to the experience, from foam spraying to pottery shaping, so headphones are recommended.
  • Regular new toys. The developer keeps adding mini-games — six arrived in the latest update, including skin-care and lipstick-cutting toys.
  • Everyone-rated and free. Free to download on Google Play with an Everyone content rating, and the core collection is playable without paying.
  • Short-session friendly. Every toy is a self-contained minute or two, so it fits waiting rooms, commutes and bedtime wind-downs alike.

Pros and cons

What works: the sheer breadth of the collection is the selling point — car washing, pottery, fidget spinners and candy carving would each be a whole app elsewhere, and here they sit side by side. The no-pressure design is honest: unlike many "relaxing" games that sneak in timers and fail states, this one genuinely lets you stop and start. The trending numbers back it up: a 27-fold weekly install jump does not happen for a dud, and the Everyone rating plus free price make it an easy recommendation to try.

What to watch: first, the rating base is small — 451 ratings against 500,000+ installs — so the 4.0-star score could move fast in either direction as more players weigh in. Second, the app contains ads, which can break the calm the games are trying to create; how intrusive they feel will depend on your device and version. Third, two content notes for parents: the listing includes a "Gun Sound Game" toy with realistic gun sound effects, which is sound effects only rather than shooting gameplay, but it sits oddly in a collection marketed for kids — and the newest waxing toys (arm, leg and armpit waxing) are harmless but strange. Neither is a reason to avoid the app, but both are worth knowing before a younger child explores the whole shelf unsupervised. Finally, at about 218 MB it is a chunky download for a collection of mini-games, so check your storage before installing.

Pricing

Stress Relief - Relaxing Game is free to download on Google Play, and AppBrain lists it as containing ads. The Play listing fetched for this review showed a price of $0 and did not disclose an in-app purchase price range, so we are not quoting one here — check the Play page's current "in-app purchases" line before spending anything in-game. The full shelf of mini-games is playable without paying.

If you like gentle, repeatable games with a satisfying loop, our Bus Parade review covers another October 2026 breakout built around a simple, calming mechanic. Stress Relief is the broader of the two: less of a game, more of a toy box — and when one toy stops working, the next one is already waiting.

Download Stress Relief on Google Play

The bottom line: Stress Relief - Relaxing Game earns its trending spot the old-fashioned way — by giving players a lot of small pleasures in one free package. The rating base is still thin, the ads will interrupt the calm now and then, and parents should note the gun-sound toy in the mix. But as a free, Everyone-rated collection of 40+ genuinely soothing mini-games, it is the easiest recommendation in this week's trending charts: download it, find your three favourite toys, and keep it on your phone for the next stressful day.
