Ocean Fish Review: Cute Fish Merging (2026)

Quick verdict — 4 / 5. Ocean Fish: Idle Merge is a genuinely relaxing merge game — cute fish, simple drag-and-drop merging, and new species and ocean scenes to unlock as you go. It has earned its 4.9-star average from more than 14,000 ratings. One honest warning, though: play it for the fish, not for payouts. Several reviewers report ads promising cash rewards and withdrawal goals that keep moving, so treat any in-game cash promise with scepticism.

Ocean Fish: Idle Merge, from developer Jamalgad, arrived on Google Play in August 2026 and has already passed 220,000 installs, with around 160,000 of those in the last 30 days — a fast climb for a casual game barely two months old. On Google Play it holds around 4.9 stars from more than 14,600 ratings, and it is currently trending on the Top Free Casual charts. That combination — a big, happy, fast-growing player base — is exactly why it earns a review here this week.

The pitch is simple and, for once, accurate: this is a merge game you play to unwind. There are no timers breathing down your neck, no fail states to dread, and no complicated systems to learn before the fun starts. You summon fish, you merge matching pairs, and your little underwater world grows more colourful with every new species you discover. If you like low-pressure collection games, this one understands the assignment.

How it plays

The loop takes about ten seconds to learn. Spend Shells, the game's currency, to summon fish onto your board. Drag one fish onto another of the same level and the two merge into a new, higher-level fish. Each merge is a small discovery: a new colour, a new shape, a new personality swimming around your tank. The higher you climb the merge chain, the rarer and more elaborate the fish become — the screenshots alone show fish at levels 10, 24, 27, 32, 49, 50 and beyond.

There is no penalty for taking your time. You can merge at your own pace, watch your collection fill out, and come back later to find things much as you left them. That gentle rhythm is the game's main strength, and it is also why so many players describe it as relaxing rather than competitive. It is a game for a quiet ten minutes, not a game that demands your evening.

Unlocking new scenes

As your collection grows, you unlock new underwater environments — fresh backdrops for your fish, from pink coral reefs to deep blue open water. It is a small reward, but it works: your tank visibly changes as you progress, so the game always feels like it is going somewhere. The September 2026 update added support for more countries and languages, which helps explain why the install numbers have kept climbing into October.

Visually, the game leans into charm. The fish are round, bright and expressive, the water is full of bubbles and drifting light, and the whole thing looks like a children's picture book in motion. It is an easy game to show a younger player, and an easy one to leave running while you decompress.

Key features

  • Simple drag-and-drop merging — match two fish of the same level to discover a higher-level species.
  • A wide collection to complete — fish in different colours, shapes and personalities, with new unlocks all the way up the merge chain.
  • Unlockable underwater scenes — new environments open up as your collection grows.
  • Shells-based summoning — spend Shells to bring new fish onto your board and keep the merges flowing.
  • Genuinely relaxing pace — no fail states, no countdown pressure, just merging at your own speed.
  • Growing language support — the latest update added more countries and languages.

Pros and cons

What we like: the merge loop is instantly understandable and satisfying; the fish designs are genuinely charming; progression feels steady rather than grindy; and the 4.9-star average across more than 14,000 ratings is unusually strong for a game this new. The scene unlocks give you a reason to keep collecting beyond the next merge.

What to watch out for: the game is light on depth — if you want puzzles, strategy or long-term goals, this is a cosy toy rather than a deep game. And the bigger caveat is about money, not gameplay: several recent reviewers report that ads for the game promise cash rewards and PayPal withdrawals, and that the withdrawal goals in-game kept moving once they were reached. The Play Store listing itself describes a relaxing merge game and promises no payouts, so treat any cash claim you see in an ad as marketing, not a feature. Download it for the merging, not for a payday.

Pricing

Ocean Fish: Idle Merge is free to download and play on Android. Shells are the in-game currency used to summon fish. As with any free game, expect optional extras inside the app; the core merging and collecting is fully playable without spending.

On privacy, the Play Store listing states that the developer shares no data with third parties, that data is encrypted in transit, and that collected data — app activity, app info and performance, and device identifiers — cannot be deleted. Worth knowing before you install, though it is a fairly standard declaration for a casual game.

Like this kind of gentle water game? We also reviewed Fish Up, another relaxing fishing title on the site. Ocean Fish is the calmer, more collectible of the two — less about catching, more about growing a tank full of discoveries.

The bottom line: if you want ten relaxing minutes of merging cute fish and unlocking a prettier tank, Ocean Fish: Idle Merge delivers exactly that, and its huge, happy early player base is no accident. Just ignore any promise of cash — the real reward here is the next ridiculous, wonderful fish.
