Police Car Parking Simulator Review (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.1 / 5. Police Car Parking Simulator is a straightforward, generous parking game: more than 500 levels, a garage full of customisation, and a modest 59 MB download. It is free, rated Everyone, and riding a fresh trending wave — but go in expecting ads between levels, which is players' most common complaint.

Police Car Parking Simulator, from developer Legion Apps Studio, has been on Google Play since April 2019 and is having another moment. Tracked installs stand at 20 million, with 130,000 of those in the last 30 days, and AppBrain's trending free games list for 27 October 2026 puts it back in the spotlight with 81,000 installs in the last 7 days — five times the week before. On Google Play it holds 4.2 stars from about 19,400 ratings. For a seven-year-old parking game, that kind of second wind is rare — and it is the reason it earns a review here this week.

The idea is exactly what the title promises. You are behind the wheel of a police car, and each level asks you to steer it into a marked bay without clipping cones, barriers, crates or other vehicles. Levels start simple — a wide bay, a gentle turn — and grow steadily tighter, with slalom cone runs, ramps, narrow gaps between parked cars and multi-stage routes. The developer's own pitch sums it up: "Ready for challenging 500+ Levels in Police Car Parking Simulator Car Games 3D". There is no story to follow and no timer breathing down your neck on the early levels, so it works well as a pick-up-and-play game in short sessions.

How it plays

Each level is a small driving test. You steer with on-screen controls, watch your angles in tight spaces, and aim for the marked bay. The screenshots show how varied the courses get: one lot is a top-down maze of crates, cones and barriers; another is a cone slalom on a honeycomb-pattern yard; a third sends you up a ramp in a sandy depot scattered with barrels and cones. A movable camera angle helps on the trickier approaches — you can swing the view to check a blind side before committing to a turn, which matters once the bays get narrow.

Progress is the hook. Completing levels earns coins, and the game hands out daily rewards for coming back, so there is always a reason to play one more level. Coins feed the garage, where the game opens up beyond simple parking: you can unlock new police cars, fit spoilers, change rims, adjust ride height for a sportier look, and add parking sensors that make tight manoeuvres easier. The most recent update, version 1.2.80 (14 May 2026), added spoilers, new police cars and improvements to controls and graphics — small touches, but they show a game still being maintained years after launch.

Why it is trending again

Police Car Parking Simulator is trending again because it is the opposite of demanding. The download is under 60 MB, it runs on Android 4.4 and up, and a level takes a minute or two — so it fits older phones, younger players and anyone who wants a driving game without a huge install or a complicated career mode. The police-car theme helps too: the black-and-white cars, the badge and the siren styling make an ordinary parking puzzle feel like a job with a uniform, and that dressing is a big part of its charm for casual players. Its best chart showings have been in the strategy charts, where it has reached the top 100 in several countries.

Key features

  • 500+ levels that progress from easy bays to difficult, multi-obstacle courses.
  • Garage customisation: new police cars, spoilers, rims, ride height and parking sensors.
  • Multiple driving controls and a movable camera angle for tight parking spots.
  • Daily rewards and coins earned from every completed level.
  • Small, light install: about 59 MB, running on Android 4.4 and up.
  • Everyone content rating — no mature content, suitable for all ages.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • More than 500 levels — a lot of game for a free download
  • Light 59 MB install that runs on older Android phones
  • Garage customisation gives progression beyond the next level
  • Everyone rating and short, session-friendly levels
  • Still updated, with new cars and control improvements in 2026

Cons

  • Ads are the top player complaint, with reports of ads after levels
  • Visuals are dated next to modern driving simulators
  • The "chase" wording in the listing oversells it — this is a parking game first
  • In-app purchases are flagged on the listing without a clear price range shown

Pricing

Police Car Parking Simulator is free to download and play on Google Play. The listing flags ads and in-app purchases; the listing we checked does not show a price range for those purchases, so treat any in-game offer on its own terms and never pay for anything the game does not clearly label inside the official store. The full game — all 500+ levels — is playable without paying, with coins earned by driving well and by collecting daily rewards.

Is it worth downloading?

If you like driving games but do not want a huge download, a steep learning curve or a long career mode, this is an easy free try — start with the early levels to learn the steering, then work through the cone courses and ramp lots as they tighten up. If you hate ads, though, go in with your eyes open: this is a free, ad-supported game, and several reviewers say ad frequency is its weakest point.
