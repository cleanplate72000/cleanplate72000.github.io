iPersia Calendar Review: Three in One (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. iPersia Calendar does one job unusually well: it puts the Persian Jalali calendar, the Gregorian calendar and the Islamic calendar on the same screen, then adds prayer times, notes, birthdays, work shifts and a date converter around them. It is free, it is only about 19 MB, and it is rated Everyone. It loses half a star because its 4.8-star average rests on fewer than 200 ratings so far, it contains ads, and the store screenshots are largely in Persian, so it is clearly built for its Persian-speaking audience first.

iPersia Calendar, from developer Exeersoft Corp, is a free productivity app that describes itself as a complete Persian (Jalali / Shamsi) calendar for daily life. The pitch is simple: see today's date in Persian, Gregorian and Islamic form at a glance, plan your month, and keep notes, tasks and reminders in the same place instead of jumping between a calendar app, a converter website and a prayer-times app.

Why review it now? Because it is climbing fast. iPersia Calendar launched on Google Play in September 2026, and AppBrain's tracking, updated on 25 October 2026, puts it at about 110,000 installs, all of them in the last 30 days — roughly 8,600 a day. That run took it to number 1 in Top New Free overall in Iran, number 6 in Top New Free Productivity in the United States, and into the top new free productivity charts in Germany and Canada as well. On the live Play listing it holds about 4.8 stars from around 200 ratings, with Play showing a 100,000+ install bracket. The latest version listed is 1.0.1, updated on 14 October 2026. That is a very fast first month — and, honestly, a very young app, which is worth keeping in mind alongside the high rating.

Three calendars, one screen

This is the feature that makes the app worth a look, and it is also the feature most general calendar apps handle badly. iPersia Calendar offers daily, monthly and yearly Persian calendar views, with Gregorian and Hijri (Islamic) dates shown side by side. Iranian national holidays and occasions are included, so you are not left guessing which days are holidays, and a fast date converter switches between Persian, Gregorian and Islamic dates. You can also work out the days between two dates and set countdown timers.

The daily view is where the idea comes together best. The screenshot above shows the Persian date in large type, the Gregorian and Islamic dates beside it, and prayer times, day-length information and a note about the day below. If you live with two or three calendar systems — for family, faith, work or travel — that single screen saves a surprising amount of mental arithmetic.

More than a calendar

Beyond dates, the Play Store description lists a solid set of planning tools. There are day notes and a notebook-style journal, to-do lists organised by category, birthdays with reminders, personal events that sync with your phone calendar, and work shifts and schedules — a useful extra if your rota does not follow a standard Monday-to-Friday pattern. Prayer times and a Qibla compass are included, with optional prayer reminders, which makes the app a daily companion rather than something you open only to check a date.

Two smaller additions round it out. Currency and gold rates, plus a currency converter, sit inside the same app — handy if you follow those rates day to day. Home screen widgets, themes and custom designs let you put the Persian date where you will actually see it, and the latest update added home and lock-screen date widgets, Control Center tiles for the Persian date and all dates, and an optional Persian day number in the status bar. Encrypted backup and restore is listed for your notes and personal data, which matters once you start keeping a journal and shift history in the app.

Key features

  • Persian, Gregorian and Islamic dates side by side, in daily, monthly and yearly views
  • Fast date converter between Persian, Gregorian and Islamic calendars, plus days-between-dates and countdown timers
  • Iranian national holidays and occasions built in
  • Local prayer (azan) times, Qibla compass and optional prayer reminders
  • Day notes, notebook-style journal, to-do lists by category, birthdays and work shifts
  • Currency and gold rates with a currency converter
  • Home and lock-screen widgets, Control Center tiles, themes and encrypted backup

Pros and cons

Pros: three calendar systems on one screen, done cleanly; a genuinely useful date converter; prayer times and Qibla included without needing a second app; notes, tasks, birthdays and shifts in one place; small download at about 19 MB; rated Everyone; free to download.

Cons: the 4.8-star average comes from fewer than 200 ratings, so it is early evidence, not a long track record; the app contains ads, according to its Play listing; the store screenshots are largely in Persian, so if you need a fully English interface, try it first before relying on it; and in-app purchase details are not shown on the Play page checked, so check inside the app before assuming every extra is free.

Pricing

iPersia Calendar is free to download on Google Play, and its Play listing shows a price of 0. The listing does say the app contains ads. No subscription price or in-app purchase list was shown on the Play page checked for this review, so treat any paid extras, if they appear inside the app, as something to check before you buy — the core calendar, converter, prayer times and planning tools are what the free listing promises.

Who should download it?

If you use the Persian calendar in daily life — or you regularly convert between Jalali, Gregorian and Islamic dates for family, religious or work reasons — this is one of the most focused options to appear on Play in 2026, and its first-month growth suggests a lot of people in exactly that situation agree. If you only need a standard Gregorian calendar, it is more than you need, and you would be better served by a simpler calendar app. For the full picture of what is trending right now, see our Top 100 page.

Download iPersia Calendar on Google Play

The honest summary is this: iPersia Calendar is a young app with a very strong start, built around a clear idea that general calendars do not serve well. The three-calendars-in-one view, the converter, prayer times and planning tools make it genuinely useful for its audience, and the small size and free download make it easy to try. The rating base is still small and there are ads, so go in with that in mind — but as a free Persian calendar for Android in 2026, it is an easy recommendation for the people it is made for.
