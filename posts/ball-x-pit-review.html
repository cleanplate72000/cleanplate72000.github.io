BALL x PIT Review: Roguelite Ball Pit (2026)

Quick verdict — 4 / 5. BALL x PIT is a proper console-style roguelite squeezed onto your phone: ricocheting balls, deep fusion builds and a town to rebuild between dives. It is free to try, rated 4.7 stars, and genuinely popular — but be clear-eyed before you download: the free version is a trial, the full game is a paid unlock, and the 426 MB download is a big one. If you like grindy, experimental action games, it is well worth the trial.

BALL x PIT, from publisher DevolverDigital, is a brick-breaking, ball-fusing, base-building survival roguelite. You play a treasure hunter diving into a seemingly bottomless pit beneath the fallen city of Ballbylon, battering hordes of monsters with ricocheting balls, gathering riches, and using them to rebuild a new settlement on the surface. It first launched on PC and consoles in October 2025, came to Android in February 2026, and is now having a strong mobile run: independent tracker AppBrain puts it at 1.4 million installs, with 180,000 of those in the last 30 days — roughly 6,000 installs a day — and on Google Play it holds 4.7 stars from more than 14,000 ratings. The Play listing is currently pushing its Naturalist Update, with a limited-time discount on the full version.

The pitch in one image: a party of treasure hunters, a pit full of monsters, and balls flying everywhere. This is a fantasy combat game, not a gentle puzzle — Google Play rates it Medium Maturity, so it is aimed at teens and adults rather than young children.

How it plays

The core loop is simple to learn and hard to put down. You aim, you fire, and your balls ricochet through ranks of enemies, chaining hits as they bounce. Between runs you fuse balls together: the Play description promises over 60 randomised balls to equip and combine, with hundreds of possible outcomes, so two players can end up with wildly different arsenals. Finding an overpowered combination — a ball that splits, pierces, or explodes at just the right moment — is the game's main thrill, and the reason reviewers keep calling it addictive.

Combat in the pit: aim your shot, then watch the ricochets do the work as balls bounce through enemy ranks.

Key features

  • Ball fusion: combine 60+ randomised balls into new, more powerful combinations and discover unexpected synergies.
  • Build New Ballbylon: spend your loot on more than 70 unique buildings that grant bonuses, unlock power-ups and open up new characters.
  • New realms and bosses: battle through deserts, frozen caverns and savage forests, each with its own hazards, enemy types and screen-filling bosses.
  • Recruitable hunters: meet fellow treasure hunters in the pit, each with unique, upgradable mechanics that change how a run plays.
  • Active updates: the Naturalist Update is the latest content drop, with the developer promising the same updates as the PC and console versions.

Each realm changes the battlefield. The desert arena above is a world away from the opening caverns, with different enemies and hazards to plan around.

The base-building half matters

What lifts BALL x PIT above a simple brick-breaker is the town. Every dive feeds New Ballbylon: buildings generate resources, homebound heroes can be set to automated tasks, and a growing settlement unlocks further power-ups and characters. That gives even a failed run a purpose — you always come back with something — and it is why players report sinking hours into the grind. The flip side, flagged honestly by reviewers, is repetition: if you do not enjoy replaying levels to chase better builds, this game will wear thin faster than a one-and-done story game.

New Ballbylon between dives: place buildings, collect resources and unlock the upgrades that make your next run stronger.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely deep fusion system with real build variety; a satisfying loop where failed runs still progress your town; no ads listed on Google Play; a full console game on mobile, still receiving updates; a strong 4.7-star rating across more than 14,000 reviews.

Cons: the free download is a trial — the full game costs extra, so check the unlock price shown on the Play page in your country before you commit (reviewers report paying around $9–$10, with at least one reporting a higher regional price); the 426 MB download is large for a mobile game; a few reviewers report lost progress or trouble restoring a purchase after reinstalling, so make sure your purchase is tied to the right Google account; and the learning curve is steep once fusion and base-building systems pile up.

Pricing

BALL x PIT is free to download on Google Play, and Google Play does not list it as containing ads. The full version is a one-time paid unlock — the Play listing itself advertises a limited-time discount on it and says “buy once, own forever.” There is no subscription-style grind here, which is refreshing, but do treat the download as a trial: play the opening, decide whether the ricochet-and-fuse loop hooks you, and only then pay for the full game. Should you download it?

Yes — if you enjoy roguelites, experimental builds and a grind that respects your time with permanent town progress. Try the free version first, watch the unlock price in your region, and go in expecting a big download and a steep early learning curve. BALL x PIT earns its 4.7 stars not by being the easiest game on the store, but by being one of the most interesting: a brick-breaker that grew up into a full survival roguelite, and a rare mobile port that does not hide behind ads or energy timers.
