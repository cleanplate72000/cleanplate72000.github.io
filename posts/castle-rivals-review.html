Castle Rivals Review: Cannon PvP (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Castle Rivals is a fast, satisfying cannon battler: aim, fire, and watch a whole fortress crumble brick by brick. Its physics destruction is genuinely fun, and its chart climb across Europe is real. It is an honest 4.0, not a 5, because some players dispute how truly live its PvP is, and progression can start to feel grindy — but for quick, explosive duels, it delivers.

Castle Rivals, from developer VOODOO, is a fast-paced PvP destruction game with a simple promise, stated right on its Google Play page: build your defenses, load the cannon, and destroy the enemy castle before they destroy yours. It has been on Google Play since March 2026, and its recent surge is striking — around 110,000 installs tracked in the last 30 days, at roughly 3,500 installs a day, taking it past the 100,000+ install bracket on Play itself. That momentum has put it at #14 in the Top Free Action chart in the Czech Republic and Denmark, #18 in Austria, #19 in Poland and #20 in Italy, with Top Free Action showings in Germany, France, Canada and the United States as well. For a game that was little-known earlier this year, that is real traction — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

On Google Play it holds around 4.5 stars from more than 2,000 ratings at the time of this review (the live Play page showed 4.52 stars from 2,371 ratings; an AppBrain snapshot from 24 October 2026 showed 4.49 from 2,023 — the exact figure moves as new ratings arrive, but the picture is consistently positive). That is a strong early average, though it sits alongside some pointed criticism, which this review keeps in view rather than smoothing over.

How it plays

The loop is easy to grasp and hard to put down. You face an enemy castle across the battlefield, and every shot matters. You aim your cannon, adjust for the shot, and fire at structural weak points — a tower base here, a loaded wall there — hoping to trigger a collapse rather than just chipping away at hit points. The destruction is fully physics-based, so no two collapses play out quite the same way: walls buckle, towers topple, and a well-placed hit can set off a chain reaction that brings down far more than the single block you struck.

That physics system is the game's real selling point. Plenty of mobile battlers fake destruction with a canned animation; here, the rubble behaves like rubble, and the satisfaction comes from watching a structure you weakened finally give way. It rewards patience as well as aggression: a rushed shot at the thickest wall wastes a turn, while a careful shot at a load-bearing corner can end a duel early.

Heroes, castles and progression

Castle Rivals is not just one cannon and one wall. The Play listing highlights unique heroes with powerful weapons and special abilities, each changing how you attack the enemy castle, and upgradeable castles that let you strengthen your own defenses and make your fortress harder to destroy. Choosing the right hero for the castle in front of you — and the right target on that castle — is where the strategy lives.

Between battles, you build and upgrade. The game's build screen lets you place and strengthen castle sections, so your fortress is partly your own design, not just a preset target. That gives losses a constructive side: when an enemy finds your weak point, you know exactly what to reinforce next time. It also means the game has a longer tail than a pure arcade shooter — there is always another upgrade, another hero ability, and another defensive layout to try.

Key features

  • Epic castle battles — fast PvP duels where only one fortress survives
  • Physics-based destruction — blast walls, collapse towers, and watch castles crumble realistically
  • Skill-based cannon combat — aim carefully, adjust your shots, and exploit structural weak points
  • Unique heroes — unlock heroes with different weapons and special abilities
  • Upgradeable castles — strengthen your defenses and make your fortress harder to destroy
  • Different battles every time — structures collapse dynamically, so no duel plays out the same way

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Physics-based castle destruction is genuinely satisfying and different every battle
  • Simple to learn — aim, fire, collapse — with real depth in weak-point targeting
  • Strong early rating: around 4.5 stars from more than 2,000 ratings
  • Fast chart climber across Europe, with 110,000 installs in the last 30 days
  • Heroes and castle upgrades give it progression beyond a single-shot arcade game

Cons

  • Some players dispute the PvP — reports of asynchronous or computer opponents with near-perfect aim
  • Progression complaints: pay-to-win concerns and grindy chest and upgrade systems reported by reviewers
  • Contains ads, which will interrupt play unless you find an in-app option to remove them
  • Large download at around 236 MB — check your storage before installing
  • Medium Maturity rating — fun for teens and adults, but not meant for young children

The honest caveats

The most important caveat is the one players argue about most. Several Play reviewers praise the game while questioning whether its duels are truly live player-versus-player: one reports opponents with perfect aim that always compensates for wind, another calls the PvP asynchronous, with opponents who upgrade just as you climb leagues. Others report the opposite experience and enjoy it as a straight duel game. The fair summary is this: the cannon-and-collapse gameplay is fun either way, but if live, human-versus-human competition is the main reason you would download it, go in knowing that some players are not convinced that is what they got.

The second caveat is progression. Reviewers who have climbed further report a grind: chests that take many wins to earn, castle upgrades that stall, and high-level opponents who can one-shot lower-level heroes, which some describe as pay-to-win. Early play is generous and fun; the question is whether the later game stays that way for free players. That is a common pattern in free battlers, but it is worth knowing before you invest hours in one fortress.

Pricing

Castle Rivals is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The listing checked for this review shows a price of 0 USD with no in-app purchase price range published in the data available, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 1.0.18, updated 22 October 2026, and the download is about 236 MB — a sizeable game, so download over Wi-Fi if your data plan is tight. It carries a Medium Maturity content rating, so it is a game for teens and adults rather than young children.

Download

Download Castle Rivals on Google Play

If you like a battler where one clever shot beats ten careless ones, Castle Rivals is worth a try: the destruction is the star, the duels are quick, and its European chart climb shows it is finding an audience fast. Just go in with eyes open about the disputed PvP and the later-game grind.
