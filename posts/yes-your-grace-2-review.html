Yes, Your Grace 2 Review: Rule Davern (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Yes, Your Grace 2: Snowfall is a rare thing on mobile: a proper story-driven kingdom-management RPG where every decision has a price. You can try the beginning free, there are no ads, and one $6.99 payment unlocks the whole game — but expect hard choices, a Teen rating, and a few rough edges carried over from launch.

Yes, Your Grace 2: Snowfall, from developer Noodlecake (the mobile publisher behind the original Yes, Your Grace), arrived on Google Play in October 2026 as the sequel to the 2020 kingdom-management hit. It puts you on the throne of Davern, a kingdom still recovering from war as a harsh winter closes in, and asks you to do the impossible job: keep everyone alive with never quite enough of anything. AppBrain tracks about 260,000 installs for the game, and it is charting in the Top New Free Role Playing lists in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia and Brazil — strong company for a slow, story-first game in a store full of fast dopamine loops. That chart climb is why it earns a review here this week.

The premise is simple and immediately tense. You are King Eryk, and every week a queue of petitioners files into your throne room: peasants, soldiers, nobles and strangers, each with a problem and a minimum expectation. Help them and you spend gold, supplies or soldiers you may need later. Refuse them and their loyalty — and sometimes their lives — are on your conscience. There is no perfect week in Davern, only weeks you survive.

How it works

Each week brings a randomised queue of petitioners, and each one wants something concrete: food, medicine, soldiers, justice, or a hearing for a grievance you may not be able to fix. You balance four pressures at once — your treasury, your supplies, your army and your people's satisfaction — and every grant or refusal ripples forward. Spend too freely early on and a later crisis finds the coffers empty; hoard everything and the kingdom's loyalty crumbles beneath you.

The game is built around consequences rather than reflexes. There are no timed taps or combat combos to master. Instead you read, weigh, decide, and live with it. Wrong calls are refunded only in the narrow sense the game allows: misallocated resources for a petition are returned, but a lost life, a broken alliance or a missed opportunity is not. That is the whole point, and it is what makes a session stick in your head long after you close the app.

Agents, laws and family

Petitioners are only half the job. You also hire royal agents — soldiers, witches and other specialists — each with their own skills, personalities and a limited number of actions. Send the right agent and a thorny problem untangles; send the wrong one and you have wasted a precious action. Agents have lives of their own, too: their personal stories and romantic entanglements can stir up trouble at court, and following their dilemmas is some of the game's best writing.

Then there are the Laws of Davern. Spend law points to reshape the kingdom itself — for example, passing a Fire Watch law that employs children and the elderly to tend communal fires, cutting the chance of freezing disasters. Laws change which quests appear and which disasters threaten you, so two playthroughs genuinely diverge.

Above all, the game keeps pulling you back to family. You watch your children grow, you try to hold the household together, and the game is explicit that their support is the strongest pillar in rebuilding the safe home Davern once was. It is this warmth, set against the cold management layer, that lifts Snowfall above a spreadsheet with a crown on it.

Why it is trending

Snowfall is trending because it is a premium-feeling game that respects the player's time and wallet: try the opening free, no ads anywhere, one payment for everything. That is an unusual offer on Google Play in 2026, and it stands out next to energy timers and gacha banners. It also arrived with a built-in audience — the first Yes, Your Grace has a loyal following — and with Apple and Google both featuring indie releases this month, including a Google Play Pass slot for Noodlecake's line-up.

The numbers back up the chart story. AppBrain's tracked installs sit around 260,000, all of them recent, and the game holds Top New Free Role Playing positions across the US, Canada, the UK, Australia and Brazil. Google Play itself lists the game in the 100,000+ downloads bracket with a 3.9-star average from about 939 ratings — a solid, not glowing, start that matches the honest caveats below.

Key features

  • Cinematic kingdom management — rule Davern as King Eryk in a Slavic-folklore-inspired world, presented in 2.5D pixel art with a side-on view.
  • Weekly petitioner queues — a randomised line of petitioners each week, every one with a problem and a minimum expectation.
  • Real consequences — taxes, laws, supplies and soldiers all interact, and every decision bears consequences for the weeks that follow.
  • Royal agents with stories — hire specialists with distinct skills and personalities, and follow their personal dilemmas.
  • Laws of Davern — pass laws that change which quests and disasters shape your reign.
  • Family at the centre — watch your children grow and keep the household together as the kingdom rebuilds.
  • Kingdom upgrades and prestige — convert prestige, upgrade the kingdom, and gather items through exploration to match petition categories.
  • Offline, single-player play — a story game you can play anywhere, with no connection and no ads.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Try before you buy: the beginning is free, and there are no ads at all
  • One $6.99 payment unlocks the full game — no energy timers or gacha pulls
  • Genuinely consequential choices: petitioners, agents, laws and family all interact
  • Strong writing and atmosphere in a distinctive Slavic-folklore setting
  • Offline single-player game, also available through Google Play Pass
Cons
  • Rated Teen (violence, blood, suggestive themes, alcohol and drug references) — not a game for young children
  • Some reviewers report an annoying save system, a real risk in a game where one bad decision ripples for hours
  • Slow, reading-heavy pace will not suit players who want fast action
  • Early rating averages around 3.9 stars on Play — good, not great, and still settling
  • The web Play listing does not publish a download size, so grab it over Wi-Fi to be safe

Pricing

Yes, Your Grace 2: Snowfall is free to download, and the beginning of the game is free to play. A one-time in-app purchase of $6.99 (US Play listing; £5.99 on the UK listing) unlocks the full game. There are no ads, and no other purchases are needed to finish the story. It is also included in Google Play Pass, so subscribers can play the full game as part of that subscription. The current version is Production_1.0.0_b98, updated 2 October 2026, and it requires Android 6.0 and up.

Download

Download Yes, Your Grace 2: Snowfall on Google Play

If you like games where the story is the gameplay — where a single ruling can save a village or doom a campaign — Snowfall is an easy recommendation: a thoughtful, funny, sometimes brutal kingdom simulator with an honest price. Go in expecting reading, not reflexes, and save often.
