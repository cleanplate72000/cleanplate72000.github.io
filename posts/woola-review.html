Woola Review: AI Photos That Move (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Woola is genuinely fun for turning a still photo into a short AI video — the viral templates are the reason it is climbing the charts. But the free experience runs on credits that paid reviewers say burn out fast, so test it free before paying anything. Fun toy, pricey habit.

Woola - AI Photo Enhancer, from developer XL Mobile, is one of the fastest-rising photography apps on Google Play right now. On the Play Store it holds 4.0 stars from about 2,400 reviews, sits in the 500,000+ downloads bracket, and is rated Everyone. Independent tracking by AppBrain puts it higher still — around 730,000 installs, with roughly 130,000 of those in the last 30 days — and it has cracked the top-grossing Photography charts in several countries, including Italy and the Netherlands. For an app that only appeared on Google Play in April 2025 (current version 1.9.6, updated 20 October 2026), that is a genuine breakout, and it is why it earns a review here.

The pitch is simple: upload a photo, pick a template, and Woola's AI turns it into a moving video. At about 59 MB it is a small install, and the app is free to download — but nearly everything beyond a taste of it runs on credits and subscriptions, which is where this review gets honest. Read the pricing section before you pay.

What Woola actually does

Woola is not a photo editor in the usual sense — it is an AI video generator built around templates. You pick a photo, pick an effect, and the app renders a short clip. The Play Store listing groups the features into eight areas, and they are worth listing because the template library is the whole product:

  • Photo Embrace & Kiss: AI-generated hugging or kissing effects between people in photos.
  • AI creative effects: Venom-style transformations, a playful squish-and-stretch effect, a muscle-builder look, fluffy wool-like hairstyles, and LEGO-style explosion effects.
  • Revive old photos: bring blurry or static old photos to life with subtle facial movement.
  • Photo sing & dance: make the person in a photo lip-sync or dance, including rap music-video styles.
  • AI outfit change: try fashion, ethnic and festive clothing styles on a photo instantly.
  • Universal add-ons: paint on bags, accessories and hairstyles with a brush.
  • Premium portraits: wedding, vintage and model-style photo artworks.
  • Character swap: mirror yourself as a movie-style character, video to video.

In practice, the home screen is a feed of trending templates — "Mermaid Mode", "Crazy", "Diamond Drip", "Gas Station", "Run for Life" — and that feed is why the app spreads: every template is built to look like the viral clips filling social feeds right now. Pick one, upload a photo, wait for the render, and share the result.

The viral templates are the point

Most AI photo apps sell you tools. Woola sells you finished ideas. The "Run for Life" template drops your photo into a monster-chase movie scene; "AI Rap MV" turns a portrait into a night-time rap video; "Mermaid Mode" puts the subject underwater. You do not need to know anything about AI prompting or editing — the template does the creative work, and your photo supplies the face. That is exactly why it is trending: the gap between "I have a photo" and "I have a shareable video" is about a minute.

The results, by the developer's own screenshots, are polished and cinematic rather than cartoonish. That is a real strength against cheaper AI apps whose output looks obviously fake. But it also means expectations matter: AI video still warps hands, faces and backgrounds on some renders, and no template guarantees the exact result shown in the promo art.

Pricing: read this before paying

Woola is free to download, but it is a credit-and-subscription app. The subscription tiers listed for the app are around $5.99 per week or $39.99 per year, and Play reviewers report credit packs of about 60 credits for roughly $6, with a larger pack near $38. Here is the honest part: the most helpful Play reviews are from paying users who ran out of credits fast — one annual subscriber reports running out after fewer than 20 uses, another says several generations failed outright, and several say the results did not match the advertised templates.

That does not make Woola a scam — the app genuinely generates the videos it promises, and its 4.0-star average reflects plenty of happy free users. But it does mean the paid tiers are poor value unless you already know you love the output. Our advice: use the free credits first, judge the results on your own photos, and only then decide whether a subscription is worth it. Never subscribe just to try one template.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Huge library of genuinely viral, ready-made templates — no prompting or editing skill needed
  • Polished, cinematic output in the developer's own examples
  • Small install (about 59 MB), rated Everyone, simple one-photo workflow
  • Reviving old family photos is a genuinely lovely use case
  • Play listing declares no data shared with third parties and no data collected
Cons
  • Credits run out fast, and paid reviewers complain about failed generations and value
  • Weekly subscription is expensive if you only want the odd clip
  • Results can differ from the polished promo art — AI output varies
  • AI effects of real people should only be made from your own photos, with consent

Privacy and safety

On the Play Store data-safety section, the developer declares no data shared with third parties and no data collected, with data encrypted in transit and deletion requests possible. That is a cleaner declaration than most AI photo apps manage. One rule still applies to every app in this category: only animate photos of yourself or people who have said yes. AI kiss, hug and face-swap effects made from someone else's photo without permission are not a joke, and Woola's templates make that easier than ever — use them kindly.

Who should download it?

If you love the viral AI-video trend and want the fastest route from a photo to a shareable clip, Woola is worth the free try — the template library is genuinely the best part of the app, and the free credits are enough to judge it. If you want a serious photo editor with manual controls, look elsewhere: our Picsart review covers a full editor instead. And if you are tempted by the subscription, run the free tier first — the paid complaints on Play are consistent enough to take seriously.

Bottom line: a fun, trending AI video toy with a template library that explains its chart run — just keep your wallet closed until the free credits have convinced you.
