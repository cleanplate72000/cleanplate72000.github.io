Mother Wolf Family Simulator Review (2026)

Quick verdict — 2.5 / 5. Mother Wolf Family Simulator has a charming idea — raise a wolf family in a pretty forest world — and it is genuinely trending, with about 38,000 installs in its first 9 days. But its 2.8-star rating, the flood of ads players report, and a data-safety section that says your data is not encrypted mean this is one to try with caution, not a must-download.

Mother Wolf Family Simulator, from developer PIZZA PAZ LIMITED, is the newest app on AppBrain's trending Android list for 26 October 2026. It only appeared on Google Play in October 2026, and AppBrain tracks about 38,000 installs in total — all of them in the last 30 days, at roughly 8,700 installs a day — with a best chart position of #9 in the Top New Free Simulation chart in Mexico, plus #16 in the United States and #11 in Brazil in the same category. That is a fast start for a brand-new simulation game, and it is exactly why it is worth a closer look before you install it: the growth is real, but so is the backlash. On the live Play listing it holds just 2.8 stars from about 1,066 ratings, and AppBrain's rating histogram shows nearly half of its roughly 1,000 ratings are one-star reviews. A game does not get that split by accident, and this review looks honestly at both sides.

The pitch, in the developer's own words, is simple: “Play wolf game! Daddy feeds mama & children. Raise your family over time.” You step into a stylised natural world as a wolf mother, team up with a wolf partner, and build a pack. The Play description promises peaceful, relaxing gameplay: explore grassy forests with your partner, raise cute wolf pups, expand your family territory, and grow your legacy until your family numbers thousands of wolf children. It is a gentle fantasy — less about hunting and survival pressure, more about bonding, exploring and watching your pack grow — and the screenshots sell that feeling well, with a cosy farmhouse, a forest full of pups, and the family carrying logs together along a country path.

Key features

  • Wolf partner: team up with your own wolf partner to explore the wild and thrive together, rather than playing alone.
  • Grow a massive family: the stated goal is to build a pack of thousands of wolf children, with every new cub making the pack larger.
  • Raise your pups: spend time with your wolf pups, feed the family and look after them as the pack grows over time.
  • Explore a stylised forest world: grassy forests, open meadows and a farmhouse home base, in a charming, cartoon-like art style.
  • Expand your territory: grow your family territory as your pack gets bigger and your legacy strengthens.
  • Relaxing gameplay: the developer presents it as peaceful, low-pressure play — bonding with your pack and protecting your wild family, not a fast, competitive game.

As a concept, this is easy to like. Animal family simulators have a loyal audience precisely because they are calm: you potter around a pretty world, your little family grows, and there is always one more pup to look after. The art style helps too. The screenshots show expressive, storybook-style wolf characters — a mother and father wolf with their cubs — in bright, green settings that look far friendlier than a realistic wildlife game would. If you like cosy simulation games and you go in expecting a light, simple time-waster rather than a deep survival game, the first few minutes will probably charm you. That is the good news, and it is genuine: the idea, the look and the low-pressure loop are all appealing, and the game is rated Everyone on Google Play, so it is pitched at a general audience.

Now the honest caveats, because they are the reason for the low rating, and they matter more than the charm. The first is the ads. The game contains ads, and Play reviewers describe them as relentless rather than occasional. One reviewer, writing on 24 October 2026, says there is an ad when you launch the app, an ad when you select a game mode, an ad when you select a difficulty, an ad when you select a mode again, and ads during play itself every few seconds — and that closing an ad sometimes restarts the app completely. Another reviewer the same week calls the game unplayable for the same reason, saying an ad appears every five seconds. A third reviewer, on 23 October 2026, warns people not to download it, says you cannot walk or play without being forced into an ad, and claims it is a copy of a game that exists on Steam. Those are user reports, not verified findings — we have not confirmed any claim about another version of the game — but when several recent reviewers independently describe the same ad pattern, it is fair to warn you: expect very frequent ads, and expect them to interrupt play, not just sit between levels.

The second caveat is the data-safety section on the Play listing, and it is unusually blunt. It says the app may share Location, App activity and two other data types with third parties, that data is not encrypted, and that data cannot be deleted. For a simple animal game, that is a lot to accept: location sharing plus unencrypted data is a combination worth pausing over, especially on a phone used by younger players, even though the content rating is Everyone. AppBrain also lists 18 permissions for the app. None of this makes the game unsafe to open, but it does mean you should read the Play data-safety section yourself before installing, and think twice before granting location access to a game that does not obviously need it.

The third caveat is that this is a very early release. The current version is 4, updated 23 October 2026, and the game has only existed for days. Early releases often improve — the developer has already pushed several updates in its first week — but right now the balance of evidence says the game is rough around the edges, and the one-star share of its ratings suggests many players felt the screenshots promised more than the game delivered. One reviewer specifically says the wolf mother and pup look different from what is advertised. Again, that is one player's report, not a verified fact, but it fits the pattern of a new game whose marketing is ahead of its current state.

Pros and cons

Pros: a charming, original idea — raise a wolf family in a pretty, peaceful world; genuinely trending, with about 38,000 installs in 9 days and top-20 new-simulation chart spots in several countries; bright, storybook art that suits the cosy premise; free to try; rated Everyone; a developer that has already updated it several times in its first week.

Cons: a low 2.8-star rating, with nearly half of its ratings at one star; players report ads at almost every step, including during play, with app restarts after ads; the Play data-safety section says shared data includes location and is not encrypted, and data cannot be deleted; a large 119.4 MB download for a simple simulator; a very early release (version 4), so expect rough edges; some players say the game does not match its screenshots.

Pricing

Mother Wolf Family Simulator is free to download on Google Play and contains ads. The Play page we checked does not highlight in-app purchase prices, so ads are the price of the free download. The current version is 4, updated 23 October 2026, the download is about 119.4 MB, and Google Play rates it Everyone.

Download Mother Wolf Family Simulator on Google Play

So, should you download it? Only if you go in with your eyes open. If you love cosy animal simulators, have patience for ads, and do not mind a rough first release, the wolf-pack idea is sweet and the forest world is genuinely pretty — try it, but check the data-safety section first and do not grant location access unless you are comfortable with it. If frequent ads drive you mad, or unencrypted data sharing is a deal-breaker for you, wait for a later version and a better rating before trying it. For a similar animal game with a calmer reputation, also read our Animal Valley review. For a fast-rising new game whose charm is currently outweighed by its ads and its data practices, Mother Wolf Family Simulator earns its 2.5 out of 5.
