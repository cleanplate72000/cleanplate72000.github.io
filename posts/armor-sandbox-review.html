Armor Sandbox Review: Real Physics (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.3 / 5. Armor Sandbox is the rare mobile game that is genuinely educational: real material physics decides every impact, not a scripted animation. It is free, has no account and no ads according to its developer, and it shot to #1 Top New Free Simulation in the UK within two weeks of launch — but the creation tools sit behind a one-time payment, and the learning curve is steep.

Armor Sandbox, from developer VBJ (formerly Armor Simulation), launched on Google Play in October 2026 and has already passed 50,000+ installs — with around 90,000 tracked installs in its first 15 days alone, averaging roughly 9,500 installs a day. On Google Play it holds 4.6 stars from over 1,100 ratings, and it is climbing the new-release charts fast: #1 Top New Free Simulation in the United Kingdom, #4 in the United States, #2 in Germany and #5 in Poland. For a niche physics simulator with no marketing push, that is a remarkable debut — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The pitch is simple: pick something to shoot, pick something to shoot at, and watch what actually happens. Nothing here is a canned animation. Every plate, projectile and fragment is made of particles that carry real material properties — density, strength, toughness, melting point and heat capacity. Deformation, fracture, spalling, heat, fire and detonation all come from those numbers. Swap steel for glass and it shatters instead of denting, because glass is brittle — not because somebody drew a shatter effect.

What is inside

The scale of the sandbox is what sets it apart. The Play listing promises 110+ materials — steels, aluminium alloys, tungsten, uranium, ceramics and glass, concrete and stone, wood, rubber, water, fuel, explosives and nuclear materials, plus working electrical parts. There are 70 projectiles, from pistol and rifle rounds to APFSDS long rods, shaped charges, HESH rounds, railgun slugs and three detailed missiles, and 90+ targets including plates, layered armour, deep blocks, ballistic gelatin, bunkers and circuits.

Then there are the 36 vehicle cutaways: Second World War tanks like the T-34-85, Tiger I, Panther and Sherman, Cold War machines like the T-55, T-72 and M103, and modern armour including the M1A2 Abrams, Leopard 2A6 and Challenger 2 — plus cars, trucks, a bus, a tractor, a fighter jet and attack helicopters, each modelled with its real armour, ammunition, fuel and engine inside. Add 60+ ready-made experiments, from a simple ricochet to a chain of nuclear devices, and there is enough here to keep a curious player busy for weeks without building anything at all.

Make your own, tune the materials, wire it up

Watching is only half the app. You can paint projectiles and targets pixel by pixel, choosing the material of every pixel, with mirror, outline, replace and snap-to-grid tools. Save designs to your library and share them with a friend or the community Workshop as a single small file. You can also change how strong, dense, hot or explosive any material is — and every simulation follows your numbers.

The electrical side goes further than most sandboxes dare: batteries, capacitors, switches, sensors, delay lines and detonators. Current goes only where you drew a path, so an insulated wire along steel leaves the steel dead while bare copper makes the whole plate live. Pressure plates, tripwires, magnetic and proximity sensors, photocells, radio receivers and high-voltage sparks that jump the air all work. Impacts, explosions, fire and projectiles flying past all sound like the material and the speed, and you can record your shots as video and share them.

It runs on a phone — with caveats

The physics core runs in a background thread, so the screen keeps up while the simulation works, with quality scaling from 18,000 particles up to a million depending on your device. The download itself is tiny at around 3 MB. But be warned: this is a demanding app. Reviewers report phones getting warm, occasional frame-rate drops, and slow-motion playback that you cannot always switch off. The newest update (version 1.8.9, 24 October 2026) reworked the phone layout with floating menus, made tanks stop falling apart after light hits, and improved penetration realism — so the developer is clearly listening.

There is also a real learning curve. There is no hand-holding campaign here; you are expected to enjoy experimenting. Some players also dispute the physics in edge cases — one reviewer notes a 9 mm round smashing through plate it should not, another says half the creation materials sit behind the paywall. Both criticisms are fair, and both are worth knowing before you install.

Pros and cons

Pros: genuine particle physics, not animations; huge library of materials, projectiles, targets and vehicles; free to watch and play every built-in experiment; no account and no ads, according to the developer; tiny 3 MB download; active developer shipping frequent fixes.

Cons: creating with energetic, nuclear and electrical materials, editing material properties and exporting designs all need the one-time full-version payment; steep learning curve with little guidance; can heat up phones and drop frames on weaker devices; a few physics results look wrong to knowledgeable players; rated Medium Maturity, so it is aimed at older teens and adults rather than young children.

Pricing

Armor Sandbox is free to download and free to watch: every built-in experiment runs in full, explosives, nuclear devices and circuits included, as does every vehicle and material a preset uses, and importing a design someone sent you is free too. The full version is a single one-time payment, not a subscription, and it unlocks painting with energetic, nuclear and electrical materials, editing the properties of every material, a library without the five-per-kind limit, exporting your designs, and the Extreme and Maximum quality levels. There are no ads either way, according to the developer.

If you like physics sandboxes, you may also enjoy our Tank Busters review — another military-themed game, but a much simpler arcade take on the same idea.

Download Armor Sandbox on Google Play

The bottom line: Armor Sandbox is the most honest physics toy on Android right now — a simulator that trusts its numbers and its players. If you have ever wondered what actually happens when a tungsten dart meets tank armour, this is the app that shows you. Just go in expecting a learning curve, and expect your phone to work hard.
