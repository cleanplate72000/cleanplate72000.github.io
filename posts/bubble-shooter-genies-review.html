Bubble Shooter Genies Review: Offline (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.2 / 5. Bubble Shooter Genies is a polished, genuinely relaxing bubble shooter with more than 1,045 puzzles, full offline play and a 4.7-star rating from over a quarter of a million players. The catch is one long-time players keep naming: ads are frequent, and later levels push hard on paid boosters. If you can live with ad breaks, it is one of the best free bubble shooters on Play.

Bubble Shooter Genies, from developer PUZZLEJOY, is one of the most enduring casual games on Google Play — live since November 2016, with more than 92 million installs in total. It is trending again right now: AppBrain's trending chart for the week ending 26 October 2026 puts it at about 250,000 installs in the last 7 days, five times the week before, and roughly 660,000 installs in the last 30 days. On the live Play listing it is rated 4.7 stars from about 273,000 ratings, it is rated Everyone, and it is free to download. The latest version is 2.97.0, updated 7 October 2026, and the app is about 93 MB.

The game is the classic bubble-shooter formula, done with care. You aim a bubble from the bottom of the screen, match three or more of the same colour, and pop them to clear the board. Many levels add a second goal: rescue the cute baby dragons trapped inside bubbles at the top. Colour, fire, lightning and magic bubbles act as power-ups, and four special boosters help you out of tight spots. Levels take you through different areas — castles, ocean worlds and more — and the developer adds new maps regularly, so the 1,045-plus puzzle count keeps growing.

What sets Genies apart from the dozens of bubble shooters crowding Play is how relaxed it feels to play. There is no timer breathing down your neck on most levels, the colours are bright without being garish, and the whole game works completely offline — the developer's own tagline is "No wifi needed". That makes it a genuinely good travel game: a long train journey or a flight is exactly where a no-internet puzzle earns its place. Daily rewards and bonuses give returning players a small reason to open it each day, and a connection is only needed for the newest events and bonuses.

Key features

  • 1,045+ puzzles across castles, ocean worlds and other themed areas, with new maps added regularly.
  • Rescue mode: free the baby dragons trapped in bubbles to complete levels.
  • Power-up bubbles: colour, fire, lightning and magic bubbles for clearing stubborn clusters.
  • Four special boosters to help pass difficult levels, plus an extended aim line for precise shots.
  • Fully offline play — no wifi or mobile data needed for the core puzzles.
  • Daily rewards and bonuses for players who come back every day.

Pros and cons

Pros: huge puzzle count that keeps growing; genuinely playable offline; easy to learn, with a relaxing pace; strong long-term rating across hundreds of thousands of players; small-ish 93 MB download for a game this old and polished.

Cons: ads are the most common complaint in recent player reviews — several players report an ad after almost every level, sometimes in sets of two or three; later levels lean on paid boosters and extra moves, and some players feel the bubble colours stop cooperating around level 85 unless you pay; the Play data-safety section notes the app may share location and device IDs with third parties and that collected data cannot be deleted, which privacy-minded players should know.

Pricing

Bubble Shooter Genies is free to download and free to play. Optional in-app purchases cover extra moves, extra lives and booster packs — the listing does not put a price on these in the summary, and they are never required to finish the early game. The trade-off for free play is advertising: expect regular ad breaks between levels, with optional reward ads for extra bonuses.

Is it worth downloading?

If you want a calm, offline puzzle game with an enormous level count, Bubble Shooter Genies is an easy recommendation — it has survived a decade on Play for a reason, and its current surge (a fivefold weekly jump in installs) shows it is finding new players all over again. If you hate ads, though, go in with your eyes open: this is a free, ad-supported game, and long-time players say ad frequency has grown over the years.
