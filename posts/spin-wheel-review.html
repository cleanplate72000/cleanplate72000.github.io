Spin Wheel Review: Decide in a Spin (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Spin Wheel: Random Chooser does exactly what its name promises: it turns "I don't know, you pick" into a quick, colourful spin. Custom wheels, a team splitter, a ranking tool and a finger picker cover most everyday decisions, and it is free with an Everyone rating — but the divided 3.7-star rating and the ads mean you should go in with realistic expectations.

Spin Wheel: Random Chooser, from developer EDEO TECH LIMITED, has been on Google Play since July 2025 and is having a sharp second wind. Google Play lists it at 1,000,000+ installs, AppBrain tracks 1.2 million installs in total with 190,000 of those in the last 30 days — roughly 6,200 new installs a day — and the app appears on AppBrain's trending free list for October 2026 with 91,000 installs in the last 7 days, 12 times the week before. Its live Google Play rating stands at 3.7 stars from 134 ratings, it is free to download, Google Play rates its content Everyone, and the listing says it contains ads. The Play listing was last updated on 7 September 2026.

The appeal is easy to understand, because the problem it solves is universal: a group of friends who cannot agree on dinner, a teacher who needs a fair way to pick a name, a coach splitting players into teams, or a family arguing about what to watch. Instead of debating for ten minutes, you spin a wheel and the argument ends — usually with laughter. The app's key art makes the same pitch, showing a group of friends gathered around a phone while a wheel offers Sushi, Chicken, Salad, Bread and Pasta.

Four tools in one app

Spin Wheel is not just one wheel. The listing describes four separate tools, and that breadth is what lifts it above the dozens of single-wheel apps on the Play Store.

First is the Roulette wheel itself: create a wheel for any question — lunch choices, game dares, who pays the bill — add your own options, and spin. The second screenshot shows this screen in action, with a "What are you eating today?" wheel offering Chicken, Pasta, Burger, Salad and Sushi, plus saved wheels for "Yes or No" and "Who will you marry?" underneath. Second is Homograft, the team divider, which splits people into fair, random groups in seconds — handy for class projects, five-a-side teams or game night. Third is Ranking, which shuffles participants into a random order for games, competitions or queue order. Fourth is the Chooser, the lucky-finger picker: everyone puts a finger on the screen and the app picks one winner, as shown in the "Choose Number" screenshot.

Customisation is the real selling point

Where a lot of spinner apps stop at a fixed wheel, this one lets you shape the wheel itself. The "Edit Roulette" screen lets you name a wheel, add and remove options with their own colours, and adjust text size, whether options can repeat, and the spin duration — the third screenshot shows a food wheel being edited with sliders for text size, repeat option and a 3-second spin duration. That matters more than it sounds: a wheel for tonight's dinner and a wheel for a classroom raffle want very different looks and pacing, and being able to save wheels and reuse them turns a novelty into a tool you actually reach for.

The app also leans into the fun side with bright animations and sound effects as the wheel spins, which is half the point at a party. The design is loud — purple gradients, chunky colours, confetti — and that is clearly deliberate. This is an app for breaking the ice, not for blending into a corporate meeting.

Who it is actually for

The listing pitches four audiences, and they all check out. Party hosts get icebreakers and dare wheels. Teachers get a quick, visible way to pick names or split a class without accusations of favouritism. Sports organisers get fast team splits. And everyone else gets an answer to "what should we eat?" in about ten seconds. The finger picker deserves a special mention: it is the feature most likely to get used at a gathering, because it needs no setup at all — just hands on a screen and a tap.

The honest caveats

Now for the part the 3.7-star rating is telling you. AppBrain's rating breakdown for this app is unusually polarised: 66 five-star ratings against 39 one-star ratings, with almost nothing in between. That split usually means the app works well for most people but frustrates a minority — and with only 134 ratings on Play, a handful of unhappy users moves the average a lot. Treat the star rating as "divided", not "bad".

There are smaller caveats too. The app contains ads, which is expected for a free app but worth knowing before you hand your phone around at a party. The listing checked shows no in-app purchase prices and no app size, so if you are on a tight data plan or a nearly-full phone, that information is not on the store page. And a note on fairness: this is a casual randomiser for fun decisions. It is not a certified draw system, so do not use it for anything where a result has to be provably fair — real-money giveaways, legal draws or anything with genuine stakes deserve a proper, auditable method.

Pros
  • Four tools in one: wheels, team splits, ranking and a finger picker
  • genuinely customisable wheels with colours, options and spin speed
  • Free, rated Everyone, and genuinely trending — 1,000,000+ installs
  • Saved wheels make it reusable, not a one-time novelty
  • Perfect for parties, classrooms and quick group decisions
Cons

  • Contains ads
  • No size or in-app purchase details shown on the listing checked

Pricing

Spin Wheel: Random Chooser is free to download on Google Play, and the listing checked shows no in-app purchase prices. It contains ads, which is how the free version is funded. If a paid, ad-free version appears later, it was not listed at the time of this review.

Final word

Spin Wheel: Random Chooser is a small app with a clear job, and it does that job with more flexibility than most of its rivals. The custom wheels, the team splitter and the finger picker together make it genuinely useful at parties, in classrooms and at home — and its current surge, with installs running 12 times higher than the week before, suggests a lot of people are discovering exactly that. The divided rating and the ads keep it from a higher score, but for a free app that settles arguments in ten seconds, 3.5 out of 5 is a fair reflection: fun, useful, and worth a spin.
