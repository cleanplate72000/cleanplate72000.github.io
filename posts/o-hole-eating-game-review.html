O! Hole! Review: Eat Everything (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. O! Hole! - Eating Game is a genuinely charming twist on the black-hole eating game: you steer Gulu, a friendly monster, around each level swallowing berries, trees and even geese, growing bigger as you eat, while a light story and new chapters keep you coming back. It loses a star because it contains ads and timers that some players say were not clear from its advertising, the download is a hefty 396 MB, and its Low Maturity rating means it is for older children and up, not young children.

O! Hole! - Eating Game, from developer SayGames Ltd, is a free casual game with a simple idea done with unusual warmth. You guide Gulu the monster — or, more precisely, Gulu's mouth — around a playing field, swallowing everything in your path. Eat enough and you grow, which lets you swallow bigger things. Each level sets goal items to eat, and later levels add barriers, timers and other obstacles, so the early free-for-all slowly turns into a game about planning your route instead of just hoovering up everything in sight.

Why review it now? Because it is climbing fast. AppBrain's tracking, updated on 25 October 2026, puts O! Hole! at about 220,000 installs, with roughly 89,000 of them in the last 30 days — around 3,000 a day — and the trending casual list for 26 October 2026 shows about 35,000 installs in 7 days, three times the week before. On the live Play listing it holds about 4.6 stars from more than 16,000 ratings (AppBrain's snapshot puts it at 4.64 stars from about 14,858 ratings), with Play showing a 100,000+ install bracket. The latest version listed is 1.2.1, updated on 22 October 2026, and the game has been on Google Play since October 2025. It is also in the top free casual charts in the United States, Japan, Canada, Norway and the United Kingdom, and reached number 89 in Top Grossing Casual in the United States.

How it plays

The core loop is easy to pick up in seconds, which is a large part of the appeal. You drag Gulu's mouth around the field and swallow items. Small items go down straight away; bigger items need you to grow first, so there is a natural order to each level: eat the berries and small creatures, grow a size or two, then tackle the trees, furniture and other large objects. Goal counters tell you how many of each target item you still need, and boosters — including a magnet that pulls items towards you — are there when a level gets awkward.

What lifts O! Hole! above the many plain hole games on the store is the presentation. SayGames built a story around Gulu, with new chapters opening as you complete levels, plus small character moments along the way — the store description proudly notes that a swallowed goose honks. The graphics are bright and chunky, the sound effects are silly rather than stressful, and the whole game reads as a cartoon, not a simulation. One recent five-star reviewer called it familiar but different in every level, praising the mini-games and power-ups; another praised the monster, the colours and the little notes Gulu leaves at the end of a level.

Levels, goals and difficulty

Levels are not all the same field with different items. The store screenshots show distinct settings — a garden, a kitchen-style board, an apple orchard and more — and the goals change with them. Some levels ask you to eat a set number of a particular item, others are about clearing the board efficiently before time runs out, and later levels add barriers that force you to think about the order in which you eat. That variety matters, because a plain eating game can get repetitive fast; here, the changing goals and the growing-size mechanic give each level a small puzzle to solve.

There is also a progression layer beyond individual levels. Completing levels opens new story chapters, and the game tracks your size and progress as you go. It is still a casual game at heart — sessions are short, controls are one-finger, and you do not need to learn a long rulebook — but there is enough structure to make "just one more level" feel like progress rather than repetition.

Key features

  • Steer Gulu's mouth to swallow items and grow bigger, unlocking larger targets as you grow
  • Story chapters that open as you complete levels, with Gulu as a recurring character rather than a plain hole
  • Varied level goals, barriers and timers that turn later levels into route-planning puzzles
  • Boosters, including a magnet, for levels where you get stuck
  • Bright cartoon graphics and playful sound effects, including the honking goose
  • Short, one-finger sessions that suit quick breaks

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Instantly understandable: steer, swallow, grow, repeat
  • Story and character give it more personality than plain hole games
  • Varied levels and goals, not the same board every time
  • Strong rating: about 4.6 stars from more than 16,000 ratings
  • Free to download, with regular updates — the latest arrived on 22 October 2026

Cons

  • Contains ads, and some players report the advertising suggested no ads and no timers, which they say is not the case — treat that as player reports, not a verified finding
  • Timers and later-level obstacles will frustrate players who want a purely relaxing game
  • Large download at about 396 MB — use Wi-Fi, not mobile data
  • Low Maturity rating: it is meant for older children and up, not young children
  • In-app purchases are offered; the exact pack prices were not shown on the Play page checked, so check before you buy

Pricing

O! Hole! - Eating Game is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The Play listing also signals in-app purchases. If ads bother you, try the free version for a few levels first — several reviewers say the ads are part of the price of a free game, while others found them intrusive, so your tolerance will decide whether any paid no-ads option is worth it. The current version is 1.2.1, updated 22 October 2026, and the download is about 396 MB, so download on Wi-Fi.
