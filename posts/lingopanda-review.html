Lingopanda Review: English & Spanish (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Lingopanda: Language Learning is a focused, speaking-first way to learn English or Spanish, with 1,000+ interactive videos, AI pronunciation feedback and real-life roleplays. It is free to start, rated Everyone, and genuinely trending in education — but go in with your eyes open: it covers only two languages, and the free tier is not the whole app, so check what sits behind the paywall before you commit.

Lingopanda: Language Learning, from developer Lingopanda (previously PingoLearn), has been on Google Play since November 2022 and is having a strong run in 2026. On its official Play listing it holds 4.5 stars from 96,500 reviews, with Play showing the 1,000,000+ installs bracket and an Everyone content rating. Independent tracker AppBrain puts total installs higher, at 2.5 million, with 130,000 of those in the last 30 days — about 4,300 a day — and the app in the top 20 free education apps in Brazil and top 30 in Turkey. Its latest version on Play is 3.9.3, updated on 17 October 2026. That combination — a mature app still growing fast, with a very large rating base — is why it earns a review this week.

All screenshots in this review are the developer's own promotional images from the official Google Play Store listing.

What Lingopanda does

Lingopanda takes a deliberately narrow approach. Instead of trying to teach dozens of languages shallowly, it teaches just two — English and Spanish — and it teaches them mainly through speaking. The Play description promises that you can master either language and speak confidently in just 30 days, with a lesson plan covering 200+ real-life topics and instant pronunciation and fluency feedback. Treat that 30-day claim as marketing, not a guarantee: how fast you improve depends on your starting level and how often you practise. What is real is the method — short, conversation-led lessons built around situations you actually meet, rather than long lists of isolated words.

The core loop is simple. Watch and listen to a native-speaker video, then speak yourself and get instant feedback on your pronunciation. The app grades your attempt, lets you revisit the words you mispronounced, and tracks your progress over time. That feedback loop is the whole point: many language apps test reading and listening well but leave speaking until last, while Lingopanda puts speaking first. For learners who understand a language better than they speak it — a very common problem — that ordering matters.

Key features

The Play listing groups the tools into several areas. First, AI speech recognition: pronunciation and fluency practice through interactive video lessons, with real-time feedback as you speak. Second, vocabulary and pronunciation training through AI-powered chats that correct mistakes instantly. Third, practical topics: 1,000+ real-life scenarios and occasions, so you practise the language of work, travel and everyday life rather than textbook sentences. Fourth, real conversations with other active learners in real time — a feature that can be genuinely useful, though the quality of any conversation with a stranger will vary. Fifth, progress tracking: instant grades, a list of mispronounced words to revisit, and a record of how you are improving. Sixth, engaging games and quizzes, so practice does not always feel like study. Seventh, reading practice through in-app book summaries, aimed at grammar and vocabulary. New content is added every week, according to the listing.

The listing also spells out who the app is for: students and learners from basic phrases to advanced conversation, business professionals who need office and professional language, and travellers and expats who want to speak confidently abroad. That range is honest — the same speaking-first method suits a job interview, a holiday, or a classroom — but it also means the app is not a deep grammar course. If you want systematic grammar explanations, you will probably need a textbook or a second resource alongside it.

Pros and cons

Pros: a clear speaking-first method with instant pronunciation feedback; 1,000+ interactive videos across real-life topics; a huge, established user base (96,500 Play reviews) with a strong 4.5-star average; an Everyone rating and an Android 10 and up requirement; and new content every week.

Cons: only English and Spanish — if you want French, German, Japanese or anything else, this is simply not your app. The free version is a starting point, not the full product: Play lists in-app purchases, and user reviews report being asked to pay after a few days to continue lessons, so expect a paywall. And the real-conversation feature depends on other learners being online and helpful, which no app can guarantee. One more caution: the app's own promotional images claim higher figures (2M+ learners, 4.7 stars) than the Play listing's current bracket and rating — judge it by the Play numbers above, not by the banner.

Pricing

Lingopanda is free to download on Google Play, with in-app purchases. The Play listing puts in-app purchases at $6.99–$99.99 per item, and prices can vary by region and offer, so check the current price inside the app before subscribing. The sensible approach is the same as with any freemium learning app: use the free lessons for several days, see whether the speaking feedback actually helps you, and only pay if the extra lessons and features are the reason you keep opening it. If you are comparing learning apps, our SmartyMe review covers a different kind of daily learning — short micro-lessons across many topics — while Lingopanda stays narrow and deep on speaking two languages.

Who should download it?

Download Lingopanda if you already know some English or Spanish and your real problem is speaking it out loud — in meetings, on trips, or in everyday conversation. It is also a good fit if short, video-led lessons suit you better than long grammar chapters, and if you like the idea of practising with an AI tutor without the pressure of a live class. Skip it if you want a different language, a deep grammar course, or a completely free app with no paywall — on all three counts, another app will serve you better.

Overall, Lingopanda earns its trending spot honestly: a focused idea, executed around speaking, with a large and happy user base behind it. Its limits are clear and easy to check before you pay anything. Start free, speak out loud for a week, and let your own progress — not the 30-day claim — decide whether it is worth keeping.
