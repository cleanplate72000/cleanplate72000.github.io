Sort Streak Review: Flower Sorting (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Sort Streak: Color Puzzle is a calm, genuinely pretty sorting game: slide colourful flowers along ropes until each colour sits together, then watch the completed group drop into its flower pot. It is easy to learn, satisfying to solve, and free — just expect ads, a chunky download, and levels that get properly tricky once locked and mystery flowers appear.

Sort Streak: Color Puzzle, from developer Oakever Games — the same studio behind the railway puzzler Connect Tracks, which we have also reviewed — arrived on Google Play in October 2026 and has already passed the 10,000+ install bracket on Play, with around 32,000 installs tracked in total, all in the last 30 days. On Google Play it holds 4.6 stars from about 1,467 ratings in the latest live listing check (an AppBrain snapshot from 23 October 2026 showed 4.2 stars from 1,025 ratings, so the score has climbed as more players have rated it), and it is climbing the puzzle charts, reaching #45 Top Free Puzzle in Brazil with top-100 puzzle showings in Portugal, Italy and Spain and a top-200 showing in India. For a game barely a month old, that is a fast, clean start — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The idea is simple, and that is the point. Colourful flowers hang on ropes, and your job is to sort them by colour, match them to the right rope, complete the level objectives, and fill the flower pots. Pick up a flower — or a connected group of flowers — from a rope, drag it onto an empty rope, or onto a rope whose free end already shows the same colour with enough space. When a full group of one colour is together on its matching rope, it collects into a flower pot. Fill the required pots and the level is cleared. The developer's own pitch sums it up: “Sort and match colorful flowers on ropes. Enjoy a satisfying sorting puzzle!”

How it plays

The loop is calm and readable, which is exactly what a relaxing puzzle needs. At the top of the screen sit the flower pots you must fill, with a counter showing your progress — 0/10, 1/10, 3/10 in the screenshots. Below them, the ropes hang in a row, each carrying a string of flowers in mixed colours. There is no timer breathing down your neck in the shots we checked, so you can take your time, look ahead, and plan the order of your moves.

But “relaxing” does not mean thoughtless. Rope space is limited, and every move can change the whole board. Move the wrong group first and you can block the rope you needed, leaving no legal move — and when there are no legal moves left, the level ends. That failure state is the game's real brain-teaser: you are not just matching colours, you are managing space, reading the order of flowers on each rope, and thinking two or three moves ahead. It is the same satisfying tension that makes the best sorting games work, dressed up in flowers instead of water tubes or train tracks.

Two twists that keep it fresh

Once the basics click, Sort Streak adds two special mechanics that stop the later levels from feeling like the early ones. Mystery Flowers hide their true colour until you work them to the free end of a rope — you have to move the flowers underneath to reveal them, then sort them by the colour they turn out to be. It is a small surprise, but it forces you to plan around unknowns instead of just matching what you can see.

Locked Flowers are the bigger obstacle. A locked flower cannot be moved at all until you complete the required colour objective or the required number of group objectives. That means some ropes are effectively frozen until you clear progress elsewhere, and you have to find a route through the puzzle that unlocks them in the right order. As the developer puts it, you must “read the flower order, overcome obstacles, manage limited space, and choose every move carefully.” In practice, that is where the game stops being a five-minute fidget and starts being a proper puzzle.

Tools, look and feel

When a level does get stuck, three tools are on hand: Hint, Undo, and Add a Rope. The screenshots show them as icons at the bottom of the screen, alongside a coin counter for in-game currency. Hint points you toward a useful move, Undo takes back a mistake, and an extra rope gives you the breathing room that a blocked board needs. How generously those tools are handed out — and whether they are earned by playing or topped up by watching ads — is something longer-term players will judge better than a first-week review can, so treat the tools as a safety net, not a guarantee.

Visually, the game is bright without being loud: soft blue backgrounds, chunky flower pots in teal, red, orange, yellow, green and purple, and flowers that are easy to tell apart by colour and shape. That readability matters in a sorting game — a misread colour is a wasted move — and the screenshots suggest Oakever has kept the board clean. The completed-group animation, where a finished colour collects into its pot, is the little dopamine hit the whole loop is built around.

Key features

  • Drag-and-drop flower sorting on ropes — pick up a flower or a connected group and place it on an empty or matching rope.
  • Flower-pot objectives — complete colour groups to fill pots and clear each level.
  • Mystery Flowers that reveal their true colour only when worked to the end of a rope.
  • Locked Flowers that cannot move until you complete the required objectives.
  • Hint, Undo and Add a Rope tools for when a board gets stuck.
  • Levels ranging from gentle introductions to complex, space-tight arrangements.
  • A relaxing, no-rush pace that still rewards logical thinking and spatial planning.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Instantly understandable — one drag-and-drop rule, no tutorial wall.
  • Pretty, readable board; colours and flower shapes are easy to distinguish.
  • Mystery and locked flowers genuinely change later levels.
  • Free to download, rated Everyone, and climbing the puzzle charts fast.
  • Satisfying pot-collection payoff for every completed colour group.
Cons
  • Contains ads, per the Play listing — expect interruptions between levels.
  • Around 125 MB download, which is chunky for a sorting puzzle.
  • Still a young rating base — long-term difficulty balance is unproven.
  • A board with no legal moves ends the level, which can frustrate new players.
  • Tool generosity over long play sessions is not yet clear from the listing.

Pricing and details

Sort Streak: Color Puzzle is free on Google Play and contains ads. The listing we checked did not show an in-app purchase price range, so we are not claiming one here. It is rated Everyone, runs about 125 MB (124.62 MB in the AppBrain snapshot), and the latest version is 1.0.4, updated 22 October 2026. Closing thoughts

Sort Streak is not reinventing the sorting puzzle, and it does not need to. It takes a proven loop, dresses it in flowers and ropes instead of bottles or tracks, and adds two mechanics — mystery and locked flowers — that give the later levels real teeth. The fast chart climb and rising rating suggest players are finding it at exactly the right moment: a new, polished, relaxing puzzler with room to grow. If you enjoy colour-sorting games and want something gentle that still makes you think, this one is worth a pot or two of your time. Just keep an eye on the ads, and remember: when the board looks stuck, look for the rope you have not used yet.
