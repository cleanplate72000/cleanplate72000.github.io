Draw One Puzzle Review: Draw It (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Draw One Puzzle: Brain Games is a simple idea executed with real charm: look at a half-finished picture, work out what is missing, and draw it in with one line — or erase the wrong part to reveal what is hidden. It is easy to pick up, genuinely funny in places, and backed by 39 million installs and a 4.6-star average. It loses a star for the thing reviewers complain about most: the ads, which several players describe as relentless. If you can tolerate frequent ad breaks, this is one of the most likeable brain teasers on Play; if you cannot, the same puzzles exist elsewhere with less interruption.

Draw One Puzzle: Brain Games, from developer Rebel Actions, is built around a single question: what is missing from this picture? Each level shows you a scene that is not quite complete — a rabbit with one ear missing, a cherry without its stalk, a man staring into an empty pizza box, a teddy bear with a leg to finish — and your job is to draw the missing part. Some levels flip the idea: instead of drawing, you erase part of the picture to reveal a hidden object or answer. The developer's own tagline sums it up: draw the missing part, use your brain, and solve the drawing puzzle.

The numbers show a game that is not just popular but still growing fast. Independent tracker AppBrain's 24 October 2026 snapshot puts it at 39 million installs, with 1.6 million of those in the last 30 days — about 54,000 a day. The live Google Play listing shows about 4.6 stars from over 107,000 ratings. It ranks #6 in Top Free Puzzle in Saudi Arabia, #13 in Russia and #21 in Indonesia, and it has been on Play since August 2024. The current version is 1.8.3, updated 7 October 2026, the download is about 115 MB, and it needs Android 8.0 or higher. It is free to download.

The whole game in one image: a rabbit, a pencil, and one missing ear. If that makes you smile, you will like what follows.

How it actually plays

The core loop could not be simpler, and that is the point. You are shown a picture, you think about what it needs, and you draw a line with your finger. Draw the rabbit's ear, the cherry's stalk, the teddy's leg, and the game fills in the rest and moves you on. Get it wrong and you try again — there is no timer in the screenshots, no lives counter breathing down your neck, and no complicated controls to learn. The Play description calls it a mind-bending experience that tests creativity, and in practice it is closer to a lateral-thinking doodle book: the answer is usually obvious once you see it, but working out what the game wants is the fun part.

The erase levels add a second flavour. Instead of adding a line, you rub part of the scene away to uncover something hidden — the Play description frames it as draw-or-erase, where each level presents a new scenario and you must figure out which part of the scene needs to disappear. That variety matters, because a game that only ever asked you to finish a drawing would get repetitive; switching between drawing and erasing keeps the same simple mechanic feeling fresh for longer. A lightbulb hint button sits at the bottom of every level in the screenshots, so when you are genuinely stuck, help is one tap away rather than a dead end.

Level 2: a cherry waiting for its stalk. Early levels teach the idea in seconds — draw the missing line and the picture completes.

Why it is trending

Draw One Puzzle is trending because it is instantly readable. You do not need a tutorial, a story, or a genre vocabulary to understand a half-drawn rabbit — anyone can look at a level and immediately want to have a go. That makes it perfect for short sessions: one level while waiting, a few more while the kettle boils, and the level counter quietly climbing (the screenshots show levels 2, 82 and 102, so there is plenty of runway). Players on Play back that up: one reviewer calls it funny and says there is nothing wrong with it, another says they play it whenever they are bored, and a third says they are having so much fun with it. For a free puzzle game, that kind of easy affection is exactly what drives 54,000 installs a day.

It also helps that the art is bright and the jokes land. The levels are little visual gags as much as puzzles — the man with the empty pizza box, the teddy that needs a leg — so solving a level often comes with a small laugh, not just a tick. That humour is what separates it from drier brain-training apps: you are not being tested, you are being entertained, and the puzzle is the excuse. With 39 million installs behind it, it is also a safe recommendation in the sense that millions of players have already road-tested the idea for you.

Level 82: the joke levels are the best ones — a man, a pizza cap, and a box with nothing in it. What is missing is the punchline.

Key features

  • Draw the missing part: complete half-finished pictures with a single line drawn by your finger
  • Erase puzzles: rub away part of a scene to reveal hidden objects and answers
  • Hundreds of levels, from quick early wins (Level 2) to much later challenges (Level 102 and beyond)
  • Hint button on every level, so a stuck puzzle never becomes a dead end
  • Simple controls: no tutorial needed, just draw, erase and think
  • Bright, cartoon art with funny, everyday scenarios
  • Short-session friendly: one level takes seconds, so it fits into any spare moment

Level 102: a teddy bear waiting for its leg. A hundred levels in, the idea is still the same — and still satisfying when the line lands.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Instantly understandable: no tutorial, no learning curve, just draw what is missing
  • Genuinely funny levels, not just abstract puzzles — the visual gags carry the game
  • Two mechanics in one: drawing and erasing, so the loop stays fresh longer
  • Proven at scale: 39 million installs and a 4.6-star average from over 107,000 ratings
  • Free to download, with a hint button when you get stuck
  • Perfect for short bursts: one level takes seconds
Cons
  • Ads are the top complaint by a distance: one reviewer says there are so many ads they are furious, and another says you cannot play even for 10 seconds without them — treat these as player reports, not verified measurements, but the pattern is consistent
  • Data safety note: the Play listing says the app may share location, app activity and two other data types, that data is not encrypted, and that it cannot be deleted
  • About 115 MB is chunky for a simple drawing game — use Wi-Fi for the download
  • The puzzles are gentle rather than hard, so players looking for a real brain-burner may find it too easy
  • Very similar in spirit to other draw-one-part games, so it will not feel new if you already play one

Pricing

Draw One Puzzle: Brain Games is free to download on Google Play, with the Play listing price shown as 0. The cost, in practice, is your attention: reviewers consistently report frequent ads between levels, so the free version is best tried with that expectation set. There is no subscription mentioned on the listing checked for this review, and the game itself — all those levels of drawing and erasing — is fully playable without paying. If the ad frequency bothers you after a few levels, you will know quickly, and you will have spent nothing to find out. The current version is 1.8.3, updated 7 October 2026, and the download is about 115 MB.
