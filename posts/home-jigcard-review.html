Home Jigcard Solitaire Puzzle Review (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Home Jigcard Solitaire Puzzle is a gentle, genuinely relaxing hybrid: solitaire-style card moves meet jigsaw puzzles, and every finished picture unlocks several home items. It is free, beautifully calm, and already a chart climber in its first month — but expect ads between rounds, and note that its own data-safety section says collected data is not encrypted.

Home Jigcard Solitaire Puzzle, from developer LoveColoring Game, arrived on Google Play in September 2026 and has already reached about 68,000 installs — every one of them in its first 30 days, according to AppBrain data updated 23 October 2026. It holds 4.87 out of 5 stars from 4,720 ratings on its live Play listing, and ranks in the top ten of Top New Free Casual games in several countries, including a number-five showing in Italy. For a quiet puzzle game with no big brand behind it, that is a fast, well-liked start — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The idea is simple and immediately appealing. Every jigsaw piece becomes a card. You move cards around, flip face-down pieces to reveal hidden sections, and follow colours, textures and outlines to find the picture sections that connect. When the last piece snaps into place, a full home scene is revealed — a sunlit living room, a snug bedroom, a charming kitchen, or a little garden full of flowers — and you unlock several home items at once, adding to a growing collection of houses you love.

How it works

Each round starts with a scattered set of picture cards. Some are face up, showing part of a home scene; others are face down, hiding their section until you flip them. Your job is to move cards, reveal the hidden ones, and spot the matches: a roofline that continues onto the next piece, a stretch of water, a patch of garden, or the outline of a piece of furniture. Connect the sections in the right order and the picture snaps into focus, piece by piece, until the whole scene is complete.

That is where the reward loop kicks in. Finishing a picture does not just end the round — it unlocks several home items and can reveal a new house to add to your collection. The game is built around that little moment of anticipation: what will this round unlock, and which house comes next? It is a gentler loop than a timer or a lives system, and it suits short, relaxed sessions — a puzzle during a break, or a few rounds before bed.

Key features

  • Solitaire meets jigsaw. Card moves, face-down reveals and picture matching in one calm mechanic — no timer pressure.
  • One round, several rewards. Every completed picture unlocks multiple home items, not just a single prize.
  • A collection of houses. Finish rounds to discover new houses and home scenes, from treehouses to lakeside cottages.
  • Big, readable pieces. Players praise the large pieces and gentle difficulty, which keep the game relaxing rather than fiddly.
  • Harder levels as you progress. Later rounds, such as the marked Hard Levels, use busier scenes with many more sections to place.
  • Save your finished pictures. Completed puzzle pictures can be saved without a watermark after watching an ad or two, according to player reviews.

Pros and cons

Pros: genuinely relaxing pace; a satisfying one-round, many-rewards loop; beautiful, cosy home artwork; large pieces that are easy to read; strong early ratings from thousands of players; free to start.

Cons: contains ads, including ads to save finished pictures without a watermark; the Play listing's data-safety section says data is not encrypted and cannot be deleted; some players expected Christmas-only pictures and found everyday scenes instead, so go in expecting a mix of home themes, not a seasonal set.

Pricing and details

Home Jigcard Solitaire Puzzle is free to download on Android and contains ads. It was updated on 30 September 2026, and the download is about 87 MB — a modest size for a picture-heavy puzzle game, so it should download fine over mobile data as well as Wi-Fi. Its Play listing says no data is shared with third parties, though it may collect device identifiers, and that data is not encrypted — worth knowing if you are privacy-conscious, even for a casual puzzle game.
