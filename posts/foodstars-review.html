Foodstars Review: Merge & Cook (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Foodstars: Merge & Cook is a warm, well-made merge puzzle: merge ingredients, cook dishes from around the world, and rebuild restaurants while working towards Michelin-level chef stars. It is free, rated Everyone, and growing fast — just be ready for a big 435 MB download and the energy limits that slow down higher levels.

Foodstars: Merge & Cook, from developer Happibits Game, is one of the fastest-growing puzzle games on Google Play right now. It holds 4.5 stars from about 19,900 ratings, has been installed roughly 5.4 million times, and about 1.2 million of those installs came in the last 30 days — around 40,000 a day. It is ranked #9 in Top Free Puzzle in Brazil and #4 in Top Grossing Puzzle in India. The app has been on Play since October 2025, and its latest version, 1.53.0, arrived on 20 October 2026. With an Everyone content rating and a free download, it is an easy family-friendly pick — and that sharp recent surge is why it earns a review here this week.

The idea is simple and satisfying. You merge matching ingredients and kitchen items to make better ones, use them to complete customer orders, and spend your progress on renovating restaurants around the world. There is no timer pressure forcing you to rush, and the game layers a light story on top: you travel, meet chefs, and collect recipes as you go. If you like cosy puzzle games where every board clears into something tangible, this is exactly that kind of game.

Foodstars in its spring event art: merge ingredients on the board to unlock dishes and decorate your restaurant.

How Foodstars plays

Each board starts with a set of producers — things like ingredient crates and kitchen tools — that spit out items when you tap them. Merge two identical items and they become a higher-level one: two strawberries become a better strawberry dish, and so on, until you have exactly what a customer ordered. Orders ask for specific dishes, and completing them earns coins, stars and progress towards the next restaurant renovation. It is the classic merge loop, but the cooking theme keeps it fresh: you are always working towards a recognisable dish rather than an abstract object.

Special events keep the loop from going stale. The screenshots show a Special Event board, a Merge and Cook board, and a timed Star Chef Fever mode, so there is usually something beyond the main orders to chase. Daily play, in other words, is not just the same board repeated — events, new restaurants and new chefs rotate in regularly, which matches the developer's record of frequent updates.

A Special Event board: merge farm ingredients to clear event orders and earn bonus rewards.

500+ recipes and restaurants around the world

The developer promises more than 500 recipes, and the world tour is the part that sets Foodstars apart from plainer merge games. The Play description name-checks Italian classics made with tomatoes and basil, French dishes with gin and lemons, and German food with sausages and bread rolls. Restaurants follow the same tour: a roadside diner in New York, a traditional Kaiseki eatery in Tokyo, a seafood bistro in Marseille and a coconut curry spot in Thailand. Renovation tasks are tied to completing orders, so decorating a restaurant feels earned rather than bought outright.

Chef Trials add a second goal. World-class chefs with different personalities set challenges, and completing them earns their recognition and their signature dishes. On top of that, global cooking challenges award Chef Star badges, building towards the Michelin-level stars the game advertises. For completionists, that gives Foodstars a longer tail than a merge game that only offers endless boards.

The core loop in action: merge lemons step by step until they become a finished lemonade.

What players actually say

The rating tells a broadly happy story, and the reviews behind it are enthusiastic about the basics. One October 2026 reviewer who played for days straight praised the game as super fun and said they still had energy and diamonds to spare, while another said they had not got stuck by level 20. The characters, the story and the steady stream of merges come up again and again as reasons people keep playing.

The complaints are just as consistent, and worth knowing before you download. Several reviewers say that at higher levels — around level 30 and beyond — energy refills slowly, orders get more complicated, and progress stalls unless you pay for extra power. One reviewer also complained about half a dozen sales pop-ups before they could even play, and another about a merge-hint hand that stayed on screen even after hints were turned off. None of this makes the game unplayable, but it does mean the free ride gets slower the further you get.

Star Chef Fever mode: complete fast orders against the clock to ignite bonus rewards.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Satisfying merge-and-cook loop with 500+ recipes to unlock
  • Restaurant renovation across real world locations, from New York to Tokyo
  • Chef Trials and Chef Star badges give long-term goals beyond endless boards
  • Strong momentum: 4.5 stars, about 19,900 ratings, 5.4 million installs, 1.2 million in the last 30 days
  • Everyone content rating, free to download, regular updates
  • Special events and timed modes keep daily play varied

Cons

  • Very large download at about 435 MB — check storage before installing
  • Energy and power limits slow progress noticeably at higher levels, reviewers say
  • Sales pop-ups on opening frustrate some players
  • In-app purchases range from $0.99 to $99.99 per item on Google Play
  • Play's data-safety section says data is not encrypted, though deletion can be requested

Pricing and practical details

Foodstars is free to download on Google Play, with in-app purchases from $0.99 to $99.99 per item. Treat anything inside the game as optional and check the price on screen before tapping buy — especially if children play on a shared device. The game needs Android 8.0 or higher. At about 435 MB it is one of the bigger puzzle downloads on this site, so it is worth clearing some space first; on the plus side, that size buys detailed art and a lot of content rather than a thin game padded with ads. Google Play does not list Foodstars as containing ads.
