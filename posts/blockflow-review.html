BlockFlow Review: Cube Sorting (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. BlockFlow - Traffic Hub is a genuinely relaxing sort-and-deliver puzzle with a twist: instead of sorting balls or water, you dispatch tiny pixel workers who haul coloured cubes onto trains bound for matching docks. It is free, it carries an Everyone content rating, Google Play lists 100,000+ installs, and AppBrain counts about 210,000 installs — roughly 180,000 of them in the last 30 days. The catch is repetition and size: at around 203 MB it is a chunky download for a casual puzzle, and several players report levels starting to repeat, which keeps it at 4.0 rather than higher.

BlockFlow - Traffic Hub, from developer Pine Stars Technology Limited-games, arrived on Google Play in August 2026 and was updated to version 17 on 22 October 2026. Its Play listing shows around 4.7 stars (4.75 from 4,459 ratings on the live Play fetch; AppBrain's crawl showed 4.73 from 3,793), and its rating histogram leans heavily five-star. That is a strong start for a game barely two months old, and the growth backs it up: AppBrain records roughly 6,100 installs a day over the last 30 days, which is why it keeps appearing on October 2026 trending lists.

The core loop is simple to learn and easy to enjoy. Each level gives you a pixel-art board made of coloured cubes — a red panda, butterflies, a strawberry cake — plus a train at the bottom and just five slots to work with. You tap a container to place it into a slot, and your little hauling workers spring into action: they travel along the board edges, pick up cubes of matching colours, and toss them into train carriages heading for the corresponding docks. Sort every colour and clear the board to win.

The five-slot limit is where the brain work lives. Every placement counts, because one wrong move can jam the whole transport route. You have to plan ahead, keep an empty slot in reserve, and arrange your workers in the right order — it feels closer to a logistics puzzle than to the usual sort-and-match games, and that is exactly what makes it stand out. Later levels add hidden containers and linked containers, so the difficulty curve keeps climbing instead of flattening out.

Two things deserve praise that many free puzzle games cannot claim. First, there are no timers and the game works offline, so it is genuinely relaxing — the developer describes it as suitable for commutes, bedtime and spare moments, and the slow, satisfying worker animations back that up. Second, the presentation is charming rather than generic: the pixel scenes span plants, fruits, animals, castles, toys and more, and players repeatedly call out how cute the little workers are in their reviews.

Key features:

  • Sort-and-deliver puzzle: workers haul coloured cubes onto trains bound for matching docks
  • Only five slots per level, so every placement is a real decision
  • Hundreds of pixel-art levels across themes like plants, fruits, animals, castles and toys
  • New mechanics over time, including hidden containers and linked containers
  • Helper power-ups: Shuffle, Grab and Vacuum (clears all cubes of one colour in one tap)
  • Offline play with no time limits and no countdown pressure

Pros: a fresh take on the sorting genre; genuinely relaxing with no timers; offline play; charming pixel art and likeable workers; a real difficulty curve; free to start with an Everyone rating.

Cons: a hefty ~203 MB download; several players report puzzles repeating at higher levels, which dulls the novelty; a few report occasional freezes mid-level; one player reported a slot-machine-style screen that would not close without restarting the app; another reported a permanent bottom ad blocking on-screen buttons after an update. The Play listing also notes the app may share app activity with third parties, that data is not encrypted, and that data cannot be deleted — worth knowing before you install.

Pricing: BlockFlow - Traffic Hub is free on Google Play and contains ads. The listing material we checked does not disclose in-app purchase prices, so treat any in-game shop as unverified until you open the current Play page — check the in-app purchases line there before spending anything. The core game — sorting, delivering and clearing boards — is playable without paying.

If you like traffic and sorting puzzles, our Bus Parade review covers another colour-matching traffic jam game — Bus Parade is about matching passengers to buses, while BlockFlow is the slower, calmer logistics puzzle of the two.

Download BlockFlow - Traffic Hub on Google Play

The bottom line: BlockFlow earns its trending spot the honest way: a likeable twist on sorting games, a satisfying worker-and-train loop, and genuinely relaxing, timer-free play. The 203 MB size, the repeating levels at higher stages, and a handful of real bug and ad-placement complaints keep it at 4.0 rather than higher, but as a free, Everyone-rated puzzle that is clearly still growing, it is an easy recommendation for puzzle fans who want something calmer than the usual frantic sort games. Just check the Play page's current purchase details first, and keep an eye on your storage.
