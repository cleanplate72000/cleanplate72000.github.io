Supermarket.io Review: Shop Tycoon (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Supermarket.io: Store Tycoon is October 2026's newest supermarket breakout: a farm-to-shelf idle game from Kooapps Games in which you grow produce, stock shelves and turn a tiny shop into a retail empire. It has an Everyone content rating, 100,000+ downloads on Google Play, and a 3.8-star rating from 339 ratings — a promising start, but a middling score that says this is a fun loop still finding its polish, not a finished classic. It is free, it contains ads, and it offers in-app purchases from $0.99 to $19.99 per item.

Supermarket.io: Store Tycoon, from developer Kooapps Games | Fun Arcade and Casual Action Games, does exactly what its name promises: it hands you a small supermarket and asks you to grow it into an empire. But unlike the many shop games that start at the checkout, this one starts in the field. You create farm plots, grow crops and raise animals, process the raw goods into products, and then stock the shelves of your own store. Lettuce, cow milk, butter and pasta are among the goods the Play listing names, and the loop is satisfyingly complete: what you farm is what you sell, and what you sell funds the next expansion.

The game arrived on Google Play on 14 October 2026, was updated to version 1.0.6 on 16 October 2026, and needs Android 7.0 or later. Independent tracker AppBrain lists it at the top of its 24 October 2026 trending free Casual chart, as the newest game on that list, with roughly 190,000 installs in total and about 24 times the previous week's installs. That is a genuine breakout pace for a game barely ten days old — and it explains why it is showing up on so many phones this week, even though its rating base is still small.

The core loop has three stages, and the game keeps them tightly linked. First, you farm: plant crops, tend animals and harvest produce. Second, you process and upgrade: machines and farm upgrades turn basic ingredients into higher-value goods, so the same shelf space earns more as you progress. Third, you sell: customers browse the aisles, buy what they need, and leave money at the cashier and through deliveries, which you collect and reinvest. Hiring a supermarket worker helps with collecting, restocking and serving customers, which matters because the store gets busier exactly when your farm needs attention too.

That tension — farm versus shop floor — is the game's best idea. In a pure idle game you watch numbers rise; here you are constantly choosing where your attention goes. Expand the store and you earn more cash, but you also need more stock. Upgrade a farm and your shelves fill faster, but only if you have remembered to hire help for the shop. The relaxing background music and simple controls keep it low-pressure, and the idle earnings mean a session can be five minutes or fifty: the store keeps ticking over while you are away, so progress never feels punished by a busy day.

Key features

  • Farm and shop in one game. Grow crops, raise animals, harvest produce and turn it into goods for your own shelves.
  • Store management. Build and manage your supermarket, sell fresh food, produce and dairy, complete customer orders and collect cashier and delivery income.
  • Upgrades that compound. Upgrade farms and processing stations to sell more products, then expand the store to earn cash faster.
  • Hire help. Supermarket workers collect, restock and serve customers so the shop keeps running while you farm.
  • Customisation. Dress your character in different outfits and hats, and keep one of the game's pets for company.
  • Idle-friendly play. Quick sessions or longer ones both work, with idle money earned between visits and relaxing music throughout.

Pros and cons

What works: the farm-to-shelf loop is coherent and satisfying — every upgrade is visible on a shelf or in a field, not just a bigger number. The Everyone rating, simple controls and short-session design make it genuinely family-friendly and easy to pick up. The art is bright and readable, customers and workers are easy to follow at a glance, and the idle earnings remove the pressure to grind.

What to watch: the 3.8-star rating is the honest warning label. With only 339 ratings so far, early players are clearly enjoying the idea more than every detail of the execution — new games at this pace often ship with balance quirks, and the update history is only two days long. Ads are part of the package, and in-app purchases run to $19.99 per item, so free players should expect the usual idle-game trade: patience, or payment, to speed things up. Players who dislike waiting for timers or watching ads for boosts will feel that friction sooner than most.

Pricing

Supermarket.io: Store Tycoon is free to download on Google Play. It contains ads and offers in-app purchases from $0.99 to $19.99 per item. Nothing in the listing suggests a subscription is required to play the full game, and the core loop — farming, stocking and expanding — is playable without paying. Treat purchases as shortcuts for impatient tycoons, not as a toll gate.

If you like management games with a hands-on twist, our Bus Parade review covers another October 2026 breakout built around a satisfying daily loop. Supermarket.io is the cosier of the two: less puzzle, more routine, and a farm attached to the shop.

Download Supermarket.io on Google Play

The bottom line: Supermarket.io: Store Tycoon earns its trending spot the honest way — a clear, relaxing loop that connects farming to retail instead of just dressing one up as the other. The 3.8-star score and the ads are real caveats, and a game ten days old can still change fast in either direction. But as a free, family-safe idle game to dip into between other things, it is an easy recommendation right now: download it, plant something, stock a shelf, and see whether your little shop starts to feel like an empire.
