Praja App Review: Local News, Nearby (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.4 / 5. Praja App is a genuinely useful idea done well: a local social network that keeps you in touch with news, events and people from your own village, mandal or district, instead of the endless global feed of the big social apps. It is free, hugely popular in India with a 5.0-star average, and it keeps improving — just watch out for the paid extras inside it, and remember that anything you post is shared with your whole local circle.

Praja App, from developer Circleapp Online Services Pvt. Ltd., is one of October 2026's fast risers on Google Play's Top Free Social charts in India, where it has reached #34. On the live Play listing it holds a rare 5.0 stars from about 28,400 ratings, sits in the 1,000,000+ installs bracket, and was updated in October 2026 (version 2610.22.04). AppBrain's tracking puts its total installs at about 4 million, with roughly 380,000 installs in the last 30 days — around 13,000 a day — which explains why it is climbing the trending lists right now.

The pitch is simple, and it fills a gap the big networks ignore. National news tells you what happened in Delhi or Mumbai; Praja App tells you what happened in your own village, mandal or district. You set your location during setup, join your local circle, and then you can share local news yourself or browse your timeline to see what neighbours are posting. A road closure, a local festival, a school event, a lost-and-found notice — the small news that actually affects your day, from the people who live next door.

Your neighbourhood, in one feed

Setup is built around place, not popularity. You tell the app your district, mandal and village, and it connects you with users from the same locality. Every post you share goes to the users in your circle, so a large local community means very little local news gets missed. Like any social app, you can react to other people's posts and discuss the news and events happening in your region — but the conversation is about your area, not the whole internet.

The app supports several Indian languages, as the screenshots show: the language picker lists Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Kerala, among others, so local news arrives in the language your community actually speaks. That matters for a local network: a village notice is no use to anyone if it is written in a language the village does not read.

More than news: circles, celebrities and creators

Praja App is not only a news feed. You can follow celebrities and friends to see their updates, and join fan circles of your favourite politicians — the promo artwork shows leader circles front and centre, so this is clearly a big part of how people use the app. Recent updates have added more creative tools too: Praja Celebrations, My Studio for creating posters and videos, and an AI Studio for personal AI photos. The screenshots show the poster side in action — colourful festival and celebration templates for local leaders and events.

That creator side is a double-edged sword, and it is worth being honest about. Poster and AI-photo tools are fun for festival wishes and local campaign posts, but they are also the part of the app where paid extras live. The app itself is free, but some users report subscription charges — one recent reviewer mentions a ₹2,399 subscription and a refund that had to be chased. Treat any in-app subscription like any other: check the price and the renewal terms before you tap confirm, and check your Play subscriptions if a charge surprises you.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely local feed for your village, mandal or district; a rare 5.0-star average from 28,000+ ratings; fast-growing, with about 13,000 installs a day; support for several Indian languages; free to download; poster and video tools for festivals and local events; a clean, simple interface.

Cons: some users report unexpected subscription charges around the paid extras, so check before subscribing; the Play data-safety section says the app may share app activity and device IDs with third parties, and it collects personal information — so think before posting personal details to a whole local circle; AppBrain lists a High Maturity content rating, so this is an app for adults rather than children; and, as with any local network, it is only as good as the community in your area — a quiet circle means a quiet feed.

Pricing

Praja App is free to download on Google Play, and the developer says all of the core local-network features are available for free. Paid extras exist around the poster, celebration and AI Studio tools, so the free version covers the local news and community features, while the creative extras may ask you to pay. The app is about 46 MB and needs Android 5.1 or later. An iOS version also exists on the App Store.

Download

Download Praja App on Google Play

If you live in India and feel that the big social apps never tell you what is happening in your own neighbourhood, Praja App is worth a try: a 5.0-star average, millions of installs, and a feed that is actually about your village, mandal or district. Just keep the two caveats in mind — check any subscription before you pay, and post as if your whole neighbourhood is reading, because it is.
