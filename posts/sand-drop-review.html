Sand Drop Review: Sandy Sorting Fun (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.3 / 5. Sand Drop is a calm, satisfying sorting puzzle: carve a path through a pixel-art picture, pour the coloured sand into the matching buckets, and watch a new image emerge grain by grain. It is easy to pick up, genuinely relaxing, and free — just expect ads, a thin tutorial, and the odd stuck-grain hiccup.

Sand Drop: Color Sort Puzzle, from developer Lion Studios Plus, launched on Google Play in June 2026 and has already passed 100,000+ installs — with around 79,000 of those in the last 30 days alone, taking its tracked total to roughly 120,000. On Google Play it holds 4.6 stars from over 1,200 ratings, and it is climbing the free puzzle charts, reaching #61 in Canada with top-100 puzzle showings in Denmark, Australia, Norway and the United Kingdom. For a sorting game in one of the most crowded genres on the store, that is a strong run — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The twist is simple but fresh. Most sorting games ask you to pour coloured water between tubes; Sand Drop turns the whole puzzle into a picture. Each level is a colourful pixel-art image made of sand — a cat among daisies, a row of houses, a sailboat on the waves. You tap a colour to release it, or drag your finger to carve a path through the image. The loose sand pours down a funnel onto a moving loop, then flows into colour-coded buckets below. Each bucket shows how many grains it still needs: fill it with the matching colour and it clears, making room for the next one.

How it plays

The catch — and the puzzle — is the loop's fill meter. Sand keeps arriving while you work, and if too much of it piles up on the loop, you run out of space. So every move is a small decision: which colour do you drop now, which do you hold back, and in what order do the buckets clear? Release the wrong colour at the wrong moment and the loop clogs; plan the sequence well and the whole picture drains away in a satisfying cascade, bucket by bucket.

There is no timer and no move limit breathing down your neck, which players consistently praise: one reviewer calls it a game you can play at your own speed, another loves simply watching the sand move and relax. Controls are one-finger simple — tap, drag, watch it pour — and the developer pitches the whole thing as a relaxing, ASMR-style experience with calm visuals and smooth controls. The promo screenshots show the range well: a black cat with pink ears among white daisies, a street of colourful houses mid-pour with the loop meter at 40%, and a bright sailboat picture waiting for its first pour.

Why it is trending

Sand Drop is trending because it gives a familiar genre something new to look at. Colour sorting is everywhere on Google Play, but a puzzle that reveals a picture as you solve it feels different from yet another tube puzzle — every level ends with a small reveal, not just an empty board. The pixel-art style reads instantly in a store screenshot, the sand physics are genuinely pleasant to watch, and the loop-meter mechanic adds just enough planning to keep it from feeling automatic.

It also helps that the game respects your time. Levels are quick to play — ideal for a coffee break or a commute — and the difficulty comes from planning the pour order, not from a countdown or a lives system. At about 127 MB, version 0.8.4 (updated 20 October 2026), it is a fair-sized download for a polished puzzle game, and small enough to try on a whim.

Key features

  • Pixel-art sand pictures — every level is a new image to sort, from animals to houses to boats.
  • Tap-and-carve controls — tap a colour to release it, or drag a path through the picture with one finger.
  • Satisfying sand physics — watch every grain pour, slide down the funnel and drain into the buckets.
  • Loop fill meter — manage the flow so the loop never overflows; order and timing are the real puzzle.
  • Colour-coded buckets — each bucket shows the grains it still needs, so progress is always clear.
  • No timers or move limits — play at your own pace, in short bursts or long relaxing sessions.
  • Relaxing ASMR feel — calm visuals, smooth controls and a gentle pace, pitched at adults and casual players.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Fresh twist on colour sorting — puzzles that reveal a pixel-art picture as you solve them
  • Strong 4.6 stars from over 1,200 ratings, with around 79,000 installs in the last 30 days
  • No timers or move limits — genuinely relaxing, play-at-your-own-pace puzzling
  • Simple one-finger controls and a clear loop-meter mechanic that rewards planning
  • Everyone content rating, and a new picture in every level keeps it fresh
Cons
  • Contains ads, as expected from a free game — one player reports ads appearing from around level 135
  • Thin tutorial: some new players report feeling lost at first, with no proper walkthrough
  • Occasional glitches reported — one player describes sand freezing mid-level and forcing a restart
  • One player reports that offline play now needs the paid ad-free version, so check before you travel
  • One game type done well, rather than a variety of modes — know what you are downloading

Pricing

Sand Drop is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The Play listing shows a price of $0 and no in-app purchase price range, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 0.8.4, updated 20 October 2026, and the download is about 127 MB — a fair size for a polished puzzle game, and worth it if the sand-pouring loop clicks for you.
