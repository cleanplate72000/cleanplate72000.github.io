Reampo Review: PDF Converter & Maker (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Reampo: PDF Converter & Maker does one everyday job well: it turns photos, scans and office files into clean PDFs in a few taps, then lets you sign, merge and read them in the same app. The catch is its age — it launched in October 2026, so it has almost no ratings yet to judge it by, and its listing says it contains ads. Worth trying for free; judge the ads before you rely on it daily.

Reampo: PDF Converter & Maker, from developer DongRuaLabs, is one of the fastest-climbing productivity apps on Google Play right now. It has been installed roughly 290,000 times in total, and — unusually — all of those installs came in its first couple of weeks, after its launch in October 2026, at roughly 23,000 installs a day. It reached #2 in Top New Free Overall in Belgium and top-10 new-free rankings in France, Poland, Germany, Austria, Denmark, Finland, Norway and Slovakia, and #1 in Top New Free Productivity in India in the chart snapshot checked. It carries an Everyone content rating, is free, and its listing says it contains ads. That sharp, genuinely recent surge is why it earns a review here this week — even though, as a brand-new app, it does not yet have enough ratings for a star score we can quote.

The pitch is simple, and it is aimed squarely at everyday paperwork: students turning lecture photos into one PDF, professionals signing an offer letter on the go, parents scanning school forms. Reampo keeps each of those tasks short, according to the developer — pick your files, tap through, and you get a single PDF ready to share. The developer's tagline sums it up: “Convert image to PDF or scan to PDF in seconds. Read and sign documents easily.”

What Reampo actually does

Reampo covers the full small-document workflow in one place, rather than making you hop between a scanner, a converter and a reader:

  • Photo to PDF — pick one photo or several and save them as a single PDF, in the order you choose. The developer pitches it for receipts, class notes and ID copies.
  • Scan to PDF — use your camera as a document scanner and save the scan as a PDF, without a separate scanning app.
  • Office files to PDF — convert DOC, XLS, PPT and TXT files to PDF, so a document keeps the same layout on every phone and computer.
  • Merge and reorder — combine PDF files into one document, then move pages into the order you need before you save it.
  • Sign PDFs — create a signature and place it on contracts, forms and letters, so you can sign on your phone without printing.
  • Password protection — add a password to lock a PDF before you share it, so only people with the password can open it.
  • Read and annotate — read PDF, DOC, XLS, PPT and TXT files in one reader, search your files, and mark favourites. Annotation tools include a pencil, highlighter, underline and strikethrough, stamps, and a cover pen to hide details, plus adding typed text to a page.
  • Simple file manager — find recent files at a glance, then rename, share, print, delete or check the details of a file.

How it works, step by step

The photo-to-PDF flow is the clearest example of how Reampo wants to feel: open the app, pick your photos, tap Next, and the PDF maker saves one file ready to share. Scanning follows the same pattern — point the camera at the document and save it as a PDF. Merging is similarly direct: combine files, drag pages into order, save. None of this is revolutionary, and that is arguably the point. For a utility app, a short, predictable flow beats a clever one, especially when the document in question is a form you need to send in the next five minutes.

Signing and password protection are the two features that lift Reampo above a bare photo-to-PDF tool. A signature you can drop onto a contract, and a password on a file before it leaves your phone, are the features people actually need once they start handling real paperwork on a mobile — and having them in the same app as the converter means one less tool to install and learn.

What to watch out for

Three honest caveats matter more than any feature list here. First, there is no rating to lean on yet. The listing checked had too few ratings to judge what users think, so this review rests on the developer's description and the install surge, not on a settled user verdict — that is why the verdict above is a cautious 3.5 out of 5 rather than a confident score. Second, the app contains ads, according to its Play listing, and a new app's ad load can change quickly; try it on a throwaway document first and see whether the ads interrupt the workflow. Third, it asks for all-files access on Android 11 and above, to open, save and manage PDF documents. The developer says your files stay on your device, which is reassuring as far as it goes, but all-files access is a broad permission — grant it knowingly, and only if you actually plan to use the app for real documents. We also could not verify any premium tier or in-app purchase prices from the listing checked, so if the app offers one inside, check what it actually unlocks before paying anything.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Photo, scan and office files to PDF in one free app
  • Signing, merging, reordering and password protection included
  • Reads and annotates PDF, DOC, XLS, PPT and TXT files
  • Very fast recent growth: about 290,000 installs in its first two weeks
  • Everyone content rating, with a short, predictable workflow
  • Top new-free productivity rankings in India and across Europe

Cons

  • No usable star rating yet — too new to judge from user feedback
  • Listing says it contains ads
  • Asks for broad all-files access on Android 11 and above
  • Premium or in-app purchase details could not be verified from the listing checked
  • As a new app, long-term reliability and update support are still unproven

Should you download it?

Yes, if you need a free PDF maker right now and you are happy to be an early user. Reampo covers the whole everyday chain — convert, scan, sign, merge, protect, read — in a single free app, and its breakneck first fortnight suggests a lot of people are finding exactly that useful. Go in with the caveats in mind: expect ads, grant the file permission deliberately, and test it on an unimportant document first.
