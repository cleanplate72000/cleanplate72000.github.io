100 Doors Escape from Prison Review (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. 100 Doors - Escape from Prison is a satisfying, old-school escape-room puzzler: you search a detailed prison cell, spot the hidden clue, crack the code and open the door. It is free, it has a huge number of levels, and its 4.5-star rating from more than 280,000 players is well earned for the core puzzle loop. It loses a star for frequent ads, for levels that vary a lot in difficulty and logic, and for a download that is far bigger than the listing's "almost no memory" claim suggests. It is also rated Medium Maturity, so it is a game for teens and adults rather than young children.

100 Doors - Escape from Prison, from developer Peaksel Games, puts you in the shoes of Megan, a young journalist who finds herself trapped inside a prison building. Your job is to help her get out, one door at a time. Each level is a single room — a cell, with a bed, a sink, a table, a barred door and a scattering of objects — and somewhere in that room is the way through. You tap, drag, combine objects, read codes and use a hint when you are truly stuck. The Play Store description sums up the pitch in one line: "Solve escape puzzles, escape rooms & unlock 150 doors by using your IQ. Can you?"

Why review it now? Because it is surging again. On AppBrain's trending free puzzle list, updated on 26 October 2026, the game recorded about 210,000 installs in 7 days — six times the week before. That is a sharp jump for a game that has been on Google Play since December 2021. On the live Play listing it holds about 4.5 stars from 280,013 ratings, and Play shows a 10,000,000+ install bracket (AppBrain's own tracking estimates about 37 million total installs). A five-year-old puzzle game does not climb like that unless new players are still discovering it — and, judging by the reviews, still enjoying the hunt.

How it plays

There is no running, jumping or timer pressure here. You look at a room, you notice what is out of place, and you work out what the game wants you to do. One level might hide a code in a poster, a calendar or a pattern of objects on a wall. Another might ask you to combine two items you picked up — a key with a lock, a tool with a panel — or to move in time and space, as the developer puts it, using objects in ways that are not immediately obvious. The controls are simple taps and drags, and the Play listing promises "simple to play, complicated to beat", which is a fair description: the first doors open easily, and later ones make you stare at the screen for a while before the answer clicks.

When you are stuck, the game offers help rather than leaving you stranded. There is a hint button in every room, and the listing also mentions a lucky spin to win extra hints. That matters in a game like this, because a single missed detail can stall you completely. Use the hint sparingly, though: part of the satisfaction is the moment you spot the clue yourself, and a hint used too early can spoil a level you would have solved in another minute.

Key features

Hidden objects and conundrums. Every room hides objects and puzzles to solve, from number codes to pattern clues, rather than repeating the same mechanic level after level.

Combine objects and brain games. You pick up items, combine them and use them in unexpected places, alongside logic tasks and mini brain games woven into the rooms.

Different jail cells. The rooms change as you progress — different layouts, decorations and colour schemes — so the game does not feel like the same cell painted over and over.

Hints and a lucky spin. Stuck players can use hints, and a spin feature offers extra hints, so a hard level does not have to mean the end of your run.

Animations and detailed graphics. The rooms are drawn in detail, with lighting, textures and small animations that make each cell feel like a real, lived-in place rather than a flat puzzle board.

How many doors are there, really?

The name promises 100 doors, the short Play description promises 150 doors, and reviewers report even more levels added over time — one July 2026 reviewer mentions 170 levels. Do not treat any single number as exact: Peaksel has kept adding to the game since its 2021 launch, so the total keeps growing. What matters for a new player is that there is a very long game here. You will not run out of rooms in a weekend, and the early levels are a good test of whether the style suits you before you commit to the long haul.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely satisfying escape-room loop — search, spot, solve, escape; a huge number of levels for a free game; detailed, atmospheric rooms that reward careful looking; simple controls with no reflex pressure; hints and a spin feature when you are stuck; a strong 4.5-star rating from a very large player base; and a clear, single goal that makes it easy to pick up for five minutes or an hour.

Cons: ads are the top complaint — one October 2026 reviewer says short levels are followed by ads so often that it breaks the flow, and the game contains ads throughout; difficulty is uneven, with some levels feeling too easy and repetitive and others feeling illogical, according to reviewers; dialogue and cash-bonus pop-ups annoy some players; the download is about 222.8 MB according to AppBrain, which sits awkwardly next to the listing's claim that it "takes up almost no memory"; and the Medium Maturity rating means it is not meant for young children, despite the simple, cartoon-style puzzle gameplay.

Pricing and safety notes

100 Doors - Escape from Prison is free to download on Google Play and contains ads. The Play listing checked for this review did not state an in-app purchase price range, so check the listing for any optional purchases before paying for anything. The current version is 3.16.0, updated 13 July 2026, it needs Android 9.0 or higher, and the download is about 222.8 MB. On data safety, the Play listing says data is encrypted in transit, you can request that data be deleted, and the app may share location, app activity and other data types with third parties — worth knowing, though this is a single-player puzzle game with no account or social features at its core.

Download

Download 100 Doors - Escape from Prison on Google Play

If you like escape-room puzzles and you do not mind ads between levels, 100 Doors - Escape from Prison is an easy recommendation: the core loop is strong, the rooms are detailed, there are far more than 100 doors waiting, and 210,000 new installs in a single week show it is still finding new fans five years on. Go in expecting uneven difficulty and frequent ads, and remember the Medium Maturity rating before handing it to a younger child.
