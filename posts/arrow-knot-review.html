Arrow Knot Review: Tap-Away Puzzles (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Arrow Knot is the calmest arrow puzzle we have reviewed: a clean grid, one clear rule, and hundreds of handcrafted levels that reward planning over speed. It is free, rated Everyone, and genuinely trending — but wrong taps cost hearts, and the timed challenges will not suit everyone.

Arrow Knot: Brain Puzzle Game, from developer PicoJoy Labs, has been on Google Play since March 2026 and is having a strong autumn. AppBrain lists it on its 26 October 2026 trending free Casual chart, with 510,000 installs in total and 32,000 of those in the last seven days — roughly three times the week before. Its live Google Play rating at the time of writing stands at 4.8 stars from about 2,465 ratings, and AppBrain rates its content Everyone. It is free to download, AppBrain lists it as containing ads, and its download size is listed at about 88 MB.

The idea could hardly be simpler, and that is the whole appeal. Each level is a grid full of arrows, every arrow pointing in one direction. Tap an arrow whose path to the edge of the board is clear, and it slides off, opening up new routes for the arrows left behind. Tap in the wrong order and you can block the board completely, so every level becomes a small sequencing puzzle: which arrow goes first, which one second, and which route must stay open until the very end?

How to play

There is no tutorial wall to fight through, because the rule fits in one sentence: an arrow can only leave the board if nothing stands in its way. The second screenshot shows Level 10 in action, with a finger tapping a red arrow that has a clear run to the right edge, while the remaining white arrows wait in a tight maze. The three hearts at the top are the stakes — the Play description says wrong taps cost a heart, so guessing your way through a level is a fast route to a failed attempt.

That heart system is what turns a relaxing-looking game into a real logic test. On easy boards you can work forward happily, clearing the obvious arrows first. On harder boards you have to work backwards, spotting the arrow that must go last and protecting its exit until everything blocking it is gone. It is the same satisfaction as solving a sliding puzzle or a Sudoku: nothing flashy happens, but the moment the final arrow slides away feels like a small mental reset, which is exactly how the developer describes it.

Two ways to play

Arrow Knot offers two modes, and the split is well judged. Endless mode is the stress-free option: solve at your own pace, with no timer, and treat each board as a quiet brain teaser. Timed Challenges are the opposite — race the clock through themed pattern boards, beat the timer to unlock the next design, and climb the challenge ladder. The fox-shaped Level 46 board shown below shows how dense those themed boards get, with arrows packed into a single picture shape rather than a plain grid.

That split matters because puzzle players are not all the same. If you want a game for a short train ride or ten minutes before a meeting, Endless mode fits perfectly: one board, one clear goal, no pressure. If you want a rush, the timed boards give you one without changing the core rule. Nothing about the game demands a long session, and the Play description pitches it precisely for those in-between moments — a short ride, a winding-down evening, a gap in the day.

Themed boards and hints

The themed pattern boards are the game's showpiece. Level 46, shown above, arranges its arrows into the shape of a fox, labelled Eagle Eye, so solving the puzzle also reveals a picture. It is a clever twist on a familiar mechanic: the arrows are no longer just a grid to clear, they are a drawing to untangle, and the finished board is satisfying in a way a plain grid never quite is. The developer says new levels are added regularly, so the supply of fresh knots should not run dry quickly.

When a board does beat you, the game gives you two lifelines rather than leaving you stuck. Long-press any arrow for Guidance mode, which previews its path before you commit to the tap, and spend a hint to reveal the next best move when the sequence gets truly tricky. The fourth screenshot shows Level 32 with the hint button highlighted, a reminder that help is there without being pushed at you. Used sparingly, hints keep a hard level enjoyable; used constantly, they would remove the puzzle entirely, so the sensible approach is to try the board yourself first and only call for help when you are genuinely stuck.

Key features

  • Tap-away arrow puzzles — tap an arrow with a clear path and it slides off the board, unblocking the rest.
  • Hundreds of handcrafted levels — relaxing warm-ups through to mind-bending tangles, with new levels added regularly.
  • Endless mode — solve at your own pace with no timer, for pure logic-puzzle flow.
  • Timed Challenges — race the clock through themed pattern boards and climb the challenge ladder.
  • Guidance mode — long-press any arrow to preview its route before you tap.
  • Hints — reveal the next best move when a knot gets too tricky.
  • Heart system — wrong taps cost a heart, so planning the order matters.
  • Clean, minimalist design — soft colours, smooth animations and no visual clutter.
  • Everyone rating — a family-safe puzzle with no age-gated content.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • One clear rule, instantly learned, but genuinely strategic in later levels
  • Strong rating: 4.8 stars from about 2,465 Play ratings, and 510,000 installs
  • Endless and timed modes suit both calm players and thrill-seekers
  • Themed pattern boards, like the Level 46 fox, make solving feel like revealing art
  • Guidance mode and hints keep hard boards fair instead of frustrating
  • Free download, Everyone rating, and a modest 88 MB install size
Cons

  • Wrong taps cost hearts, so careless tapping is punished quickly
  • Timed challenges will not suit players who came for a calm puzzle

Download

Download Arrow Knot on Google Play

Arrow Knot is an easy recommendation for anyone who likes logic puzzles without noise: one rule, hundreds of boards, a calm look, and a timed mode when you want a jolt. Start in Endless mode, learn to plan backwards from the last arrow, and save your hints for the boards that truly earn them.
