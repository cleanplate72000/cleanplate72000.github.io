Bubble Pop Review: Bubble Shooter Fun (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Bubble Pop: Ball Shooter Game is the classic bubble shooter done cleanly: aim, match three of a colour, and watch the wall collapse. It is free, tiny at about 27 MB, works offline, and carries an Everyone rating — a genuinely easy recommendation for a quick, relaxing puzzle, even if it is one familiar loop done well rather than a reinvention.

Bubble Pop: Ball Shooter Game, from developer HDuo Fun Games, has been on Google Play since November 2021 and has now passed 32 million installs — with about 1.1 million of those in the last 30 days, roughly 36,000 installs a day, and around 390,000 installs in the last 7 days alone, which is why it sits on AppBrain's trending free puzzle list for October 2026. On Google Play it holds an unusually high 4.96 stars from about 92,000 ratings, and it ranks #41 in Top Free Puzzle in India and #69 in Saudi Arabia. For a five-year-old bubble shooter to still be adding installs at this pace, it must be doing the basics very well — and, as this review explains, it is.

The pitch from the developer is deliberately simple: “Bubble ball blast game with real stress relief! Simple and easy to play, the difficulty of bubble level gradually increases.” There is no story to follow and no complicated system to learn. You get a shooter at the bottom of the screen, a wall of coloured bubbles above it, and one job — clear the wall. That simplicity is the whole appeal, and it is also why the game works so well in short sessions: one level takes a couple of minutes, and you can stop anywhere.

How it plays

The loop will be familiar to anyone who has ever played a bubble shooter, and that is a strength here rather than a weakness. You tap to switch the bubble loaded in your shooter, swipe to aim, and release to fire. Match three or more bubbles of the same colour and they burst; chain your shots well and you trigger bigger cascades and colourful effects. Bubbles that lose their support drop away too, which is where the satisfying big clears come from — a well-placed shot can bring down a whole dangling section at once.

As the levels climb, the boards get trickier: tighter clusters, awkward angles, and layouts where a careless shot wastes your best colour. The developer says the difficulty “gradually increases”, and the screenshots back that up — early boards are open and forgiving, while later ones pack the bubbles into rings and pockets that demand real aiming. The game also hands you boosters for the hard spots, including fire balls and friendly genie helpers, plus extra bubble-shooter rewards for beating levels and unlocking new cute episodes with animal friends.

Why it is trending

Bubble Pop is trending for a refreshingly ordinary reason: it is a polished, free, offline-friendly version of a game everybody already knows how to play. There is no new mechanic to explain and no account to set up before the first shot — you open it, aim, and pop. At about 27 MB it is also one of the smallest games on the trending lists, so it is an easy impulse download on a budget phone or a nearly-full one, and it runs on Android 6.0 and up, so older devices are not left out.

The offline angle matters more than it sounds. The developer explicitly promises “No WiFi! Popping on the go!”, and for a game built for commutes, waiting rooms and patchy connections, that is a real selling point — no signal, no problem. Add the Everyone content rating, the developer's own emphasis on a safe environment for children, and the colour-matching gameplay that the listing says helps children practise basic colour matching, and you have a game parents can hand over without a second thought.

Key features

  • Classic bubble-shooter gameplay — aim, match three or more of a colour, and clear the wall.
  • Tons of levels — a large level set with difficulty that rises gradually as you progress.
  • Boosters for tough boards — fire balls, genies and other bubble boosters to break stubborn layouts.
  • Cute episodes and animal friends — beating levels unlocks new episodes and sweet pet companions.
  • Offline play — no WiFi needed, so it works on commutes and anywhere without a signal.
  • Tap-and-swipe controls — switch bubbles, aim precisely, and shoot with one finger.
  • Small download — about 27 MB, and it runs on Android 6.0 and up.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Exceptional 4.96 stars from about 92,000 ratings, with 32 million installs and 1.1 million in the last 30 days
  • Instantly understandable — no tutorial wall, no account, just aim and pop
  • Tiny 27 MB download that works offline on Android 6.0 and up
  • Everyone content rating, with colour-matching gameplay suitable for younger players
  • Real difficulty curve plus boosters, so later boards stay interesting
Cons
  • One classic loop done well, not a new idea — if you want variety, this is not it
  • A near-perfect 4.96 average is unusually high; treat it as enthusiasm, not a guarantee you will love every level
  • One recent reviewer complained the download took longer than expected, so allow a little patience on slow connections
  • Last updated 14 July 2026 — fine for a finished puzzle game, but do not expect frequent new features
  • Free-to-download does not mean everything inside is free: check any paid booster or offer before accepting it

Pricing

Bubble Pop: Ball Shooter Game is free to download on Google Play. The listing data checked for this review shows it as free with no in-app purchase price range displayed, so this review makes no claim about specific prices inside the app — if you meet a paid booster or offer while playing, check what it costs before accepting it. The current version is 1.361, updated 14 July 2026, and at about 27 MB it is one of the lightest trending games you can try this week.
