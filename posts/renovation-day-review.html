Renovation Day Review: House Makeover (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Renovation Day: House Makeover is a genuinely relaxing house-flipper game: you clean up a tornado-wrecked home, collect materials as you go, and furnish each room your own way, with no timers pushing you along. The decorating loop is satisfying and the game looks charming, but the ad load is the real spoiler — several recent reviewers complain about frequent forced ads — so go in expecting interruptions unless you pay to remove them.

Renovation Day: House Makeover, from developer SayGames Ltd, is one of the fastest-climbing casual games on Google Play this week. AppBrain's trending-games snapshot for 26 October 2026 puts it at roughly 270,000 installs in the last seven days, with about 610,000 installs in the last 30 days and 6.7 million installs in total since its launch in May 2024. It holds a 4.6-star rating from more than 65,000 ratings, carries an Everyone content rating, and ranks in the top free Role Playing charts in the United Kingdom (11th), Canada (12th), France (19th), Germany (21st) and the United States (34th) in the ranking snapshot checked. That combination — a big, recent surge plus a settled, high rating — is exactly why it earns a review here this week.

The premise is simple and immediately appealing. Your brand-new home is a royal mess after a tornado tore through it, and you are the person with the can-do attitude and the hammer who is going to put it right. There is no renovation budget to juggle, no client breathing down your neck, and no clock counting down. You walk your character through the mess, clear it room by room, and turn a wreck into a home you actually chose the furniture for. For anyone who enjoys the satisfying part of home makeovers — the before-and-after, the tidying, the choosing — without any of the real-world dust, cost or stress, that pitch lands straight away.

How Renovation Day actually plays

The core loop has three steps that repeat across every room, and the game is upfront about all of them. First, you clean up: guide your character through the mess, pick up debris and clear each space so you can see what you are working with. Second, you collect to create: the materials you gather are not just clutter to throw away, they are resources you transform into useful items for decorating and fixing up the property. The developer describes idle-style elements here, so materials keep working for you and progress continues even when you step away. Third, you decorate: choose from plants, chairs, paintings and other pieces of decor, earn points for your choices, and advance through levels as your house takes shape.

That loop is deliberately low-pressure. The developer states there are no timers or timed tasks, so you can potter around a room at your own pace, leave a half-finished space and come back to it, and never feel punished for playing slowly. The graphics are bright and whimsical rather than realistic, which fits the tone: this is a game about the fun of redecorating, not a simulation of plastering a wall. Rooms, buildings across your property and eventually whole new spaces open up as you progress, so there is always another messy corner waiting for your attention.

What makes it work

  • Satisfying clean-up loop — clearing debris, collecting materials and watching a wrecked room become liveable is the game's main pleasure, and it repeats well across rooms.
  • Real decorating choices — you pick the decor yourself, from furniture to plants and wall art, rather than just tapping a single “fix” button and watching a scripted makeover.
  • Collect-to-create crafting — gathered materials become decor and repair items, so tidying up feeds directly into the creative part instead of feeling like busywork.
  • No timers, no rush — the developer promises no timed tasks, so the game stays relaxing even in longer sessions.
  • Idle-style progress — materials and crafting keep ticking along, which suits short bursts of play as well as longer sessions.
  • Everyone rating, family-friendly tone — bright, cartoonish visuals and a wholesome premise make it easy to recommend to younger players and casual gamers alike.
  • Proven, popular and still growing — 4.6 stars from over 65,000 ratings and 6.7 million installs, with a strong surge in the last month, is a track record most casual games never reach.

What to watch out for

The biggest caveat is the advertising, and it is worth being blunt about it. The listing says the game contains ads, and recent player reviews are much harsher than that dry label suggests: one reviewer in September 2026 described a forced ad roughly every 30 seconds, while another in October 2026 complained that an ad-free bundle still left in-game purchase prompts in place and that a refund request was refused. Your experience may vary — ad frequency often depends on region and session length — but do not download this expecting an ad-free game unless you are prepared to pay for the ad-removal option, and check exactly what that option removes before buying it.

The second caveat is repetition. Several reviewers describe the gameplay as a loop of similar tasks, and one long-term player reported repeating tasks around level 37 with a “coming soon” clock, suggesting the content can thin out at higher levels until an update arrives. The game is also a chunky download at roughly 291 MB, and it requires Android 8.0 or higher, so make sure you have space before you install it. Finally, as with any free game with purchases, the in-app purchase prices were not fully listed on the Play page checked for this review, so check the prices inside the game itself before spending anything.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Relaxing, stress-free house makeover loop with no timers
  • You choose the decor, not just watch a scripted renovation
  • Collect-to-create crafting makes tidying feel rewarding
  • 4.6 stars from over 65,000 ratings; 6.7 million installs
  • Everyone content rating and cheerful, family-friendly visuals
  • Strong recent growth: about 270,000 installs in the last week

Cons

  • Frequent forced ads reported by recent reviewers
  • Ad-free bundle wording has drawn complaints from players
  • Gameplay can feel repetitive at higher levels
  • Large download at roughly 291 MB
  • In-app purchase prices not fully listed on the Play page checked

Pricing

Renovation Day: House Makeover is free to download on Google Play, and its listing states that it contains ads. The current version checked is 2.28.1, updated on 24 September 2026, and it needs Android 8.0 or higher. In-app purchases are available inside the game, including an ad-removal option mentioned by players, but exact prices were not shown on the Play page checked, so confirm them in-game before buying. Our advice is simple: play the free version first, see how the ad load feels in your own sessions, and only then decide whether removing ads is worth paying for.

Should you download it?

Yes, if you like low-stress, creative games and you can tolerate ads — or you are happy to pay to remove them. Renovation Day nails the satisfying heart of a house makeover: the mess, the materials, the choices and the before-and-after, all without timers or pressure. It is not a deep simulation and it will not stay fresh forever at the highest levels, but for a free, family-friendly game with a 4.6-star track record and a surge of new players this week, it is an easy recommendation with eyes open about the ads.
