Hexa Pop Review: Rainbow Hex Sorting (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Hexa Pop: Color Sort Puzzle is a bright, fast colour-sort puzzle with a satisfying core loop: stack ten chips of one colour and the whole tower bursts. It is free, needs no in-app purchases, works offline, and is already rated 4.7 stars — but ads are frequent, the download is a chunky 127 MB, and the developer's data-safety page says your data cannot be deleted, so it is best for puzzle fans who do not mind ad breaks.

Hexa Pop: Color Sort Puzzle, from developer Loyalty Gaming — the same studio behind our Domino Blast review — is one of the fastest-rising new casual games on Google Play in October 2026. It reached number 6 in the Top New Free Casual chart in Canada and number 9 in the United States, and also ranks in the Top New Free Games charts in both countries. AppBrain puts it at about 77,000 installs, all in the last 30 days — roughly 3,200 a day — after its release on 28 September 2026. On the live Google Play listing it is rated 4.7 stars from about 5,270 ratings, it is rated Everyone, and it is free to download. The listing says it contains ads, including optional reward ads, and states plainly that there are no in-app purchases. The latest version is 1.2, updated 14 October 2026, it needs Android 7.0 and up, and AppBrain lists the app at about 127 MB.

The idea takes about a minute to learn, which is exactly why it works as a quick-session game. You pick one of three chip stacks from a tray and drop it onto any empty hex on a honeycomb board. Chips of the same colour on top of neighbouring stacks slide across to join the new stack, chaining from cell to cell. Build a single-colour tower of ten or more and it bursts in a shower of confetti and coins, filling the level's goal bar. Keep enough space free, because if the board fills up before you hit the goal, the level is over. That is the whole game — and it is genuinely hard to put down, because every drop can set off a chain you did not plan.

What lifts Hexa Pop above a plain sort puzzle is the chain system. A smart drop can trigger streaks the game labels COMBO, AMAZING and INCREDIBLE, and the screenshots show why players keep screenshots of their best boards: a Level 26 board mid-burst, flashing INCREDIBLE x5 with a +550 score pop. Two special pieces add real decision-making. The Rainbow Bomb clears every colour to light the rainbow, then fires a bomb that pulls a whole colour into one giant blast. The rainbow WILD chip takes the colour of its neighbours, which makes it the perfect rescue piece when a board is nearly full. Between levels, Undo, Hammer and Refresh boosters get you out of a tight spot, and Lucky Payday levels pay extra coins — in-game coins, not real money, a distinction worth making plainly below.

How to play

Each level gives you a goal — a number of chips to clear — rather than asking you to survive forever. Drop a stack from the tray onto an empty hex, watch same-colour chips slide across to join it, and stack ten of one colour to burst the tower. Fill the goal bar before the board fills up and the level clears with a star rating. Later levels add new twists, breather levels for a calmer session, and tougher boss levels, with a fresh board and sky every 25 levels — from amethyst and ocean to sunset and jade. The listing promises 1,000+ levels, and most levels take under a minute, so it fits the one-more-level habit very well. Puzzles play offline with no Wi-Fi needed, although ads and ad rewards need a connection.

Key features

  • Stack-ten bursting towers: build a single-colour tower of 10 or more chips and it bursts, filling the goal bar.
  • Chaining colour slides: same-colour chips hop across from neighbours, cell to cell, so one drop can cascade.
  • COMBO, AMAZING and INCREDIBLE streaks: chained bursts earn named streaks and bigger scores.
  • Rainbow Bomb: clear every colour to light the rainbow, then blast a whole colour at once.
  • Wild chips: the rainbow WILD chip takes its neighbours' colour and rescues a crowded board.
  • 1,000+ levels: new twists keep arriving, with breather levels and tough boss levels along the way.
  • A new world every 25 levels: fresh boards and skies, from amethyst and ocean to sunset and jade.
  • Undo, Hammer and Refresh boosters: three rescue moves for a board that is nearly full.
  • Lucky Payday levels: special levels that pay extra in-game coins.
  • Offline play: no Wi-Fi needed for the puzzles themselves, and no in-app purchases at all.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely satisfying stack-and-burst loop that is easy to learn in seconds; short levels that suit quick sessions; 1,000+ levels with new worlds every 25 levels; free with no in-app purchases to chase; offline play; and a strong 4.7-star rating from more than 5,000 players.

Cons: ads are the biggest complaint in early player reviews, with one two-star reviewer reporting two or three pop-up ads in every round plus ads before and after levels; it is a chunky 127 MB download for a puzzle game; and the developer's data-safety page says device IDs are collected and shared for advertising and that data cannot be deleted, which privacy-minded players should know before installing. One small quirk: the body of the Play description calls the game “Hexa Stack” even though the store title is Hexa Pop — the game itself is the same.

Pricing and a note on in-game coins

Hexa Pop is free to play, the Play listing flags ads including optional reward ads, and there are no in-app purchases — a genuinely player-friendly combination for a free puzzle game. A note worth making plainly: the listing promises in-game coins and rewards — not real money. Some player comments online talk about “earning” and even about cashing out, but nothing on the official Play listing offers cash payouts, and the description's “Lucky Payday” levels pay extra in-game coins only. If you play it, play it for the puzzle, not for earnings.

Is it worth downloading?

If you like colour-sort and stacking puzzles and you want one with a bit more drama than sorting tubes, Hexa Pop is an easy recommendation — the ten-chip bursts are satisfying, the levels are short, and the early player rating is strong. If you hate ads, though, go in with your eyes open: this is a free, ad-supported game, and several early reviewers say the ad frequency is its weakest point. For a calmer alternative in the same puzzle family from the same studio, try our Domino Blast review.
