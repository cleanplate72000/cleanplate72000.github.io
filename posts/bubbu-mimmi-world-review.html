Bubbu & Mimmi World Review: Pet Care (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Bubbu & Mimmi World is a bright, gentle virtual-pet game for children: you feed, wash, dress and nurse cartoon cats (plus dogs, rabbits and bears), explore a little cartoon world, and dip into mini-games. It is free, rated Everyone, and COPPA-certified — but the 4.0-star average, a large download and an auto-renewing subscription mean parents should set it up first.

Bubbu & Mimmi World, from developer Bubadu, stars Bubbu the Cat — already a familiar virtual pet for many children — and his playful kitten friend Mimmi. The game has been on Google Play since December 2024, and AppBrain tracks 25 million installs in total, with about 990,000 of those in the last 30 days. Its live Google Play rating is about 4.0 stars from more than 24,000 ratings, the current version is 1.64 (updated 14 October 2026), the download is about 228 MB, it needs Android 6.0 or later, and Google Play rates its content Everyone. It is free to download, with advertising and in-app purchases, including a subscription.

The appeal is easy to understand. Where many trending games this month are sorting puzzles or merge boards, this is a toy box: a pet to look after, rooms to visit, outfits to try on, and small jobs to do. Nothing here is timed, competitive or violent, and there is no chat with strangers. For a young child, that calm, colourful structure is the whole point — and for a parent, it is a much easier yes than most of the chart.

Pet care first: wash, feed and comfort

The heart of the game is looking after your pets. The first screenshot shows that loop in action: one cat soaping up an orange kitten in a pink bath, a third cat waiting with a towel, and the bathroom stocked with shampoo, sponges and a first-aid cabinet. The developer's own description frames this deliberately as learning responsibility and empathy — pets rely on you, and you meet their daily needs to keep them happy and healthy. In practice that means washing, feeding, putting pets to bed and cheering them up, all with big, tappable buttons and no fail state to worry about.

That simplicity is a strength for the target audience, and a limit for older players. There is no complex strategy here, no levelling system to master and no high score to chase. If you are buying — or rather, downloading — this for a child aged roughly 3 to 8, that is exactly right. If you are a teenager or adult looking for a game with depth, you will exhaust the novelty quickly, and the rating reflects that split: a solid 4.0 stars from a large, mostly family audience, rather than the 4.7-plus scores of the puzzle hits.

Dress-up, makeovers and celebrations

Once the pets are clean, the game opens up its dress-up side. The developer promises hundreds of outfits, hairstyles, make-up options and accessories, plus themed set pieces: a K-pop superstar makeover with stage wear and dance moves, and a wedding-day planner mode where you style the bride and groom and design the rings. The second screenshot shows the beauty salon in action, with a white cat getting a full purple make-up look while palettes and brushes line the shelves.

Children who like dolls, dress-up and pretend play will get the most out of this half of the game. You can also hatch mystery eggs to discover new pets and combine them into more creatures, expanding the family you look after. It is collection without pressure: new pets are a surprise to unwrap, not a timer to beat.

A little world to wander, not just a room

Beyond the house, Bubbu & Mimmi World is a small explorable world: magical castles, enchanted forests, a city centre and the seaside, plus everyday role-play locations. Visit the supermarket, get pampered at the spa, enjoy a makeover at the beauty salon and hair salon (shown in the third screenshot, with the cat's new purple hairstyle and a shelf of dryers, scissors and sprays), throw a birthday party, or lend a hand at the hospital. The locations are the game's way of teaching everyday routines through play — shopping, grooming, celebrating and caring — without ever feeling like a lesson.

The newest update leans into that learning angle. Version 1.64, released 14 October 2026, opens a new school, with five mini-games for numbers, clocks, tangram puzzles, animals and music, plus new outfits, light shows and a school pet owl. If your child has already played an earlier version, the school is the fresh reason to come back.

Mini-games and daily surprises

Between pet-care sessions, the game offers a bundle of mini-games: a block puzzle, a Candyland adventure, a beach arcade catching game, and a relaxing round of Solitaire, plus daily gifts and surprises. These are short, self-contained and low-stakes — the kind of thing a child can play for five minutes while waiting. The fourth screenshot shows the hospital role-play at its gentlest: two poorly cats tucked into beds, with medicine, ice packs and a thermometer to apply, and no scary imagery anywhere.

That gentleness is consistent across the game. The art is bright and rounded, the pets are expressive rather than realistic, and even the hospital is a place of comfort, not illness. Parents worried about content will find very little to object to here — the concerns with this app are commercial, not content-based, and they are covered honestly below.

Safety, ads and the subscription — read this first

Bubadu states that the game is designed as a safe, family-friendly space, and it is certified compliant with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) by PRIVO, an FTC-approved safe harbour. That is a genuine reassurance on privacy, and it is rarer than it should be among children's games. There is no chat, no user-generated content and no social feed to moderate.

The catches are the ones the developer itself discloses on the Play listing, and they deserve a parent's attention before a child gets the phone. First, the game contains advertising, including third-party ads that can redirect to other sites or apps. Second, some items and features require real-money purchases, and the game offers a subscription that renews automatically unless cancelled at least 24 hours before the end of the current period. The sensible setup is to turn on purchase approval (or a password for every purchase) in Google Play, check the subscription screen together, and cancel anything you did not mean to start. Do that once, and the game itself is as safe as its COPPA badge suggests.

The other practical caveat is size: at about 228 MB, this is a chunky download for a children's game, so use Wi-Fi and check storage on older phones or tablets. And keep expectations honest — the divided 4.0-star rating suggests some families hit bugs, crashes or paywalled content, so if the game misbehaves on your device, deleting it costs nothing.

Key features

  • Virtual pets to care for — cats, dogs, rabbits and bears to wash, feed, comfort and put to bed.
  • Dress-up and makeovers — hundreds of outfits, hairstyles and accessories, plus K-pop and wedding themes.
  • An explorable cartoon world — castles, forests, city, seaside, supermarket, spa, salons and hospital.
  • Mini-games and daily gifts — block puzzles, Candyland, beach arcade, Solitaire and daily surprises.
  • New school update — numbers, clocks, tangrams, animals and music mini-games, plus a pet owl.
  • Everyone rating, COPPA-certified — no chat, no user content, privacy certified by PRIVO.
  • Egg hatching and pet collection — hatch mystery eggs and grow your pet family.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Gentle, genuinely child-friendly content: no violence, no chat, no competitive pressure
  • 25 million installs and nearly a million new players in the last 30 days — a proven hit
  • COPPA-certified privacy, a real reassurance for a children's game
  • Lots to do: pet care, dress-up, role-play locations, mini-games and a new school
  • Free to try, rated Everyone, works on Android 6.0 and later
Cons
  • Contains third-party ads that can link out to other sites or apps
  • Auto-renewing subscription — set purchase approval before handing it to a child
  • Large 228 MB download for a children's game — use Wi-Fi
  • Divided 4.0-star rating; some content is paywalled and older devices may struggle
  • Little depth for older children or adults — this is a young child's toy box

Pricing

Bubbu & Mimmi World is free to download on Google Play. The listing states that it contains ads and offers in-app purchases, including a subscription that renews automatically unless cancelled at least 24 hours before the period ends. No specific in-app price range was shown on the Play listing checked for this review, so check the listing for current offers before buying anything — and set up purchase approval first if a child will play.

Download

Download Bubbu & Mimmi World on Google Play

For a young child who loves animals and pretend play, Bubbu & Mimmi World is an easy recommendation: free, gentle, packed with things to do, and backed by 25 million installs and a COPPA badge. Just do the grown-up setup first — purchase approval on, subscription checked — and it is one of the safer trending games on the chart.
