Pixel Habit Tracker Review: Private (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Pixel Habit Tracker is the rare habit app that asks for nothing: no account, no ads, no tracking, and it works fully offline. It is free, rated Everyone, and its 4.9-star rating is the highest we have seen on a tracker this small — but with just over 1,000 downloads so far, it is still an early favourite rather than a proven classic.

Pixel Habit Tracker, from developer Pixel.al, is a habit tracker built around one promise: everything runs entirely on your device. Most habit apps want you signed up before they show you anything. This one opens straight into your habits, works with the network off, and, in the developer's own words, has nowhere to send your data — no trackers, no analytics, no third-party software kits. It was last updated on 6 October 2026, it is free to download, it is rated Everyone, and its live Google Play rating at the time of writing stands at 4.9 stars from close to 100 ratings, with 1,000+ downloads on Play. That small install base is exactly why it is trending now: it was picked up in October 2026 roundups of the month's best new Android apps, and early users are rating it unusually highly.

The first screenshot shows the heart of the app: a clean Today view where your habits are grouped by category, such as Work and Health, each with its own check-in button and progress bar. A habit like "Push code" can carry its own steps — "Arrive at the lake", "Walk to the middle", "Finish line" — so a big routine breaks into small tickable pieces instead of one intimidating checkbox. That step-by-step design is the app's best idea: it turns "go for a walk" into three small wins, and three small wins are much easier to repeat tomorrow.

Track what matters, your way

Habits here are flexible in the ways that actually matter. You can track simple yes-or-no habits, or measurable ones with a target you set — a number of steps, pages, or minutes. Targets can work as "at least" goals for habits you want to build, or "at most" limits for habits you want to cut back, which means the same app can handle both "read every day" and "fewer sugary drinks". You pick the days of the week each habit runs on, group habits into categories, and set a reminder per habit with follow-ups, a snooze option, and quiet hours that hold notifications overnight.

Reminders are handled sensibly rather than naggingly. Each habit gets its own reminder, you can snooze one you miss, and quiet hours stop the app buzzing you at night. The fourth screenshot shows the habit options screen, where steps, the "done when" rule, a daily reminder toggle, and a notes field all sit in one place. Nothing here demands a tutorial: the app's own pitch is that it is simple enough to start in 30 seconds, and from the layout of these screens, that claim holds up.

Seven widgets, all free

Where many habit apps lock their home screen widgets behind a subscription, Pixel Habit Tracker gives you all seven for free: a habit list you can tick off without opening the app, a single habit widget, a contribution graph, a single-habit heatmap, a streak counter, a week progress widget, and a month calendar. You can set background opacity and corner roundness per widget, so two widgets of the same kind can look different. There is also a Wear OS app, so today's habits sit on your watch and can be ticked off from there — a habit with steps arrives on the wrist as a checklist.

That widget-first approach fits the app's philosophy. A habit tracker only works if checking in takes seconds, and a widget on your home screen is the fastest possible check-in. Combined with the offline design, it means the app is usable on a locked-down phone, on a flight, or anywhere with no signal — the data never leaves the device, so there is nothing to sync and nothing to wait for.

Weekly reports and smart insights

The second screenshot shows the Activity Dashboard, the app's reporting hub. Weekly reports come with a productivity matrix, a radar chart, and insight cards that flag a streak at risk, a personal record, a comeback after a lapse, or your weakest weekday. Per habit you can see your best streak, success rate, weekday breakdown, and totals for measurable habits. It is a genuinely useful level of analysis for a free app, and it answers the question every habit tracker should answer: not just "did I do it today", but "where am I slipping, and what is working?"

Streaks that keep you honest

The third screenshot shows the streak view, and it is motivating without being pushy. A "Push code" habit shows a 34-day streak with a flame icon, a best streak of 34, a 92-day total, and an 86% success rate, with today at 100% and marked as achieved. The app also keeps a full activity heatmap across weeks and months, so your year fills in pixel by pixel — the visual metaphor behind the app's name. Miss a day and you see the gap, which is exactly the nudge that stops one missed day becoming two.

There is also a small honesty feature worth noting: the free version lets you fill in or correct the last 3 days, so a forgotten check-in is not a ruined streak. The Pro upgrade extends that history to 365 days, so you can go back and fix last month — a fair distinction between free and paid, and one that does not punish free users for an honest mistake.

Key features

  • Fully offline, private by default — no account, no cloud sync, no data collection, no trackers or analytics
  • Yes/no and measurable habits — "at least" and "at most" targets for building or breaking habits
  • Habit steps — break a big routine into small tickable steps
  • Smart reminders — one per habit, with follow-ups, snooze, and overnight quiet hours
  • Seven free widgets — habit list, single habit, contribution graph, heatmap, streak counter, week progress, month calendar
  • Wear OS app — check off today's habits from your watch
  • Streaks and heatmaps — activity heatmap across weeks and months, best streak and success rate per habit
  • Weekly reports — productivity matrix, radar chart, and insight cards for streaks at risk and personal records
  • Material 3 Expressive design — dynamic colour that follows your wallpaper, light, dark and system themes, 16 languages
  • Everyone rating — a family-safe productivity app with no age-gated content

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Truly private: no ads, no account, no tracking, fully offline
  • Excellent rating: 4.9 stars from close to 100 Play ratings
  • All seven widgets free, plus a Wear OS app — no subscription wall for the basics
  • Steps, flexible targets and quiet hours make it practical, not just pretty
  • Weekly reports with real insights, not just a calendar of ticks
  • Free to download, Everyone rating, and a genuinely free tier
Cons
  • Small install base (1,000+ downloads), so long-term support is still unproven
  • No cloud sync by design — your data lives on one device, backed up only to a local file
  • Free history editing covers only the last 3 days; 365 days needs Pro
  • Pro is a one-time purchase, but its price was not confirmed in the listings checked
  • No iPhone version mentioned in the Play listing, so this is an Android-only pick for now

Pricing

Pixel Habit Tracker is free to download on Google Play, with no ads and no subscription. The free tier already includes unlimited habits, 5 categories, all seven widgets, the Wear OS app, weekly reports, full stats, dynamic colour and theming, and 3 days of history editing. Pro is a one-time purchase, not a subscription, and adds flexible weekly goals, App Lock with fingerprint, face or PIN, unlimited categories, 365 days of history, monthly activity reports, extra insight types, True Black dark mode, extra themes and app icons, and more Instagram Story designs. The listings checked for this review did not confirm the Pro price, so check the in-app price before buying — and note that paying once, rather than monthly, is the whole point of the offer.

Download

Download Pixel Habit Tracker on Google Play

Pixel Habit Tracker is an easy recommendation for anyone who wants to build a routine without handing their data to another account: open it, add one habit, tick it off from a widget, and let the streak do the persuading. Start with a single daily habit, use steps to make it small, and check the weekly report on Sunday to see what is actually working.
