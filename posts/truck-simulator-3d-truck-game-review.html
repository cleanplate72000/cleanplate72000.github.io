Truck Simulator 3D Review: Cargo Hauls (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Truck Simulator: 3D Truck Game is a straightforward, free cargo-hauling sim that is growing fast — roughly 350,000 installs in the last 30 days — but its small, divided rating base means you should try it free first and judge the controls for yourself.

Truck Simulator: 3D Truck Game, from developer Innovative Gaming Studios (package com.igs.truck.simulator.truck.games), puts you in the cab of a semi truck and asks you to do the job properly: pick up cargo at one station, drive it across the city or off road, and deliver it at another. There is no story to follow and no fleet empire to build — this is a driving game first, and it knows it.

The numbers explain why it is climbing the charts. The game passed 1,000,000 installs on Google Play in October 2026, with AppBrain tracking about 1.1 million total installs and roughly 350,000 in the last 30 days — around 12,000 a day. It ranks #24 in the Top Free Role Playing chart in Denmark and Norway, #39 in India and #32 in Saudi Arabia. The live Play listing shows 4 stars from about 950 ratings, while AppBrain's 24 October snapshot recorded 3.4 stars from 837 ratings — a thin, mixed base, so treat the star rating as a rough signal rather than a verdict. The game is rated Everyone, is free with ads, is about 85 MB, needs Android 6.0 or higher, and was last updated on 5 October 2026, adding city, offroad and parking modes, a night environment and weather selection.

Five ways to drive

The game splits into five modes, and this variety is its best feature. City Cargo Delivery has you hauling freight through urban streets on time. Truck Parking tests precision as you slot a heavy semi past obstacles. Offroad Truck Cargo Delivery takes the same job onto bumpy, hilly terrain. Steering Only Parking restricts you to the on-screen wheel for a harder precision challenge. And Free Roam Mode lets you explore city and highway routes with no delivery deadline at all — the mode to pick when you just want to drive.

Behind the wheel in the city: the in-cab view, working mirrors and a full set of on-screen controls.

Across all modes you get a day-night cycle and changing weather — bright days, rainy nights and everything between — plus a choice of steering wheel, tilt or arrow controls, working mirror views, and a music controller for the in-game soundtracks. Players praise the mirror views and the feeling of driving from inside the cab, and several reviewers note the game plays offline, which makes it a decent pick for commutes without reliable signal.

Night work: loading up at the depot before a long haul in the dark.

Key features

  • Five modes: city cargo delivery, truck parking, offroad cargo delivery, steering-only parking and free roam.
  • In-cab driving with working mirrors, plus steering wheel, tilt or arrow controls.
  • Day-night cycle and selectable weather, including rain and night driving.
  • A wide variety of cargo types and numerous levels across all modes.
  • Offline-friendly play, according to player reports, at around 85 MB.

A night highway run: the day-night cycle and weather are the game's best atmosphere builders.

Pros and cons

Pros: genuinely varied modes for a free game; in-cab view with mirrors feels more like a simulator than an arcade racer; day-night and weather systems add atmosphere; small download that runs on Android 6.0 and up; fast-growing player base.

Cons: the rating base is small and divided, so quality is genuinely uncertain; the screen is crowded with control icons, which can overwhelm first-time players; ads are part of the free experience; the graphics are functional rather than beautiful, and there is no multiplayer or fleet management for players who want a deeper trucking career.

Car-carrier duty: one of the many cargo types, loaded at the depot before departure.

Pricing

Truck Simulator: 3D Truck Game is free to download and play on Google Play, with ads. The store listing we checked shows no in-app purchase prices, so expect the standard free-with-ads model rather than a paid upgrade path — and never pay anything the game itself does not clearly label inside the official store listing.

Who should download it?

If you like driving simulators but do not want a huge download or a complicated career mode, this is an easy free try — start with Free Roam to learn the controls, then move to city deliveries. If you want a deeper trucking experience or polished graphics, you may bounce off it quickly. Fans of vehicle games may also like our Bus Parade review on The App Wire.

In short: a free, fast-growing truck sim with real variety and a likeable in-cab feel, held back by a thin rating record and a busy screen. At 3.5 out of 5, it is worth the free download — with expectations set to “solid time-passer”, not “PC simulator on your phone”.
