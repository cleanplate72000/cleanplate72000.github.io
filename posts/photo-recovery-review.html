Photo Recovery Review: Deleted Files (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Photo Recovery - AllRecover is a free, Everyone-rated tool for hunting down deleted photos, videos, audio and documents on your own phone. It is genuinely trending — about 230,000 installs in the last 7 days — but recovery is never guaranteed, the ads are a real annoyance, and you should preview everything before you delete or restore anything.

Photo Recovery - AllRecover, from developer imtiaz sarwar, is one of those apps people only search for in a small panic: a photo deleted by mistake, a video gone after a clean-up, a document that vanished. It has been on Google Play since October 2025, and it is having a sharp moment right now. AppBrain's trending free list for 25 October 2026 puts it among the fastest-growing free apps, with roughly 230,000 installs in the last 7 days — about 10 times the week before — and about 260,000 installs in the last 30 days, for a tracked total of around 720,000 installs. On Google Play itself it sits in the 500,000+ installs bracket, with a 3.5-star average from about 780 ratings. That split — fast growth, middling rating — is exactly why this review leads with the limits, not just the promise.

The idea is simple. Deleted files are not always gone at once: traces can remain in storage or in cached files left by other apps until they are overwritten. Photo Recovery scans your phone's internal storage for those traces, shows you what it found, and lets you pick what to bring back. The developer is unusually upfront about this in the Play listing itself: success depends on your device, the health of its storage and its file system, complete recovery cannot be guaranteed, and files deleted before a factory reset cannot be recovered after the reset. If an app will not say that plainly, be suspicious. This one does — and that honesty is part of its score here.

How it works

Open the app and you get four clear starting points: Recover Photos, Recover Video, Recover Audios and Recover Files. Pick one, let it scan, and then browse what it found before you restore anything. That preview step matters more than any other feature here, because a recovery scan can surface a lot of old, low-quality or duplicate material alongside the file you actually want. The listing also promises a Recovered section, so restored items are kept in one place rather than scattered back across your storage.

The scan screen itself is deliberately simple: a big restoring animation while the app works through storage. There is no account to create and no cloud upload in the pitch — the developer states that all scanning, recovery and cleaning take place entirely on your device, and that no personal data is uploaded, stored externally or shared with any third party. Google Play's data-safety section agrees on the headline point, listing no data shared with third parties, with data encrypted in transit, while also noting that the app may collect app info, performance data and device IDs, and that data cannot be deleted through the app. Read that last line before you install, not after.

More than just recovery

Recovery is the headline, but the app bundles three storage tools alongside it. The junk files cleaner scans for unnecessary junk and shows how much space it could free — the home screen in the screenshots shows 8.81 MB in one example, which is a useful reminder that this is a tidy-up tool, not a miracle space-saver. There is also a similar pictures cleaner for near-duplicate images and a screenshot remover for clearing out old screenshots. Used carefully, with a preview first, these are the most practical everyday features in the app: most people will free a little space far more often than they will rescue a deleted file.

Document recovery gets its own screen, and it is the most business-like part of the app. Found files are grouped by date, with familiar formats — Word documents, text files, PDFs and spreadsheets — each with a tick box, so you can restore one report without restoring everything the scan found. Filters and sorting by date help when a scan returns a long list, and a Select all option is there if you do want the lot. The sensible way to use it is the opposite, though: tick only the files you recognise, and leave the rest.

Why it is trending

Photo Recovery is trending for a very human reason: losing photos hurts, and a free tool that claims to undo it spreads fast by word of mouth and search. The timing helps too — the Play listing was updated on 23 October 2026, just as the install surge was building, and the app's pitch is easy to repeat: free, no account, everything happens on your phone. At about 38 MB it is also a small download, so trying it costs little time or storage.

The rating tells the other half of the story. A 3.5-star average from under 800 ratings is divided, not enthusiastic: AppBrain's histogram shows roughly 380 five-star ratings against 200 one-star ratings. The most helpful critical review on Play, marked helpful by 298 people, says the app found far fewer photos than a rival recovery app on the same day and complains that ads appear constantly, including when switching back to the app and after the device locks. Other reviewers report confusion about what was actually recovered. None of that makes the app unusable, but it does mean you should go in with modest expectations: this is a free first attempt at recovery, not a professional data-recovery service.

Key features

  • Photo recovery — scan internal storage for deleted or lost images and preview them before restoring.
  • Video recovery — bring back missing videos, with results grouped and dated for easier browsing.
  • Audio recovery — recover deleted audio files, including music and voice recordings.
  • Document recovery — find PDFs, Word documents, Excel sheets and other files, and restore only the ones you tick.
  • File preview — check a file's name, path, resolution, size and date before you commit to recovering it.
  • Junk files cleaner — scan for unnecessary junk and see how much space cleaning could free.
  • Similar pictures and screenshot cleaner — clear near-duplicates and old screenshots to tidy your gallery.
  • On-device processing — scanning, recovery and cleaning happen on your phone, with no data shared with third parties according to the Play listing.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Trending fast: about 230,000 installs in the last 7 days, roughly 10 times the week before
  • Free, small (about 38 MB) and covers photos, video, audio and documents in one app
  • Preview before restoring, with file details shown, so you restore the right file
  • Honest listing: recovery is not guaranteed, and factory-reset files cannot be recovered
  • On-device scanning with no third-party data sharing declared on Play
  • Useful extras: junk cleaner, similar-pictures cleaner and screenshot remover
Cons
  • Recovery is not guaranteed — some files will simply not come back, and results vary by device
  • Contains ads, and the most helpful critical review calls them constant and intrusive
  • Mixed 3.5-star rating from only about 780 ratings, with 200 one-star ratings in the mix
  • One detailed reviewer found far fewer photos than a rival app on the same device
  • AppBrain lists 40 permissions — broad storage access, so check the permission list before installing
  • Play's data-safety section says data collected by the app cannot be deleted through it

Pricing

Photo Recovery - AllRecover is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The listing data checked for this review shows no in-app purchase price range, so treat any offer you meet inside the app with care and check it before accepting. The current version is 1.0.0.3, updated 23 October 2026, and the download is about 38 MB — small enough to try over mobile data, though a Wi-Fi download is still the safer habit.

Download

Download Photo Recovery on Google Play

If you have just deleted something precious, Photo Recovery is a reasonable free first try: scan, preview, and restore only what you recognise — and stop there if it does not find your file, rather than paying for promises no app can keep.
