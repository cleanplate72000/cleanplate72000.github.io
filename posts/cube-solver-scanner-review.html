Cube Solver Review: Scan & Solve (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Cube Solver Scanner: 3x3 Cube does exactly what its name promises: point your camera at a scrambled cube, or tap in the colours by hand, and it shows you the moves to solve it, step by step. It is a genuinely useful tool for anyone stuck on a cube — but go in knowing the camera scan is not magic, and in poor light or at a bad angle it can misread a face and send you back to re-scan.

Cube Solver Scanner: 3x3 Cube, from developer Pipi Chick Studio, is a camera-based cube solver for Android. It launched on Google Play in October 2023 and has grown into one of the store's most-installed cube tools: Google Play lists 7,000,000+ installs, independent tracker AppBrain counts roughly 230,000 installs in the last 30 days (about 7,700 a day), and the app is currently climbing again — AppBrain's trending free Puzzle list credits it with around 100,000 installs in the last 7 days, nearly double the week before. On Google Play it holds 4.4 stars from 13,520 ratings, and it is rated Everyone, so it works as a family-friendly puzzle helper. The current version is 7.0.2, updated on 1 July 2026, and the download is about 63 MB for Android 8.0 and up.

The home screen lays out the whole toolbox at a glance: scan a real cube, play a virtual one, enter colours by hand, time yourself, or compete.

How it actually works

The headline feature is the camera scan. Hold your phone over each face of your cube, and the app reads the colours and works out a solution, shown as a sequence of moves to follow. If the scan struggles — and several Play reviewers say it does in dim light or when the cube is held at an angle — there is a manual fallback: tap each colour onto a 3D face diagram with the colour picker, face by face, and the solver works from that instead. In practice, the manual route is the reliable one, and the scan is the fast one when the light is good and the cube is held flat to the camera.

Once a solution is ready, the app walks you through it move by move on a realistic 3D cube you can rotate, zoom and pan, so you can check each turn from any angle before you make it on the real cube. A reorient option resets the virtual cube to its starting state, which makes it easy to practise the same solution twice — useful if you are trying to learn the sequence rather than just copy it once.

Scan and Solve: the camera reads a real cube face by face, then the app works out the solution for you.

More than just a solver

The app is not only for rescuing a scrambled cube. Its Play 3D Cube mode lets you twist a virtual cube on screen in sizes from 2x2 all the way up to 15x15, which is a good way to practise without a physical cube to hand. A built-in solution timer tracks how fast you solve, and a Compete mode pits your times against other players on leaderboards — the screenshots show timed head-to-head matches with named opponents. For a beginner, the combination of a solver plus a practice cube in one free app is the real selling point: you can get unstuck, then immediately try the same moves on the virtual cube to understand them.

Manual input: when the camera struggles, tap each colour onto the face diagram with the colour picker instead.

Key features

  • Camera scan: scan a real 3x3 cube with your phone camera and get a solution in seconds.
  • Manual colour input: enter colours face by face with an intuitive colour picker when scanning is not practical.
  • Step-by-step solution: follow the moves on a realistic 3D cube that rotates, zooms and pans.
  • Play 3D Cube: practise on virtual cubes from 2x2 to 15x15.
  • Solution timer and Compete mode: time your solves and race other players on leaderboards.
  • Reorient and reset: return the virtual cube to its starting state to practise a solution again.

Prove your skills: the Compete mode matches your timed solves against other players.

Pros and cons

Pros: solves a real cube from a camera scan or manual input; step-by-step 3D walkthrough you can rotate and inspect; virtual cubes from 2x2 to 15x15 for practice; timer and competitive mode included; free to download; Everyone content rating; a large, established user base with 7 million installs behind it.

Cons: the camera scan can misread faces in poor light or at awkward angles, and some Play reviewers report repeated failed scans; a few tricky positions, such as two swapped corners, have stumped it for some users; it hands you the moves rather than teaching a solving method, so you will only learn if you study the sequence; and the promotional screenshots are small and low-resolution, which says something about the age of the listing even if the app itself is current.

Pricing

Cube Solver Scanner: 3x3 Cube is free to download on Google Play. The app also appears on Google Play's Top Grossing Puzzle chart in Indonesia, which suggests optional paid items may be offered inside the app — check the Play page for the current in-app items before paying for anything. The developer's own listing notes that Cube Solver is independently owned and is not affiliated with any cube brand.

Download

Download Cube Solver Scanner: 3x3 Cube on Google Play

If you have a scrambled cube gathering dust, Cube Solver Scanner: 3x3 Cube is the fastest way to see it solved: scan it in good light, follow the 3D walkthrough, and fall back to manual colour input when the camera disagrees with you. It is not a cube coach, and it will not teach you the method on its own — but as a solver and practice box in one free app, with 7 million installs and a 4.4-star rating behind it, it earns its place on your phone.
