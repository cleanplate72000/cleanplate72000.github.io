Mortal to Immortal Review: Idle RPG (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Mortal to Immortal: Idle RPG is a promising new idle cultivation game: a clear fantasy hook, offline progress that suits short sessions, and flexible character builds. It earns its fast chart climb — but a very large download, a rocky launch day, and a small rating base mean it is worth trying free before you invest serious time in it.

Mortal to Immortal: Idle RPG, from developer Acingfun, arrived on Google Play in October 2026 and has already gathered 28,000 installs — all of them in its first 11 days, according to AppBrain data updated on 25 October 2026. On Google Play it holds about 4.3 stars from over 600 ratings, it is rated Everyone, and it has reached #18 in India's Top New Free Casual chart, as well as the top 40 for new free casual games in the United States and Australia. For a brand-new idle RPG, that is a fast start — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The premise is the classic xianxia fantasy: you start as an ordinary mortal with nothing, and you cultivate your way towards immortality. The developer's own tagline puts it simply — cultivate, break through realms, and become immortal, even while offline. Heaven blocks your path, the game says, so you cut through it; the realms stand above you, so you break through them. It is a power fantasy built for short, regular sessions rather than long sittings, and that is exactly what makes an idle game like this appealing on a phone.

What you actually do

The core loop is cultivation: gather resources, strengthen your techniques, challenge trials, and survive heavenly tribulations to rise from one realm to the next. Every breakthrough unlocks greater power, new combat options, and stronger enemies, so progress always feels like it is going somewhere. Because the game is idle, your cultivation never stops — you earn resources and grow even while offline, then return to claim rewards, upgrade techniques, refine artifacts, and push towards the next realm.

That offline progress is the game's biggest practical selling point. It is designed for players who want a long-term game without a long daily commitment: check in, collect what you have earned, make a few upgrades, set your next goal, and put the phone down. If you like progression games but do not want one that demands hours a day, this structure is a genuine fit.

Key features

  • Choose your Dao — master the Five Elements and combine countless cultivation techniques, building around sword intent, elemental attacks, survival, burst damage, or balanced power.
  • Realm breakthroughs — gather cultivation resources, challenge trials, overcome heavenly tribulations, and rise step by step from mortal to legendary immortal.
  • Forge your legendary weapon — discover spirit weapons and immortal artifacts, refine their power, awaken weapon spirits, and forge a soul-bound weapon.
  • Roguelike battles, endless builds — each challenge offers new techniques and tactical choices, so you can combine skills, adapt your build, and create different synergies.
  • Idle and AFK progress — earn resources and grow even while offline, then return stronger for your next tribulation.
  • Ink-wash fantasy art — a distinctive visual style inspired by Chinese xianxia, wuxia, and martial arts fantasy, with dramatic combat effects.
  • Freedom to switch builds — change your cultivation path freely and experiment without being locked into one build or losing progress.

The build freedom deserves a special mention, because many mobile RPGs punish experimentation. Here, the developer explicitly promises that you can change your cultivation path without losing progress, which makes trying a new elemental build or a different combat style a low-risk decision rather than a costly restart.

Pros and cons

Pros: a clear, appealing fantasy premise; genuine offline and AFK progression for short sessions; flexible builds with no lock-in; distinctive ink-wash art; rated Everyone; free to download.

Cons: a very large 771 MB download; a rocky launch day with maintenance complaints from early players; a still-small rating base of just over 600 ratings; the store listing checked for this review does not publish an in-app purchase price range; AppBrain lists 29 permissions for the app.

The honest caveats

First, the size. At about 771 MB, this is a big download — check your storage and use Wi-Fi before you install it, because it is far larger than most casual games. Second, the launch. Early user comments from 19 October 2026 report a long maintenance period on launch day, with several players saying they could not enter the game for hours. That may well be a one-off launch problem rather than an ongoing one, and the game's chart climb since then suggests players have kept coming — but it is worth knowing before you install, and it is part of why this review scores the game 4.0 rather than higher despite its 4.3-star average.

Third, the rating base is still small. Just over 600 ratings is enough to show early players broadly like the game, but not enough to settle how it holds up over months of play, especially for an idle game whose long-term balance only becomes clear over time. Treat the current score as an early signal, not a final verdict.

Pricing and privacy

Mortal to Immortal: Idle RPG is free to download, version 1.3.0, updated on 22 October 2026. The store listing checked for this review does not publish an in-app purchase price range, so check the costs shown inside the game before you spend anything — do not assume every upgrade is free just because the download is.

On privacy, the Play data-safety listing is mixed in an interesting way: the developer states that the app shares no data with third parties and collects no user data, and that data is encrypted in transit — but it also states that data cannot be deleted. That combination is worth noting if you care about account control, even though the no-collection claim itself is reassuring.

Mortal to Immortal is the one to pick if you want steady, long-term progression — a character who keeps growing even when you are not playing — rather than a quick puzzle or a cosy wander.

The bottom line: Mortal to Immortal earns its trending spot. The cultivation fantasy is clear, the offline progress is genuinely convenient, and the flexible builds remove one of the usual frustrations of mobile RPGs. Just go in with eyes open about the 771 MB download and the bumpy launch, try it free first, and let it prove its long-term staying power before you spend anything on it.
