PDF Pilot Review: Handy PDF Tools (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. PDF Pilot - Reader & Tools is a small, focused document app that does one job well: it opens PDFs and office files, turns pictures into PDFs, and merges or splits files without making you learn a complicated editor. It is free, light at about 20 MB, and already popular — just remember it is a first version, so expect ads and a still-small rating base.

PDF Pilot - Reader & Tools, from developer Suffron, arrived on Google Play in September 2026 and has already reached about 210,000 installs tracked in total — all of them in its first 30 days, averaging roughly 16,000 installs a day according to AppBrain's 24 October 2026 snapshot. On Google Play it holds 4.5 stars from about 400 ratings, and it has climbed into the top 100 free Tools apps, reaching #27 in India and #50 in Japan. For a version 1.0.0 app barely a month old, that is a remarkably fast start — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The pitch is refreshingly plain. Most phones already open a PDF somehow, but juggling a separate reader, a scanner-style converter and a merge tool gets messy fast. PDF Pilot puts the everyday document jobs in one place: open a file, find it again later, make a PDF from a photo, convert between formats, merge two PDFs into one, or split one PDF apart. It is aimed, in the developer's own words, at students, office workers and anyone who regularly handles digital documents — not at power users who need deep PDF editing.

How it works

Open the app and you get a simple document list. The home screen groups files by type — All, Word, Excel, PPT, PDF and TXT — with a search bar on top, so a form you opened last week is a tap away instead of a hunt through your downloads folder. Each entry shows its name, date and size, and a recent-files view keeps your latest documents at hand. That sounds basic, but it is exactly the part many free readers fumble: finding the file again is half the job.

Reading itself is straightforward. The reader view shows a clean page with controls for full screen, landscape, share and more, so you can settle in with a long article or flip a wide document sideways. There is also a read-aloud feature: the app can read supported document text out loud, and playback keeps going when the app moves to the background or the screen locks, with pause and stop from the media notification. That is a genuinely useful touch for long reports, study notes or anything you would rather listen to than stare at.

Creating, converting and editing PDFs

Where PDF Pilot earns its keep is the tools screen. From here you can create a PDF from JPG or PNG images and from camera scans — handy for turning a photographed form, receipt or handwritten page into a shareable file — and convert supported documents and images into PDF. The conversion list includes Word to PDF, image to PDF and PDF to image, so a document that arrives in the wrong format does not need a second app.

The editing tools cover the two jobs people actually ask about most: merge (combine several PDFs into one document) and split (divide one PDF into separate files), with a prompt to review the selected files before each operation starts. The tools screen also lists an e-signature option. The store description itself does not go into detail about that option, so treat it as a listed tool to try rather than a promised full signing workflow — but for everyday merging and splitting, the feature set matches what the screenshots promise.

Why it is trending

PDF Pilot is trending because it is small, simple and immediately useful. At about 20 MB, it is a fraction of the size of many office suites, and it does not ask you to learn a new workflow — it opens the files already on your phone and adds the three conversions people actually need. AppBrain calls it “New, Growing fast, Loved by users”, and the numbers back that up: every one of its roughly 210,000 installs arrived in the last 30 days, which is rare for a utility app without a big brand behind it.

Timing helps too. Students settling into a new term and office workers clearing end-of-year paperwork both need exactly this kind of tool, and a free app that handles PDFs, Word, Excel and PowerPoint files in one place is an easy recommendation to pass along. The 4.5-star average suggests early users are happy — though, as with any new app, that average rests on only about 400 ratings so far, and it will move as more people arrive.

Key features

  • Open many document formats — PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and TXT files in one app, on phone and tablet.
  • Simple document list — files grouped by type with a search bar and a recent-files view, so documents are easy to find again.
  • Create PDFs from images and scans — turn JPG or PNG pictures and camera scans into PDF documents.
  • Convert between formats — Word to PDF, image to PDF and PDF to image conversion from the tools screen.
  • Merge and split PDFs — combine several PDFs into one, or divide one PDF into separate files, reviewing your selection first.
  • Read-aloud playback — listen to supported document text, with playback continuing in the background or with the screen locked.
  • Local document management — find and organise compatible documents stored in different folders on your device.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Strong start: 4.5 stars and about 210,000 installs, all in its first month, with top-100 Tools chart showings in India and Japan
  • Genuinely useful everyday toolkit — reading, creating, converting, merging and splitting in one small app
  • Light download at about 20 MB, and a clean, simple layout that needs no learning curve
  • Read-aloud with background and lock-screen playback is a thoughtful extra for long documents
  • Everyone content rating, and free to download
Cons
  • Contains ads, as expected from a free utility — and the listing data checked shows no ad-free option to buy
  • Still version 1.0.0 with only about 400 ratings, so the 4.5 average is early and may move
  • The e-signature tool is listed in the app's screenshots but not detailed in the store description, so its limits are unclear
  • AppBrain lists 27 permissions for the app, which is a fair number for a document reader — worth a glance before installing
  • Focused on everyday document jobs, not deep PDF editing — power users will still want a full editor

Pricing

PDF Pilot - Reader & Tools is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The listing data checked for this review shows it as free with ads and no in-app purchase price range, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 1.0.0, updated 18 September 2026, and the download is about 20 MB — small enough to try without thinking twice.

Download

Download PDF Pilot on Google Play

If you just want your phone to handle documents without fuss — open it, find it again, turn a photo into a PDF, merge two files into one — PDF Pilot is an easy recommendation: a 4.5-star average, a 20 MB download, and a toolkit that covers the jobs most people actually need.
