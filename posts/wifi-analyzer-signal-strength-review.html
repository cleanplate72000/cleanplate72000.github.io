WiFi Analyzer Signal Strength Review (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. WiFi Analyzer: Signal Strength, from developer SPIRITUAL CAPITAL INC, does the core job it promises: it measures your Wi-Fi signal in real time, tests download speed, upload speed and ping, lists nearby networks and shows the devices on your own network — all in a small 24.9 MB free app. But go in with your eyes open: the Play listing confirms it contains ads, and the most recent one-star reviews complain most about long, interrupting ads. Worth trying if your Wi-Fi has a dead spot; do not install it expecting it to reveal a Wi-Fi password or kick devices off your network, because it does neither.

WiFi Analyzer: Signal Strength (package com.wifi.analyzer.connection.monitor.app) has been on Google Play since August 2026 and is currently at version 1.0.1, updated 8 October 2026. On Google Play it holds 4.2 stars from about 2,216 ratings and sits in the 100,000+ install bracket, while independent tracker AppBrain estimates roughly 340,000 total installs, with about 280,000 of those in the last 30 days. It is trending now because that growth is accelerating: AppBrain's trending list for 26 October 2026 puts it at about 180,000 installs in the last 7 days, three times the week before, and it ranks in the Top Free Personalization lists in several countries. It is free to download, contains ads, needs Android 7.0 or higher, and Google Play rates its content Everyone.

That combination — a very young app, a sharp weekly jump, and a genuinely everyday problem — is exactly why it earns a review this week. Most people blame their internet provider when Wi-Fi feels slow, when the real culprit is often a weak signal in one room, a congested channel, or a router in the wrong corner. This app is built for that diagnosis: walk around with the signal meter running, and you can see, room by room, where the signal holds up and where it falls away.

What it actually does

The developer's own feature list is refreshingly plain, and it matches the screenshots. The WiFi Network Analyzer shows details of your current connection, the WiFi Signal Strength meter measures signal in real time so you can find a stronger spot, and the WiFi Speed Tester checks download speed, upload speed and ping. Around those three core tools sit a WiFi Finder for browsing nearby networks, a Connected Devices list for seeing what is on your current network, a WiFi Hotspot shortcut that opens your device's hotspot settings, a WiFi QR Scanner and Sharing tool, a Password Generator for creating strong new passwords, and a set of general Network Tools in one interface.

None of this is magic, and that is the point. A signal meter will not make your broadband faster, but it will tell you whether the problem is the Wi-Fi or the connection itself. If the meter reads strong next to the router and weak in the bedroom, moving the router, or moving yourself closer to it, is the fix — no technician needed. If the speed test shows a healthy result on the router but a poor one on your phone in the far room, you have found a dead spot rather than a provider fault.

The signal meter is the star

The store screenshots lead with the signal analysis screen: a large percentage gauge, the network name, and the signal, strength and frequency details underneath. That screen is the app's best reason to exist. Stand where the Wi-Fi feels slow, check the reading, then move a few steps and check again. A simple before-and-after like that is more useful than any list of technical terms, and it is the feature a first-time user should try first.

The speed test is the natural second check. A slow video call can be caused by a weak signal, a slow broadband line, or both, and testing the two separately helps you tell them apart. Run the signal meter first, then the speed test in the same spot, and you have a much clearer picture to take to your provider — or to act on yourself by repositioning the router.

Networks, devices and handy extras

The WiFi Manager menu gathers the everyday extras in one place: scan a Wi-Fi QR code, share your Wi-Fi details as a QR code, generate a password, view saved passwords for networks you manage, check connected devices, open the Wi-Fi map, run a speed test and check signal strength. For a household, the two most useful extras are the QR sharing — letting a guest join without dictating a long password — and the connected-devices list, which shows at a glance whether an unfamiliar device is sitting on your home network.

Two limits are worth stating plainly, because reviewers have tripped over them. First, the connected-devices list is a list, not a remote control: it shows what is connected, but it does not disconnect a device for you — that still has to be done in your router's own settings. Second, the password generator creates new strong passwords; it does not reveal the password of a network you are not already connected to, and one reviewer who expected exactly that left a one-star review when it did not. Judged on what it actually claims to do, the app is fair; judged on what some downloaders hoped it would do, it disappoints, and that gap explains part of its divided recent reviews.

Why the verdict is 3.5, not higher

The honest caveat is the advertising. The Play listing confirms the app contains ads, and the most recent one-star reviews are blunt about them: long ads on nearly every tap, ads that delay getting into the app at all, and frustration that the ad load spoils an otherwise simple tool. A 4.2-star average built from about 2,200 ratings still leans positive, but the recent trend of complaints is the thing a new downloader will actually feel first. If you only need a one-off signal check — find the dead spot, move the router, done — the ads are a brief annoyance. If you plan to live in the app daily, they may wear you down.

Two smaller caveats are worth knowing before you install. First, this is a very young app: it launched in August 2026 and is only on version 1.0.1, so expect rough edges and further updates rather than a finished, polished product. Second, AppBrain counts 26 permissions and 39 libraries in the app, which is a broad footprint for a simple meter — worth a glance at the permission list on the Play page before you grant anything it does not need for the feature you want. On the plus side, at 24.9 MB it is a light download, it runs on Android 7.0 and up, and it costs nothing to try.

Pros
  • Trending fast: about 180,000 installs in the last 7 days, three times the week before, and roughly 340,000 total installs
  • Useful core tools: real-time signal meter, download/upload/ping speed test, nearby-network finder and connected-devices list
  • Handy extras: Wi-Fi QR scan and share, password generator and a hotspot shortcut in one menu
  • Free to download, small at 24.9 MB, runs on Android 7.0 and up, rated Everyone
  • 4.2 stars from about 2,216 ratings on Google Play
Cons
  • Contains ads, and recent one-star reviews complain most about long, interrupting ads
  • Lists connected devices but cannot disconnect them — that needs your router's own settings
  • Password generator makes new passwords; it does not reveal an unknown network's password
  • Very young app on version 1.0.1, so expect rough edges and further updates
  • Broad permission footprint (26 permissions per AppBrain) — check what you grant before use

Pricing

WiFi Analyzer: Signal Strength is free to download on Google Play, and its listing confirms it contains ads. The store page checked for this review shows no in-app purchase price range, so the free download is the honest headline — but the advertising is the real cost to weigh. If you need one diagnosis for a slow room, try it, run the signal meter and speed test, and judge it on that single job before keeping it.

Download

Download WiFi Analyzer: Signal Strength on Google Play

If your Wi-Fi has a room where video calls freeze and videos buffer, WiFi Analyzer: Signal Strength is worth one walk-around test: a clear signal meter, a proper speed test and a device list, free, in a 25 MB app. If the ads get in your way, delete it once you have your answer — the diagnosis is the value, not the app itself.
