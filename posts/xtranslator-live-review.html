XTranslator Live Review: Translate All (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. XTranslator Live is a genuinely handy all-in-one translator: camera, voice, screen and PDF translation in one free app, with 100+ languages and a fast-growing user base. It earns its place here on usefulness alone — just go in knowing that its own listing says it contains ads, despite the Play description calling its interface ad-free, and that some recent reviewers complain about aggressive advertising.

XTranslator Live: AI Translate, from developer AI Translator, is one of the fastest-climbing productivity apps on Google Play right now. It holds 4.4 stars from about 1,800 ratings, has been installed roughly 470,000 times in total, and about 160,000 of those installs came in the last 30 days — around 5,200 a day. It is ranked #7 in Top Free Productivity in South Korea, #8 in Japan and #51 in the United States. The app has been on Play since April 2026, and its latest version, 1.0.26, arrived on 11 October 2026. At about 64.9 MB with an Everyone content rating, it is a light, family-friendly download — and that sharp recent surge is why it earns a review here this week.

The pitch is simple: translate anything, anywhere. Point your camera at a sign, speak naturally into a conversation, read text inside another app, or drop in a whole document. The developer's tagline sums it up — “Translate camera, voice & screen instantly. 100+ languages.” For travellers, students and anyone who works across languages, that combination in a single free app is the whole appeal.

What XTranslator Live actually does

The app covers five translation jobs that would normally need two or three separate tools:

  • Camera and photo translation — point the camera at menus, signs, documents or any printed text and get an instant overlay translation. It also works with photos already in your gallery, so a picture of a timetable or a label taken earlier can be translated later.
  • Voice and conversation translation — real-time two-way voice translation. You speak in your language, the app translates aloud, the other person replies, and it translates back to you. That is the feature that matters most for face-to-face travel and meetings.
  • Screen translation — translate text inside other apps, including WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, email, news and games, without endless copy-pasting.
  • PDF and document translation — upload a PDF or document and get the whole thing translated while, according to the developer, keeping the original layout, paragraphs, images, tables and font styles. It also handles scanned and image-based PDFs using OCR, which is genuinely useful for work reports, academic papers, contracts and ebooks.
  • Text translation and an AI Coach — type or paste any text for a context-aware translation, and practise speaking with pronunciation feedback and conversation practice if you are learning a language rather than just translating.

The language list is broad: English, Spanish, French, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, German, Italian, Russian, Arabic, Hindi, Thai and many more — over 100 in total, according to the developer. The developer also claims results in under 2 seconds and context-aware handling of slang and idioms. Treat that speed claim as the developer's own wording rather than an independently measured figure, but the feature set itself is unusually complete for a free app.

Camera translation in practice

Camera translation is where this app is most likely to win you over. The classic use cases all work the same way: a restaurant menu in Tokyo, a street sign in Seoul, a museum label in Paris, a product label in a foreign supermarket. Point, read, move on. The PDF feature is the quieter star, though — translating a whole document while keeping its layout is a chore in most free tools, and here it is presented as a core feature rather than an afterthought.

Who it is best for

Travellers will get the most immediate value: menus, signs, tickets and quick conversations, all in one app. Students can use it for foreign-language reading and for the AI Coach's speaking practice. Professionals in international meetings get the conversation mode and document translation. And anyone who follows creators or news in another language can use screen translation to read foreign social-media posts without switching apps. If you only ever translate the odd word, your phone's built-in tools may be enough — this app earns its space when you translate often, in several different ways.

Pricing

XTranslator Live is free to download on Google Play (price listed as 0 USD). One point needs honesty rather than polish: the Play description describes a “clean, ad-free interface”, but the Play listing data for the app says it contains ads, and several recent reviewers complain about aggressive advertising, including ads that push other app downloads. That contradiction is worth knowing before you install. We could not verify a premium tier or subscription price from the listing checked, so if the app offers one inside, judge it on its own terms and check what it actually unlocks before paying anything.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Camera, voice, screen, text and PDF translation in one free app
  • 100+ languages, including Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Arabic and Thai
  • Fast-growing and well rated: 4.4 stars, about 470,000 installs
  • Light download at about 64.9 MB, Everyone content rating
  • PDF translation keeps document layout, according to the developer
  • AI Coach adds language-learning practice, not just translation

Cons

  • Listing says it contains ads, despite the description claiming an ad-free interface
  • Recent reviewers report aggressive ads and misleading ad promotions
  • Premium or subscription details could not be verified from the listing checked
  • Translation quality for slang and idioms will vary by language pair, as with any machine translator
  • Smaller rating base (about 1,800 ratings) than the biggest translator apps

Should you download it?

Yes, with eyes open. If you translate in more than one way — camera one minute, voice the next, a PDF later — XTranslator Live replaces several apps with one free download, and its recent growth suggests a lot of people are finding exactly that useful. The ad situation is the real caveat: try the free version first, and if the ads bother you, that is your answer before spending anything.
