Brain Test Review: Tricky Puzzles (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.2 / 5. Brain Test: Tricky Puzzles is one of the most popular puzzle games on Google Play for a good reason: its levels refuse to be solved the obvious way, and that twist is genuinely funny. It is free, rated Everyone, works offline, and has a 4.7-star average from more than 9 million ratings — but be ready for very frequent ads, which are the game's biggest and most complained-about flaw.

Brain Test: Tricky Puzzles, from developer Unico Studio, has been on Google Play since November 2019 and is still climbing the charts in October 2026. AppBrain tracks 390 million installs in total, with 3.2 million of those in the last 30 days — around 110,000 new installs a day — and the game holds #1 in the Top Free Trivia chart in the United States and several other countries. Its live Google Play rating stands at 4.7 stars from about 9.3 million ratings, the current version is 2.776.0 (updated 16 September 2026), the download is about 178 MB, it needs Android 7.0 or later, and Google Play rates its content Everyone. It is free to download and contains ads.

The idea is simple, and it is the whole joke. Each level gives you a short instruction — fix the kettle, calm the angry fruit, work out what the star equals — and the answer is almost never the one you try first. You may need to drag an object, shake the phone, tap things in an unexpected order, or simply read the question more carefully than you did. The game calls this breaking common sense, and after a few levels you start to expect the trap, which is half the fun: you catch yourself thinking like the puzzle designer instead of like a test-taker.

A puzzle game that rewards lateral thinking

Level 498, shown in the second screenshot, is a good example of the format. The prompt says "The button is not working", and the scene shows a kettle, a glass of water, a remote control and a pair of scissors, all drawn in the game's cheerful notebook style. Nothing in the picture tells you the answer outright, and that is the point. You poke at the scene, try the obvious fix, watch it fail, and then try something sillier — and the sillier idea is usually the right one. It is a short, self-contained laugh rather than a long brain-training programme, and it works best in exactly that form: a few levels while you wait, not a two-hour session.

The notebook presentation helps. Every level looks like a page from a doodle pad, with the level number at the top, a hint bulb in the corner, and the puzzle drawn in bright, hand-drawn style. There is no timer breathing down your neck and no lives system punishing a wrong guess in the early levels, so failing a level feels like part of the joke rather than a penalty. That low-pressure design is a big reason the game works for family gatherings, which the developer itself highlights: one person holds the phone, everyone else shouts suggestions, and the wrong suggestions are often funnier than the right one.

Not a test — a trick, every time

Do not download Brain Test expecting a serious IQ measurement. The developer markets it as a funny tricky test, but the levels are lateral-thinking jokes, not psychometric questions, and your score says nothing reliable about your intelligence. What the game genuinely exercises is a different skill: stopping yourself from answering on autopilot. Level 592, in the third screenshot, asks what a star equals after a triangle equals 3, a square equals 4 and a pentagon equals 5. The pattern is there if you slow down and count — and the satisfaction comes from spotting it yourself rather than from being told you are clever.

That distinction matters for younger players too. Because the content is rated Everyone and the puzzles are visual and word-based rather than violent or scary, it is an easy game to hand to a child for a few levels — with one important exception, which is the ads (see below). The puzzles themselves are family-safe, the humour is gentle, and there is no chat with strangers or user-generated content to worry about. It is a single-player puzzle game, and it stays that way.

Offline play, and hundreds of levels

Another genuine strength is that Brain Test works offline. The developer lists offline play as a headline feature, and for a puzzle game that matters: it is exactly the kind of game you open on a flight, in a waiting room, or somewhere with patchy signal. The promo art promises 500+ funny levels, and with a back catalogue running since 2019 plus sequels in the same series — Brain Test 2, 3 and 4 — you are unlikely to run out of puzzles quickly. If you do finish and want more of the same style, the same developer's sequels are the obvious next stop rather than a random clone.

The game also added social login support in its latest update, so you can save progress and carry your profile across devices. That is a convenience, not a requirement: the core game is still a solo puzzle experience, and you should not feel obliged to sign in just to play a few levels. If you do sign in, use an account you are happy to tie to a game, as with any app that offers cloud saves.

The ads problem, honestly stated

Here is the catch, and it is a big one: Brain Test's most common complaint in recent Play reviews is the sheer frequency of ads. Players report short bursts of gameplay interrupted by long ad breaks, with some saying the ads feel more frequent than the puzzles themselves. The game's 4.7-star average survives that criticism because the puzzles themselves are well liked, but if you are someone who hates interrupted play, this will frustrate you fast. The honest advice is to try the free version for a few levels first and see whether the ad rhythm bothers you before you invest time in hundreds of levels. Do not pay for anything inside the game until you know you enjoy it enough to keep playing past the first dozen levels.

The large download size is a smaller caveat: at about 178 MB, it is heavier than many puzzle games, so download it over Wi-Fi if your data is limited. And a final note on expectations: once you have played a hundred levels, you will start to recognise the designer's favourite tricks, and the surprise fades a little. That is normal for this kind of game — take a break, come back later, and the next batch of levels will feel fresh again.

Key features

  • Tricky, lateral-thinking puzzles — every level hides an unexpected answer behind an obvious-looking question.
  • 500+ funny levels — a huge back catalogue, plus sequels from the same developer when you finish.
  • Offline play — no internet needed, so it works on flights and in low-signal spots.
  • Everyone rating — family-safe, single-player puzzles with no chat or user-generated content.
  • Relaxed, untimed format — no lives system punishing wrong guesses, so failing is part of the fun.
  • Notebook art style — bright, hand-drawn levels that are easy to read at a glance.
  • Social login saves — optional progress saving across devices in the latest version.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Hugely popular and well-loved: 4.7 stars from 9.3 million ratings, 390 million installs
  • Genuinely clever puzzles that reward thinking differently, not just tapping faster
  • Free, rated Everyone, and playable offline — ideal for short breaks and family play
  • No timer or lives pressure, so it stays relaxing rather than stressful
  • Still actively updated, with 3.2 million installs in the last 30 days alone
Cons
  • Very frequent ads are the top complaint in recent player reviews
  • Large 178 MB download for a puzzle game — use Wi-Fi if data is tight
  • The surprise wears thin after many levels, once you learn the designer's tricks
  • Not a real IQ test, despite the marketing — treat the "test" framing as a joke
  • Optional sign-in for cloud saves is a convenience, not something every player needs

Pricing

Brain Test: Tricky Puzzles is free to download on Google Play, and the listing data checked for this review shows it contains ads. Optional in-app purchases may be offered inside the game; check the Play listing for the current offers before buying anything, and try the free levels first to see whether the ad frequency bothers you. For a game you may only play in short bursts, the free version is the right place to start.

Download

Download Brain Test: Tricky Puzzles on Google Play

If you like puzzles that make you laugh at your own first answer, Brain Test is an easy recommendation: free, family-safe, playable offline, and proven by hundreds of millions of installs. Just go in knowing the ads are frequent, and play it in short bursts where it shines.
