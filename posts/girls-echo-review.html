Girls Echo Review: Goddess RPG (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Girls Echo is a polished, story-first goddess RPG that has shot up the new-release charts for a reason: beautiful animated characters, a team-building battle system, and an offline-growth loop that keeps your party improving while you are away. It is free, Teen-rated, and already past 100,000 downloads on Google Play. The caveats are real, though — reviewers report a punishing paywall further in, a tutorial that can break on slower phones, and a download of around 463 MB, so check your storage before you tap install.

Girls Echo, from developer HOWLYO GAME, launched on Google Play in October 2026 and was updated on 16 October 2026. In barely two weeks it climbed to #2 in the Top New Free Role Playing charts in the United States, and it holds around 4.3 stars from more than 12,700 ratings. Google Play shows the download bracket as 100,000+, while AppBrain estimates roughly 290,000 installs since launch — either way, this is one of the fastest-growing new RPGs of the month, and that is why it earns a review here.

The premise is pure fantasy romance. A catastrophe is tearing apart the boundary between the real world and the ancient realm of Shenzhou, and Phantoms from forgotten eras and legends are emerging to erase everything you know. You enter into a covenant with a team of goddesses, and your job is to build that team, fight back the Phantoms, and grow closer to your goddesses along the way. If you like anime RPGs where the characters matter as much as the combat, this is very much your kind of game.

How it plays

At its core, Girls Echo is a team-building RPG. You collect goddesses, each with her own look, personality and abilities, arrange them into a dream team, and set your strategy before battle. Combat itself is animated and flashy — your goddesses unleash elemental attacks on Phantom enemies while damage numbers fly — and the presentation is a clear step above most free mobile RPGs, with expressive character animation and detailed, colourful art throughout.

What sets it apart from a standard gacha battler is the bonding system. Outside battle, you can spend time with your goddesses on dates, deepen your relationship, and unlock special moments and exclusive CG endings. Those bonds are not just for show: the closer you become to a goddess, the stronger she grows in battle, so time spent on dates feeds directly back into your combat power. It is a neat loop — affection becomes progression — and it gives you a reason to care about characters beyond their stats.

Offline growth, and a story worth following

Girls Echo also leans on offline growth: your girls keep growing even while you are away, so you come back to progress rather than a dead stop. That makes it a good fit for players who want an RPG they can dip into rather than live in — short sessions still move your team forward.

The story itself gets unusual praise in player reviews. One reviewer calls it one of the better RPG stories they have played on mobile and credits the developers with taking real care over the storytelling, even while noting that the plot can be hard to follow in places and that long text passages sometimes scroll past too quickly to read. That is an honest picture: ambitious writing, slightly rough presentation.

Key features

  • Goddess team building — collect beautifully animated goddesses and build your own dream team with real strategy behind the line-up.
  • Animated, story-driven battles — fight Phantoms from the ancient realm of Shenzhou in flashy, expressive combat.
  • Dates and bonds that boost power — spend time with your goddesses, raise intimacy, unlock special moments and CG endings, and grow stronger together.
  • Offline growth — your team keeps progressing while you are away, so short sessions still count.
  • A fantasy story with ambition — two worlds, an ancient pact, and a cast players single out for its writing.
  • Fast-growing community — a #2 new-release RPG ranking in the US and more than 12,700 ratings within weeks of launch.

Pros and cons

What we like: the art and animation are genuinely lovely; the bond system makes characters feel worth investing in; offline growth respects your time; and the early chart run plus a 4.3-star average suggest most new players are enjoying the ride.

What to watch out for: the paywall. Several reviewers say progression stalls once your profile level gates the story, leaving you waiting on passive battle experience unless you pay for extra quick battles — one calls it pay-to-play after a strong start. A tutorial bug is also reported on slower phones, where a forced step can leave a new player stuck. And at roughly 463 MB, this is a big install: free up space first, and note that the game contains ads and in-app purchases. Finally, this is a Teen-rated game (mild violence) built around romance and dating mechanics — it is a teen-and-up RPG, not a kids' title.

Pricing

Girls Echo is free to download and play on Android. It contains ads and offers in-app purchases, and as the reviews above make clear, spending can speed up progression considerably — but the core game, including team building, battles, dates and offline growth, is playable without paying.

On privacy, the Play Store listing states that the app may share and collect personal info, that data is encrypted in transit, and that you can request that data be deleted. Worth a glance at the developer's privacy details before you sign in, as with any account-based RPG.

Like this kind of anime RPG? We also reviewed Dragonbound, another new fantasy RPG on the site. Dragonbound is the dragon-raising adventure; Girls Echo is the one to pick if story, character bonds and a goddess team are what draw you in.

The bottom line: Girls Echo earns its chart climb — it looks gorgeous, its bond system gives the RPG loop real heart, and its offline growth suits busy players. Just go in with eyes open about the mid-game paywall and the hefty download size, and you will get a very good free RPG out of it.
