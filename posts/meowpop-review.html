Meowpop Review: Cosy Cat Sorting (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Meowpop is a genuinely charming idea — fluffy cats in bubbles, sorted three at a time into cosy beds — and it is already a chart climber for new puzzle games. But it is an honest 3.5, not a 5: its early rating is mixed, extra beds can feel gated, and some players report short timers that sit awkwardly with its relaxing promise. Download it for the cats and the sorting loop, not for a flawless puzzle game.

Meowpop: Cat Sort Puzzle, from developer Hyper Surfer Games, launched on Google Play in September 2026 and has already passed the 100,000+ install mark on the Play Store itself, with around 160,000 installs tracked in its first month — roughly 6,800 installs a day. That pace has put it at #8 in the Top New Free Puzzle chart in Brazil, #11 in Japan and #17 in France, with further Top New Free showings in Germany and the United States. For a sorting game barely a month old, that is real traction — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

On Google Play it holds around 3.8 stars from fewer than 100 ratings at the time of this review. That is a small, early base, and it matters: the average will move as more players arrive, and the split in early reviews tells a useful story. Fans praise the adorable cats and the smooth, simple loop; critics complain about timers and about levels that feel blocked until extra beds are unlocked. Both things can be true of the same game, and this review keeps both in view.

How it plays

The idea is delightfully simple, and the game explains it in one screen. Cats of every colour and breed are floating in bubbles. Each bed at the top of the board wants three cats of the same kind — check the tag under the bed to see which cat it is waiting for. Tap a cat in a bubble and it hops into its matching bed, or waits in one of 5 holding spots if its bed is not ready yet. Fill a bed with three matching cats and it is sent off, bringing a new bed in. Clear every bubble and you win the level.

The catch is the planning. When all 5 holding spots are full, the level is lost, so you cannot just tap cats at random and hope. You have to look ahead: which bed is one cat away from complete, which bubble cats are blocking the ones you need, and whether taking a cat now will strand you two moves later. It is the same satisfying tension as any good sorting puzzle, softened by the fact that the pieces are cats in bubbles rather than coloured balls in tubes. Early levels teach the loop gently; later levels, as the screenshots show, stack more beds, more breeds and more bubble tricks on top of it.

What is inside

Meowpop leans hard into its cosy presentation. The Play listing promises 1,000+ levels that get more challenging as you go, with adorable 3D cats — orange tabbies, calicos, Siamese, fluffy longhairs and more — soft sounds and a warm, relaxing look. There are no lives and no waiting in the listing's own words, so a failed level costs you a retry, not a timer on your next attempt — although, as noted above, some early players report short in-level timers on certain stages, so treat the relaxing promise as mostly true rather than absolute.

Three boosters help when a board turns against you: Fill completes a bed right away, Clear empties your 5 holding spots, and Shuffle mixes up the cats in the bubbles. The gameplay screenshots show numbered booster bubbles and special bubble types sitting on the board itself, which suggests later levels layer these tools into the puzzles rather than leaving them as a panic button on the side. Extra beds can also be unlocked when you need more room to sort — and this is where the honest caveat lands, because early reviewers say those extra beds can feel gated behind watching ads, with some levels feeling near-impossible on the starting bed count alone.

Who it suits

Meowpop suits anyone who likes a sorting puzzle but is tired of coloured balls and tubes. The one-tap controls mean it is genuinely easy to pick up — one tap is all it takes, and a quick level fits a coffee break or a commute. Cat lovers will enjoy recognising breeds and watching the beds fill, and the lack of lives means you can retry a tricky board without being locked out. It is rated Everyone on Google Play, so it is a safe, family-friendly pick in terms of content.

It suits patient players more than perfectionists. If you want a puzzle game that never pushes back, the holding-spot limit and the occasional timer complaint may frustrate you. If you enjoy planning three moves ahead and do not mind the odd ad break in a free game, the loop itself — spot, send, clear — stays satisfying well past the first few dozen levels, and the sheer number of levels means there is plenty of game here for the price, which is nothing.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Genuinely charming twist on sorting — cats in bubbles and cosy beds, instantly easy to understand
  • Real chart traction: 100,000+ Play installs and Top New Free Puzzle rankings in Brazil, Japan and France
  • One-tap play, 1,000+ levels, and no lives or waiting between attempts, per the Play listing
  • Everyone content rating, with a warm, relaxing look and soft sounds
  • Useful boosters — Fill, Clear and Shuffle — for boards that turn against you
Cons
  • Mixed early rating of around 3.8 stars from fewer than 100 ratings — a small base that may move
  • Some early players report short timers and levels that feel blocked until extra beds are unlocked, sometimes via ads
  • Contains ads, as expected from a free game
  • About 152 MB — a fair size for a puzzle game, though not a tiny one
  • One game type done well, rather than a variety of modes — know what you are downloading

Pricing

Meowpop: Cat Sort Puzzle is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The listing data checked for this review shows it as free with ads and no in-app purchase price range, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 1.2.1, updated 16 October 2026, and the download is about 152 MB — worth it if the cat-sorting loop clicks for you, and easy to delete if the timer complaints turn out to match your experience.

Download

Download Meowpop on Google Play

If you like a sorting puzzle that rewards observation over speed, Meowpop is worth a try: a fresh cat-and-bed twist, real chart momentum, and a loop — pop, sort, clear — that stays satisfying well past the first few boards. Just go in with eyes open about the mixed early rating and the ad-gated extra beds.
