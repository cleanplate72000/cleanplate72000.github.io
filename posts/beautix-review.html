Beautix Review: Try a New Hairstyle (2026)

Quick verdict — 3 / 5. Beautix does exactly what it promises: it shows you a new hairstyle or hair colour on your own photo before you commit at the salon. But the free experience is buried under ads and paywalls, with reviewers reporting that free uses run out fast. Great idea, frustrating free tier — try it free, and only pay if you love it.

Beautix - AI Hair Changer, from developer Growly, is one of the fastest-climbing photography apps on Google Play right now. It holds 3.8 stars from about 7,300 ratings, sits in the 1,000,000+ downloads bracket, and is rated Everyone. Independent tracking by AppBrain puts it higher still — around 2.3 million installs, with roughly 630,000 of those in the last 30 days — and it has reached #3 in Top Free Photography in India, with top-10 Photography placings in Denmark and Norway as well. For an app that only appeared on Google Play in May 2026 (current version 1.3.6, updated 19 October 2026), that is a genuine breakout, and it is why it earns a review here.

The pitch is one every salon customer understands: no more guessing. Upload a photo of yourself, pick a cut or a colour, and Beautix's AI shows you the result in seconds — before scissors or dye touch your real hair. At about 145 MB it is a chunky install for a single-purpose app, and it is free to download, but nearly everything beyond a taste of it sits behind ads and a premium upgrade. Read the pricing section before you pay.

What Beautix actually does

Beautix is a virtual try-on app, not a general photo editor. You upload a photo (or take a selfie in the app), and its AI maps your hair and face, then re-renders the photo with a different hairstyle, colour, or both. The Play Store listing groups the features into four areas:

  • AI Hairstyle Generator: a huge range of styles in one tap — curly, straight, bobs, waves, braids, fades, and more, for every gender and hair type.
  • Realistic Hair Coloring: natural shades, trendy pastels, bold fashion colours, and highlights, so you can test the colour that suits you best.
  • Personalized Suggestions: upload your photo and let the AI recommend flattering styles based on your features and preferences.
  • Diverse Style Library: everything from the latest trends to timeless classic looks, all in one place.

The workflow is deliberately simple: no tools, no mess, no appointments — just upload a photo and start exploring. That simplicity is the whole appeal. You do not need to know anything about editing or AI prompting; the app does the creative work, and your photo supplies the face.

Colours, cuts and seasonal styles

Where Beautix earns its keep is the range. The colour changer covers the shades people actually ask for at the salon — red, dark brown, blonde, blue, silver and purple are all one tap away — and the results keep hair texture and shine rather than painting a flat block of colour over the photo. That matters: a colour preview only helps if it still looks like hair, and Beautix's examples do a convincing job of that.

Men are not an afterthought here, either. A dedicated men's section previews fades and short cuts on male photos, which many rival hair apps skip entirely. And the seasonal styles show the app is keeping up with trends: a Halloween style, for example, turns an ordinary portrait into a full pumpkin-queen costume look — fun for a party photo, and exactly the kind of shareable result that spreads on social media.

Pricing: read this before paying

Beautix is free to download, and Google Play lists it as containing ads. Play also lists in-app purchases at up to $89.99 per item, and a premium, no-ads upgrade is offered inside the app. Here is the honest part: the most helpful recent Play reviews are blunt about the free experience — one reviewer counts six ads before even adding a photo, more ads between every step, and a premium upsell at each turn, while another reports that free uses ran out after a single try and the app then demanded premium to continue. Several reviewers also say results did not always match what they picked.

That does not make Beautix useless — the app genuinely generates the hairstyle previews it promises, and its chart run shows plenty of people enjoy it. But it does mean the free tier is best treated as a quick demo, not a tool you can browse freely. Our advice: use the free tries first, judge the results on your own photo, and only then decide whether the premium upgrade is worth it. Never pay just to see one style.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Shows a new cut or colour on your own photo before a real salon visit — genuinely useful
  • Wide style library for women and men, including fades and seasonal looks
  • Colour previews keep hair texture and shine, so results look believable
  • Rated Everyone, simple one-photo workflow, no editing skill needed
  • Chart-topping growth: 2.3 million installs and climbing fast
Cons
  • Free tier is heavy on ads, with a premium upsell at almost every step
  • Free uses can run out after very few tries, per Play reviewers
  • Results can differ from the polished promo art — AI output varies
  • Large install (about 145 MB) for a single-purpose app
  • Only previews hair — no broader photo editing tools

Privacy and safety

One rule applies to every app in this category: only try styles on photos of yourself or people who have said yes. Beautix works on portraits, and its previews are only as kind as the photo you feed it — use your own selfies, and keep the seasonal costume fun to willing participants. The app is rated Everyone, so the content itself is family-safe, but the ad-heavy free tier means younger users will hit plenty of upsell screens, so it is best used with a grown-up nearby.

Who should download it?

If you are planning a haircut or colour change and want to see it first, Beautix is worth the free try — the preview idea is genuinely practical, and the style library is wide. If you want a full photo editor with filters and manual controls, look elsewhere: our Picsart review covers a full editor instead. And if you are tempted by the premium upgrade, run the free tier first — the ad complaints on Play are consistent enough to take seriously.

Bottom line: a clever, trending salon-preview idea with a style library that explains its chart run — just go in expecting ads, and keep your wallet closed until the free tries have convinced you.
