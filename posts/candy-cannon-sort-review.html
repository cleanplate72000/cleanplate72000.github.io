Candy Cannon Sort Review: Fling & Sort (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Candy Cannon Sort takes the familiar colour-sorting puzzle and fires it out of a cannon: aim, fling a candy ball into a glass tube, and when four of one colour land together the tube erupts in confetti. It is fast, bright and genuinely different from tube-swapping sorters — just expect ads, a hefty 130 MB download, and levels that get properly tricky.

Candy Cannon Sort, from developer Loyalty Gaming, launched on Google Play in October 2026 and has already passed 50,000+ installs on the store, with around 61,000 installs tracked in total, all in the last 30 days — roughly 2,800 installs a day. It holds 4.8 stars from about 3,600 ratings (4.79 in the latest AppBrain snapshot), and it is topping the new-arcade charts, reaching #1 Top New Free Arcade in Canada, France and the United Kingdom, #3 in the United States and #2 in Germany, Spain and Iran. For a game barely three weeks old, that is a remarkable start — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The twist is simple but it changes everything. In most sorting games you move balls from tube to tube. Here you do not move anything at all: you fire glossy candy balls from a cannon straight into glass tubes. Fill a tube with 4 balls of one colour and it erupts in a geyser of rainbow confetti and clears. That one change turns a calm sorting exercise into something with aim, timing and a satisfying payoff on almost every shot.

How it plays

You drag back and let go, or just tap a tube, to fling the loaded candy. A chute shows you the balls coming next, so you can plan ahead instead of firing blind. When a tube holds four of one colour, it erupts — and here is the clever part: every eruption shakes the wrong balls off the neighbouring tubes. Those stray balls fly home into a matching tube or back into your cannon, which can set off a chain all by itself. Clear every colour to win the level; run out of room and the level is over.

Good shots build into named combos — SWEET, GREAT, AMAZING and finally SUGAR RUSH — each one louder and bigger than the last, with rainbow confetti, candy rain and a LEVEL CLEAR party on every win. Most levels take under a minute, so it is a genuine one-more-level game: a quick round fits a bus stop, a queue or a coffee break, and the next level starts right away.

Why it is trending

Candy Cannon Sort is trending because it reads in a single screenshot and feels different in the first minute. Colour-sorting games are everywhere, but a game where you shoot the candy into place — with confetti eruptions, chain reactions and a cannon that feels like a toy — is instantly more playful than another tube-swapping clone. Players seem to agree: one five-star reviewer calls these games keepers after playing a lot of them, while another four-star reviewer says it is a little different from the other sorting games, runs quick and smooth, and would probably be loved by kids.

It also helps that the game respects short sessions and offline play. The goal is always visible, the controls need one finger, and the developer says the puzzles work offline, with no Wi-Fi needed. At version 1.2, updated 14 October 2026, it already offers over 1,000 levels across five candy worlds — Lollipop Lane, Gumdrop Grove, Caramel Canyon, Bubblegum Bay and Minty Peaks — so the early rush of new players has plenty to chew through.

Key features

  • Cannon-fired sorting — fire candy balls into tubes instead of moving balls from tube to tube.
  • Four-of-a-colour eruptions — fill a tube and it bursts in rainbow confetti and clears.
  • Chain reactions — eruptions shake stray balls off neighbouring tubes, setting off SWEET, GREAT, AMAZING and SUGAR RUSH combos.
  • Over 1,000 levels in five worlds — Lollipop Lane, Gumdrop Grove, Caramel Canyon, Bubblegum Bay and Minty Peaks.
  • Mystery Candy and Jawbreakers — purple "?" candies that only reveal their colour at the top, plus rainbow Jawbreakers.
  • Tricky levels and Sweet Shot bonuses — harder layouts plus bonus levels that pay triple coins.
  • Handy boosters — Undo, Hammer and Shuffle help you out of a tight spot.
  • Quick, one-finger, offline play — most levels take under a minute and need no Wi-Fi.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • A genuinely fresh twist on sorting — firing candy from a cannon, with confetti eruptions and chain reactions
  • Strong 4.8 stars from about 3,600 ratings, with all 61,000 tracked installs in the last 30 days
  • #1 Top New Free Arcade in Canada, France and the UK — real chart traction, not just a good trailer
  • Over 1,000 levels across five worlds, with mystery candies, Jawbreakers and bonus levels for variety
  • Free with no in-app purchases, offline play, and levels short enough for a coffee break
  • Everyone content rating, and one-finger controls anyone can pick up
Cons
  • Contains ads — one reviewer complains an ad can pop up mid-game instead of waiting until a hand finishes
  • About 130.6 MB — a large download for a puzzle game, worth knowing before you tap install
  • Sound effects divide opinion: one reviewer calls them too brassy and wants a softer looping melody
  • Still a young game: about 3,600 ratings is a solid start, but the average may move as more players arrive
  • One game type done very well, rather than a variety of modes — know what you are downloading

Pricing

Candy Cannon Sort is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. Importantly, the listing states there are no in-app purchases — some rewards are unlocked by watching an optional ad, but there is nothing to buy. The current version is 1.2, updated 14 October 2026, and the download is about 130.6 MB — large for a puzzle game, so grab it on Wi-Fi if you can.

Download

Download Candy Cannon Sort on Google Play

If you like a sorting puzzle that rewards aim as well as observation, Candy Cannon Sort is an easy recommendation: a fresh cannon-fired twist, a 4.8-star average, and a loop — aim, fling, erupt — that stays satisfying well past the first few worlds.
