Cardegory Review: Picture Sorting (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Cardegory: Picture Sort Tiles is a calm, genuinely clever sorting game: spot the tiles that belong together, drag them into the matching album, and clear the board before your moves run out. It is relaxing, offline-friendly and easy to pick up — just expect ads, a chunky 204 MB download, and a still-small rating base, so treat the star score as early days rather than settled opinion.

Cardegory: Picture Sort Tiles, from developer ZenEcho Studio, launched on Google Play in September 2026 and has already passed the 100,000+ installs bracket — with around 130,000 installs tracked in total, all in the last 30 days, including roughly 89,000 installs in the last 7 days alone. That pace put it at #70 in the Top Free Puzzle chart in the United States, with top-100 puzzle showings in Canada, India, Australia, Denmark, the Netherlands, Brazil and Norway. On Google Play it holds around 4.1 to 4.3 stars from under 100 ratings at the time of writing — a fast start, but a small sample, so the score could move a lot as more players weigh in. For a game barely a month old, that is exactly the kind of breakout worth reviewing this week.

The idea is refreshingly simple, and that is the whole appeal. There are no timers ticking down and no frantic swiping. Each level shows you a board of illustrated tiles — a cute cat, a red rocket, a slice of cake — and a row of albums at the top, each one asking for a category. You find the tiles that belong together, drag them into the matching album, and watch the album clear once it reaches its target count. Then a fresh album drops in, fresh tiles drop from above, and the puzzle keeps reshaping itself underneath you. The developer's own pitch sums it up well: “Spot, sort, and match tiles by category in this relaxing picture puzzle game!”

How it plays

Every level gives you a fixed number of moves — 60 moves in the early levels shown in the game's own screenshots, rising to 120 moves by level 28 — and you must clear every tile before those moves run out to win. That move limit is the real tension in the game. There is no clock, so you can stare at the board for as long as you like, but every drag costs a move, so careless sorting will run you dry. Hidden tiles sit face-down on the board and drop into play as you clear the tiles above them, which means the board you start with is never quite the board you finish with — a nice surprise that stops levels feeling solved on sight.

The categories themselves are the fun part. Early levels sort by simple, obvious groups — fruit, animals — but later levels ask you to sort waffles from popcorn from cupcakes, and then move on to people: clowns, kings and queens, each with their own hidden tiles to reveal. With over 6,000 illustrated tiles spanning animals, food, everyday objects and dozens of other themes, the game rarely repeats the same board twice. The hand-drawn art style helps too: tiles are bright, chunky and easy to tell apart at a glance, which matters when you are scanning a crowded board for one last matching tile. A cheerful cartoon dog keeps you company at the bottom of the screen, which gives the whole thing a gentle, family-friendly feel.

What makes it different

Plenty of sorting games ask you to stack coloured items into tubes or onto branches. Cardegory's twist is the album system: instead of clearing a pile, you are filling a category. That changes how you think. You are not just matching identical pictures — you are recognising what a picture is. Is that a fruit or a dessert? A waffle or a cupcake? The game rewards a sharp eye and a bit of general knowledge, not just fast fingers, which is why it works so well as a relaxing brain-training game rather than a reflex test.

The drop system is the other half of the appeal. Because new tiles drop in every time you clear an album, no level ever feels static. You might clear the fruit album only to find a fresh batch of animal tiles tumbling in behind it, changing which album you should tackle next. Combined with the move limit, that keeps the strategy live all the way through a level: do you finish the nearly-full album first, or clear a blocking tile to reveal what is hiding underneath? Small decisions, but satisfying ones.

Key features

  • Category sorting, not colour sorting: drag tiles into matching albums — fruit, animals, food, people and many more themes.
  • Move-limited levels: clear every tile before your moves run out; no timer, so you play entirely at your own pace.
  • Drop system: fresh tiles and fresh albums drop in as you clear matches, so every level keeps changing.
  • Hidden tiles: face-down tiles reveal themselves as the board clears, adding a satisfying reveal to later levels.
  • 6,000+ illustrated tiles: a large library of hand-drawn pictures across animals, food, everyday objects and more.
  • Fully offline: the game works without an internet connection, so it is a genuine pick-up-and-play option for travel or patchy signal.
  • Relaxing pace: short sessions, no pressure, and a cheerful art style that suits players of every age — the game carries an Everyone rating on Google Play.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Calm, clever sorting that rewards observation over speed
  • No timer — genuinely relaxing, play-at-your-own-pace design
  • Works fully offline
  • Huge tile library keeps levels feeling fresh
  • Everyone rating, family-friendly art and themes
  • Fast-growing: 100,000+ installs in its first month
Cons
  • Ads are the top complaint in player reviews, including ads appearing mid-play
  • One player reports level 25 feeling unwinnable without watching ads — attributed, not confirmed as a design rule
  • Some players would prefer tap-to-sort instead of dragging every tile
  • Chunky download at around 204 MB
  • Very small rating base (under 100 ratings), so the star score is still early days

Pricing

Cardegory is free to download and play on Google Play, with ads shown in the game. The Play listing shows a price of $0 and does not list a set of in-app purchase prices in the information we verified, so we are not quoting a purchase range here — if you play on a shared device, it is worth checking the store page for any offers before handing it to a child, since free games in this genre commonly sell extra moves or ad-removal options. The honest headline is this: the game itself costs nothing, but the ads are frequent enough that several reviewers mention them, so go in expecting interruptions between levels.

Download

Download Cardegory on Google Play

If you like a puzzle that rewards a sharp eye over fast fingers, Cardegory is an easy recommendation this week: a fast-growing debut, a relaxing offline loop, and a sorting twist that feels genuinely different from the usual tube-and-branch sorters. Just be ready for ads, and for a star rating that is still finding its level.
