Mini Relax Games Stress Relief Review (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Mini Relax Games Stress Relief is not one game but a whole pocket arcade: 25 quick mini games in a single free, Everyone-rated app, from calm solo puzzles to same-device multiplayer matches with a friend. It holds about 4.7 stars, has passed 20 million installs, and added roughly 890,000 installs in the last 30 days alone. The trade-offs are the usual ones for a free collection: it contains ads, and no single mini game is deep enough to carry the app on its own. As a break-time pick-and-mix, though, it is one of the easiest recommendations in the trending puzzle charts right now.

Mini Relax Games Stress Relief, from developer QY Studio, is a casual game collection built for short breaks, travel, waiting rooms and winding down at home. Open the app, pick a game card from its wooden menu, and you are playing within seconds — no long tutorials, no complicated settings, no difficult controls. The developer's own short description sums it up well: 25 fun mini games to relax and relieve stress anytime, with many of the main games playable offline. That combination of instant starts and offline play is a big part of why it keeps climbing: AppBrain ranks it around #24 in Top Free Puzzle in India, with top-100 puzzle placings in Saudi Arabia, Brazil, France, Italy, Mexico and several other countries.

The numbers back up the chart run. Google Play shows a 10,000,000+ installs bracket, while AppBrain's tracking puts total installs at about 20 million, including roughly 890,000 installs in the last 30 days — around 30,000 a day — and about 350,000 installs in the last 7 days. The live Play listing shows about 4.6 stars from nearly 15,700 ratings, and AppBrain's snapshot puts it at 4.65 stars from about 13,600 ratings. It has been on Google Play since July 2025, the current version is 1.37.37, updated on 28 September 2026, and it is a modest 77.5 MB download. Play rates it Everyone, and it is free to download with ads.

Playing solo: calm puzzles, one card at a time

The solo section is where most players will spend their time, and it focuses on quick, satisfying puzzle gameplay. Draw a safe line, erase the right part of a picture, move pins in the correct order, guide balls through tracks, slide blocks to open a path, connect points, sort colours, complete pictures, solve number boards and arrange grid blocks. Each mode has its own rules and goals, so every session feels a little different, and the controls stay simple throughout: tap, drag, draw, slide, aim, erase, release or react at the right moment.

That simplicity is the point. Some levels are relaxing and simple, some are funny and unexpected, and some need a little more thinking before you make your next move — but none of them demands a long learning curve. If you only have five minutes, you can finish a level or two and put the phone down feeling like you actually completed something. If you have longer, you can hop between modes whenever one starts to feel stale, which is exactly how a collection like this avoids the boredom that kills single-mechanic puzzle games.

Playing together: one phone, two players

The local multiplayer section turns your phone into a small party game table. Two players share the same screen and compete in quick rounds with simple touch controls: flying challenges, tap-and-react games, tank-style duels, coin collecting, tabletop ball games, pinball-style action, aiming challenges, running moments, tug-of-war and arena battles. There is no online matchmaking to wait for and no second device to set up — hand the phone over, or rather share it, and the match starts.

This is the feature that sets the app apart from most stress-relief collections, which are usually solo-only. It makes the app a decent option for families on a journey or friends killing time together, and it gives the collection a second life once the solo puzzles start to feel familiar. The rounds are deliberately short, so nobody is stuck holding the phone for long, and the cartoon art and lighthearted scenes keep the competition friendly rather than intense.

Key features

  • 25 mini games in one app — puzzles, action, logic, drawing, physics, blocks, balls, colours and reflex challenges, all from one menu.
  • Solo and local multiplayer — calm puzzle breaks alone, or quick same-device matches with a friend or family member.
  • Short rounds — built for breaks, waiting time, travel and relaxing moments before bed.
  • Simple touch controls — tap, drag, draw, slide, aim and react, with no long tutorials.
  • Offline play — many of the main mini games can be enjoyed without Wi-Fi.
  • Cartoon art and clear goals — every mode is easy to understand just by looking at the screen.

Pros and cons

Pros: huge variety in one small download; genuinely quick to start; offline-friendly; the same-device multiplayer is a rare and welcome extra; strong 4.7-star average across tens of thousands of installs a day.

Cons: it contains ads, which will interrupt longer sessions; no single mini game is deep, so players looking for one game to master will tire of the variety; a few players report an occasional level not registering as solved, so progress in a tricky level can occasionally feel unfair; and a couple of the cartoon scenarios are slapstick thief-style puzzles, so parents of very young children may want a quick look first despite the Everyone rating.

Pricing

Mini Relax Games Stress Relief is free to download on Google Play and contains ads. That is the deal: dozens of mini games and the multiplayer modes cost nothing up front, and advertising pays for them. Players who hate ad breaks should expect them here, especially between rounds, but there is no paywall standing between you and the games themselves.

Download

Download Mini Relax Games Stress Relief on Google Play

If your idea of relaxing is ten different tiny challenges rather than one long game, Mini Relax Games Stress Relief is an easy recommendation: it is small, free, offline-friendly and genuinely varied, and its 4.7-star average is earned by the breadth of the collection rather than a single hit mode. Go in expecting ads and snack-sized games rather than a deep adventure, and you will find one of the most useful break-time apps trending on Google Play right now.
