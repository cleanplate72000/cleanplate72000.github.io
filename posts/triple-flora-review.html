Triple Flora Review: Blooming Puzzles (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Triple Flora is a genuinely calming triple-match puzzler: beautiful flower tiles, layered boards that reward planning, and helpful boosters for tight spots. Its 4.9-star average comes from a small early audience, and the 162 MB download is chunky for a puzzle game, but if you want a relaxing daily puzzle, this one blooms nicely.

Triple Flora: Match Puzzle, from developer SecondWonder Games, arrived on Google Play in September 2026 and has already reached the 260,000-install mark tracked by AppBrain, with 150,000 of those in the last 30 days — roughly 5,100 new players a day. Its Play rating stands at 4.9 stars, although that average currently rests on only around 117 ratings, so treat it as an enthusiastic early verdict rather than a settled one. The current version is 1.1.1, updated 22 October 2026, the app is about 162 MB, and Google Play rates its content Everyone. It sits in the Puzzle category, and it has already climbed the Top New Free puzzle charts in several countries, reaching #4 in Italy and the top 10 in Japan, Brazil, Russia and South Korea.

The pitch is right there in the developer's tagline: find your calm in beautiful flower tiles and satisfying triple-match puzzles. There is no timer breathing down your neck and no complicated meta-game to learn first — just a board of flower tiles, a tray with limited space, and the quiet satisfaction of clearing three of a kind. That simplicity is exactly why the triple-match genre keeps producing hits, and Triple Flora dresses it up in the prettiest theme of the bunch.

How it works

The rules take about ten seconds to learn. Tap a flower tile to place it in the tray at the top of the screen, collect three identical tiles to match and remove them, and clear every tile to complete the level. The catch — and the whole strategy of the game — is that the tray has limited space, and some tiles are hidden beneath others. Tap in the wrong order and the tray fills up before you can finish; plan ahead and a buried lotus becomes the key to the whole board.

The flower theme is more than decoration. The boards are filled with roses, lilies, lotus flowers, blue blossoms and more, each one clearly drawn so you can tell tiles apart at a glance — important when a board is stacked three layers deep. The soft colours and gentle art turn every level into a calming little escape, which is a real contrast to the loud, flashing puzzlers that dominate the charts. Early reviewers agree: one calls it "absolutely nice and good to play and full of enjoy", while another writes "Love, love, love it. Keep those levels coming."

Why it is trending

Triple Flora is trending because it launched at exactly the right moment and into exactly the right niche. Relaxing puzzle games are perennial chart climbers, and a brand-new one with a fresh floral look, a 4.9-star early rating and 150,000 installs in a single month stands out in a crowded puzzle category. Its chart run tells the story: top 10 in the Top New Free puzzle lists in Italy, Japan, Brazil, Russia and South Korea within weeks of launch, plus a steady 5,000-plus installs a day.

The other reason is restraint. Triple Flora does not try to be ten games at once. There is no sprawling base-building mode, no forced social features, no endless pop-ups — just the board, the tray and the next level. For players who open a puzzle game to unwind for a few minutes, that focus is the feature. The progression is satisfying without being punishing: new arrangements appear as you advance, layouts get cleverer, and the layered boards keep asking you to think one move further ahead.

Boosters when you get stuck

Every triple-match player eventually paints themselves into a corner, and Triple Flora gives you three ways out. The booster bar at the bottom of the screen offers an undo to take back a bad tap, a shuffle to rearrange the board when nothing lines up, and a special tool for the really stubborn spots. The screenshots show each booster with a small counter, so you know exactly how many you have left before you spend one — a small honesty that many rivals skip.

Used well, the boosters change how you play. Undo lets you experiment with a risky move instead of playing it safe every time; shuffle rescues a board that looks dead. The screenshots from levels 10, 30 and 50 show the same clean layout each time — tray on top, boosters below, board in the middle — so once you have learned the game, you can focus entirely on the flowers.

Key features

  • Triple-match gameplay — tap tiles, collect three of a kind, clear the board.
  • A garden of flower tiles — roses, lilies, lotus flowers, blue blossoms and more, all easy to tell apart.
  • Layered puzzle boards — tiles hidden beneath others reward careful planning over fast tapping.
  • Easy to learn, rewarding to master — simple controls meet layouts that get cleverer as you advance.
  • Relaxing flower theme — soft colours and charming floral art, with no timer pressure.
  • Helpful boosters — undo a move, shuffle the board, or use a special tool when you need a hand.
  • Satisfying progression — new arrangements and sharper strategy as you climb through the levels.
  • Everyone rating — a family-safe puzzle with no age-gated content.

Pricing

Triple Flora is free to download on Google Play. The Play page checked for this review shows no in-app purchase price range and no contains-ads label, so treat the free download as the only pricing the listing itself confirms — check any paid offer inside the app on its own merits before accepting it. For a puzzle game this polished, the free entry point makes it easy to try a few levels before deciding whether it earns a permanent spot on your home screen.
