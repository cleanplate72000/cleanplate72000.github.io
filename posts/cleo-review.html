Cleo Review: Push-Stone Puzzles (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Cleo: Logic Puzzle Adventure is October 2026's quiet puzzle breakout: a push-stone logic game starring a hungry little mouse, with a 4.8-star rating, an Everyone content rating, and about 63,000 installs in its first weeks — all of them in the last 30 days. You push stones, but you can never pull them, so every move is a commitment, and one wrong shove can trap the cheese behind a stone you can no longer move. It is gentle, clever and genuinely relaxing — with two honest caveats: the tap-and-drag controls frustrate some players, and the game does contain ads.

Cleo: Logic Puzzle Adventure, from German developer AsgardSoft GmbH, is built around one of the oldest puzzle ideas in games — the push-block puzzle — dressed up in its friendliest possible form. Cleo the mouse wants cheese. Stones and traps stand between her and it. You swipe to move Cleo around a grid, push stones into traps to clear a path, and collect every piece of cheese to unlock the next level. The catch that makes the whole game work: you can push a stone, but you cannot pull it back. Push the wrong stone into the wrong corner and the level is unwinnable, so you restart and think harder. It is Sokoban with a storybook coat on, and it is exactly as satisfying as that sounds.

On its official Google Play listing it holds 4.8 stars from 218 ratings, with Play showing 50,000+ installs; independent tracker AppBrain puts the total higher, at about 63,000 installs, all of them in the last 30 days — a genuinely new breakout, not a slow burner. It has an Everyone content rating, is free to download, and was last updated on 14 October 2026 with a new campaign of 99 puzzles across nine worlds. It is also climbing the Top New Free Puzzle charts in several countries, which is how it landed on our radar this week.

The core loop in one picture: Cleo (top right) must reach the cheese, but the stones can only be pushed, never pulled — so the order you move them in is the whole puzzle.

Key features

  • 99 puzzles across nine worlds: the campaign runs from the forest through harbors, islands and castles all the way to the underworld, with detailed scenery in each world.
  • Push, never pull: stones move only when pushed, so every move counts and every level is a small logic problem, not a reflex test.
  • Simple swipe controls: no buttons to learn — swipe to move Cleo, and the game handles the rest, making it easy to pick up for any age.
  • Stones, traps and paths: push stones into traps to neutralise them, clear a route, and collect every piece of cheese before the level counts as solved.
  • Hint system: players report a light-bulb hint that previews the solution step by step, with pause and replay, while keeping your progress intact — a lifeline when a level has you stuck.
  • Relaxing pace: there are no timers and no lives ticking away, so you can stare at a level for as long as you like before committing to a move.

Progress is a winding path through each world, with levels marked as little wedges of cheese. Locked levels stay locked until you finish the one before — no skipping ahead.

Who is it for?

This is a game for players who like thinking more than tapping. If you enjoy puzzle games where the answer is a clever sequence rather than fast fingers — and if you want something calm to play in short bursts — Cleo fits beautifully, and it pairs naturally with our Maze Out review if you want another brain-teaser with a different twist. Parents will like the Everyone rating and the absence of any scary or violent content: the worst thing that happens is a mouse missing out on cheese. The honest caveats come from the players themselves. One reviewer praises the graphics but complains that moving Cleo means tapping and dragging on every block, over and over, which makes the game feel more like a tapping exercise than a puzzle; another says the mouse moves slowly and needs constant pressing. If you dislike fiddly touch controls, that is worth knowing before you install. The game also contains ads, and its data-safety section notes that collected data cannot be deleted — a small privacy footnote worth a mention.

Later worlds change the scenery but not the rules: the farm world adds sunflowers, crops and more stones, and the same push-only logic decides whether the cheese is reachable.

Pros
  • A genuinely clever rule — push only, never pull — that makes every level a real logic puzzle
  • Excellent launch: 4.8 stars, about 63,000 installs in weeks, Everyone rating
  • 99 puzzles across nine worlds, from forest to underworld, with no timers or pressure
  • Simple swipe controls and a step-by-step hint system when you get stuck
  • Charming, family-safe presentation with no violence or scary content
Cons
  • Tap-and-drag controls frustrate some players, with slow mouse movement a repeated complaint
  • Contains ads, and in-app purchases are listed on the store page
  • Small rating base (218 ratings) — the 4.8 score could move as more players arrive
  • Data safety section says collected data cannot be deleted
  • One wrong push can make a level unwinnable, which will annoy impatient players

Pricing

Cleo: Logic Puzzle Adventure is free to download on Google Play. The listing states that it contains ads and offers in-app purchases, although no price range is shown on the store page at the time of writing — so treat any purchase prompt inside the game with the usual caution and check the price before confirming. The full 99-puzzle campaign is the game itself, not a demo, so the free version is a complete puzzle game with ads as the trade-off.

Download Cleo on Google Play

The bottom line: judged on what it actually is — a gentle, well-made push-stone puzzler with a memorable rule, a charming heroine and breakout momentum in its first month — Cleo earns its October 2026 buzz. Its rating is strong, its growth is fast, and its Everyone rating makes it an easy recommendation for puzzle fans of any age. Just go in knowing the controls are tap-heavy and the ads are part of the deal, and you will find one of the most relaxing new puzzle games on Play right now.
