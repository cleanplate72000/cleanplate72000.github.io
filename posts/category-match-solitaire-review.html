Category Match Solitaire Review: New (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Category Match Solitaire is a genuinely fresh twist on a card puzzle: instead of suits and numbers, you group cards by topic — Halloween, Food, Japan, Chess — to clear the board and reveal what is hidden underneath. The idea is easy to grasp, the boards are colourful, and it is free to try. But keep expectations in check: this is a very new game with only a few dozen ratings so far, some players report repeated cards and ads for extra space, and at around 290 MB it is a surprisingly large download for a puzzle game.

Category Match Solitaire, from developer kiwilab, is one of the fastest-climbing new puzzle games on Google Play this week. It launched on Google Play in September 2026, was updated most recently on 23 October 2026, and has already passed 50,000+ installs — all of them, according to AppBrain's tracking, in its first month. It reached the top 10 in the free puzzle charts in Poland and ranks in the top free puzzle lists in Belgium and Austria as well. That kind of start for a brand-new puzzle game is exactly why it earns a review here, even though its rating history is still very short.

The core idea is simple, and that is the point. You are shown a board of face-up cards, each carrying a picture, plus a set of face-down cards waiting underneath. Your job is to group together the cards that belong to the same topic — all the Halloween cards together, all the Food cards together, and so on — and clear them off the board. Every card you clear uncovers a hidden card beneath it, so each move changes what you can see and what you can do next. The game describes this as planning ahead, because a careless group can bury the very card you needed. It is solitaire in spirit — one player, one board, one puzzle to solve — but the matching rule is about meaning, not numbers, which makes it feel different from the card games most of us already know.

Level 3 in action: Ghost, Halloween and Autumn are three separate groups on one board, and the move counter sits at 106.

How it plays

Controls are deliberately simple: tap, swipe or drag to move the cards. There is no complicated tutorial to wade through, and the first levels teach the rule by showing it — group the matching topic, watch the board clear, watch new cards appear. A move counter at the top of the screen sets the pressure: Level 1 gives you 50 moves, Level 3 gives 106, and later levels tighten up again, with Level 15 in the screenshots down to a single move left and a warning on screen. That mix of a relaxed idea and a limited move budget is what gives the game its tension. You can take your time thinking, but you cannot waste moves forever.

The themes are a big part of the appeal. The screenshots show boards built around Halloween, Autumn, Food, Furniture, Animals, Drink, Japan, Seafood, Shapes, Emoji, Capybara and Chess, and the developer promises a variety of fun themes to collect. For a sorting game, that variety matters: recognising a topic at a glance is the whole skill, and a board full of capybaras, chess pieces and flowers is simply more inviting than a board full of identical symbols. The game is pitched as good for both quick breaks and longer sessions, and that rings true — a level is short enough to finish while waiting, but the move limit means you do have to concentrate.

Level 1 keeps it gentle: Food, Furniture and Animals, with 50 moves to learn the grouping rule.

Key features

  • Topic-matching card puzzles — group cards by theme, not by suit or number.
  • Hidden cards revealed as you clear the board, so every move changes the puzzle.
  • Simple tap, swipe and drag controls with no complicated setup.
  • A wide range of colourful card themes, from Halloween and Japan to Capybara and Chess.
  • A move counter on every level, so planning ahead matters as much as speed.
  • Short, self-contained levels that suit both quick breaks and longer play sessions.

Level 6 widens the net: Drink, Japan and Seafood on one board, with a magnifier highlighting a lucky cat card.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely different matching rule that is easy to learn; bright, varied themed boards; simple controls; free to download and try; a fast-growing new game with a recent update (23 October 2026).

Cons: a very small rating sample, so the score could move a lot; players report cards repeating across levels and ads being needed for an extra category space; a large download at around 290 MB for a puzzle game; and the Play listing's data-safety section notes that data is not encrypted and cannot be deleted, which privacy-conscious players should know before installing.

Pricing and safety

Category Match Solitaire is free to download on Google Play, with in-app purchases listed on its Play page; the Play page checked for this review does not list specific purchase prices, so do not take any price quoted elsewhere at face value. It carries an Everyone content rating, making it a family-safe pick in content terms, and it contains no gambling, adult or crypto-mining content. One honest flag: the Play listing's data-safety information says the app may collect location and device identifiers, that data is not encrypted, and that data cannot be deleted. That is the developer's own disclosure on the store page, and it is worth weighing alongside the fun — especially on a shared or family device.

On ratings, be careful with the numbers. Google Play shows around 3.75 stars from 52 ratings at the time of checking, while AppBrain's snapshot shows 3.57 stars from 36 ratings — both agree on the important point, which is that the sample is tiny. A handful of new reviews could swing the score either way, so treat the current rating as an early impression, not a settled verdict. The early reviews that are visible include a player who enjoyed the game until the same cards kept repeating on every level, and another who uninstalled after being asked to watch two full ads for an extra category space. Those are individual reports, not a consensus, but they match the two things a new player is most likely to notice: repetition at higher levels, and ads around extra space.

Level 15 turns up the pressure: Shapes, Emoji, Capybara and Chess, with just one move left on the counter.

Category Match Solitaire is worth a try if you like sorting and matching games and want something that is not just another colour-sort clone. The topic-matching rule is clever, the themed boards are charming, and a new game climbing the puzzle charts in several countries is usually doing something right. Just go in with open eyes: it is early days, the download is large, repetition and ads for extra space are the most common complaints so far, and the data-safety disclosure is weaker than many players would like.
