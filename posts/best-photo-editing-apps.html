10 Best Free Photo Editing Apps for Android (2026)

Quick verdict

Short on time? Picsart is the best all-rounder for most people, Adobe Lightroom wins for serious colour work, and Snapseed is the best completely free editor with no fuss. Every download button below goes to Google Play only — we never link APK files.

Looking for the best free photo editing apps for Android in 2026? We tested the obvious names and the quiet achievers, and these ten earned their place — for quick social edits, AI touch-ups, film looks, and proper colour grading. All ten are free to start; where an app has a paid tier, we say so.

1. Picsart Photo Editor — best all-rounder

Picsart packs photo editing, AI background tools, stickers, collage, and drawing into one app. It is the editor our own creator work runs on, and its free tier is genuinely usable for everyday edits.

Pros

  • Huge all-in-one toolkit — no second app needed
  • Strong AI tools: background changer, object remover
  • Massive template and sticker library for social posts

Cons

  • Best AI features sit behind the paid Gold tier
  • Home screen pushes premium offers often

Download on Google Play

2. Canva — best for designs and social posts

Canva is less a pure photo editor and more a design studio: edit the photo, then drop it straight into a post, story, or thumbnail template sized correctly for every platform.

Pros

  • Templates for every social platform
  • Beginner-friendly drag-and-drop
  • Good free tier

Cons

  • Photo retouching is shallower than dedicated editors
  • Some elements are Pro-only

Download on Google Play

3. Adobe Lightroom — best for serious colour

Lightroom's mobile editor brings desktop-grade colour grading, curves, and masking to your phone. If your photos matter more than your captions, start here.

Pros

  • Best-in-class colour and light controls
  • Presets sync the look across photos
  • Excellent RAW support

Cons

  • Best tools need an Adobe subscription
  • Learning curve for beginners

Download on Google Play

4. Snapseed — best fully free editor

Google's Snapseed is precise, powerful, and completely free — selective edits, healing, HDR, and perspective tools with no ads and no subscription nagging.

Pros

  • Completely free, no ads
  • Precise selective and brush edits
  • Clean, fast interface

Cons

  • No built-in collage or design templates
  • Interface updates are infrequent

Download on Google Play

5. CapCut — best for photo-to-video social content

CapCut started as a video editor, and its photo tools plus auto-templates make it the fastest route from a still photo to a trending reel or TikTok.

Pros

  • Brilliant templates and auto-captions for video
  • Photo editor included
  • Free core features

Cons

  • Overkill if you only edit stills
  • Account prompts for some features

Download on Google Play

6. VSCO — best for film looks

VSCO is famous for soft, film-like filters that make feeds look curated rather than filtered. A quieter, more aesthetic editing experience.

Pros

  • Beautiful film-style presets
  • Calm, minimal interface
  • Strong photo community

Cons

  • Best presets are membership-only
  • Fewer stickers/text tools

Download on Google Play

7. InShot — best for quick social edits

InShot trims, crops, filters, and captions photos and short videos in minutes. It is the 'get it posted' editor.

Pros

  • Very fast for stories and reels
  • Good text and sticker tools
  • Simple for beginners

Cons

  • Free version adds a watermark on some exports
  • Ads in the free tier

Download on Google Play

8. Remini — best AI photo enhancer

Remini's AI sharpens blurry, old, or low-resolution photos in one tap. It will not fix everything, but on faces the results can be startling.

Pros

  • One-tap AI enhancement
  • Great on old family photos
  • Before/after preview

Cons

  • Full use needs a subscription or watching ads
  • Results vary — check faces closely

Download on Google Play

9. Google Photos — best built-in editor

Already on most Android phones, Google Photos quietly became a solid editor: Magic Editor-style suggestions, portrait light, and unblur on supported devices, plus backup built in.

Pros

  • Already installed — nothing to download
  • Backup and editing in one place
  • Smart one-tap suggestions

Cons

  • Advanced tools vary by device and subscription
  • Less creative control than dedicated editors

Download on Google Play

10. Pixlr — best lightweight editor

Pixlr is the fast, light option: overlays, effects, collage, and double-exposure tools without a heavy app or account wall.

Pros

  • Fast and light on storage
  • Fun overlays and effects
  • Works well on older phones

Cons

  • Ads in the free tier
  • Premium assets behind a paywall

Download on Google Play

Which one should you pick?

  • One app for everything: Picsart
  • Posts, stories, thumbnails: Canva
  • Serious photos: Adobe Lightroom
  • Free, no ads, no account: Snapseed
  • Fixing old photos: Remini
