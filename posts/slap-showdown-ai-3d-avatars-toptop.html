Slap Showdown Brings AI 3D Avatars to TopTop

Newborn Town's game-oriented social platform TopTop has launched Slap Showdown, an AI-powered 3D interactive mini-game built with Viggle, which brings world-model technology into social gaming for the first time on the platform.

Why this matters: Instead of picking a preset avatar, players turn a photo from their phone into a personalised 3D character and take it straight into multiplayer slap battles — a small, playful test of whether AI-generated 3D characters can work as real gameplay, not just decoration.

Slap Showdown: the key facts

  • Game: Slap Showdown, an AI-powered 3D interactive mini-game on TopTop, Newborn Town's game-oriented social platform.
  • Built with: Viggle's self-developed Joint Space-Time (JST) world model, which generates 3D characters, motion and environments directly.
  • How it works: upload a photo from your phone and generate a personalised 3D character in around 30 seconds — an anime protagonist, a cyberpunk cyborg, a LEGO-style minifigure, or something else entirely.
  • Gameplay: take your custom character into multiplayer battles against other players, using press-and-hold, tap and drag controls, with characters responding with physics-based movements.
  • Launched: in September 2026, and now available in multiple countries and regions worldwide, with particular traction in Japan, among Spanish-speaking users in North America, and across the Middle East.
  • Early engagement: TopTop's operations team reports a 97% match completion rate in the game's first month, with a game-start rate above the platform-wide average.
  • The tech behind it: Viggle launched JST-2 in full on October 19, 2026, a physics-native model that represents 3D scenes with units resembling digital atoms, each with position, material, colour, mass and movement data — and can generate a 3D scene in seconds at an inference cost of under one US cent.
  • What is next: TopTop says future releases could use AI-powered 3D generation beyond avatars, including customised environments, and Viggle is already working on a next-generation JST-3 model intended as a comprehensive world simulator.

Why it matters

Most AI character tools stop at a picture. Slap Showdown's point is different: the generated character has to move, react and take a hit in a live multiplayer match. If the physics feel right, the same technology could spread from avatars to whole custom environments — and from one mini-game to the rest of TopTop's social platform. The early numbers suggest players are finishing matches rather than bouncing off, which is the first test any social game has to pass.

What to watch next

Watch whether TopTop extends the same 3D generation to other games and to custom environments, as its operations team has suggested, and whether JST-3 arrives as the fuller world simulator Viggle describes. Because this is a social platform with photo uploads and multiplayer battles, younger players should check TopTop's store age rating first.
