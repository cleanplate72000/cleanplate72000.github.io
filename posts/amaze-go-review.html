Amaze GO! Review: Calm Arrow Puzzles (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Amaze GO! is a calm, clever arrow puzzle that turns a simple idea — slide every arrow off the board in the right order — into a genuinely relaxing brain workout. It is free, works offline, is rated Everyone, and is one of the fastest-growing puzzle games on Google Play right now. The ads are the main downside: they arrive often, and some players report them as the one thing that breaks the calm.

Amaze GO!, from developer Oakever Games, is having a remarkable year. It holds 4.8 stars from about 433,500 ratings, has passed 100 million installs on Google Play, and roughly 15 million of those installs came in the last 30 days — around 500,000 a day. It launched in February 2026 and has already reached #1 in Top Free Games in the Czech Republic, Denmark and Norway, and #2 in the United Kingdom, Australia and several other countries. It is also available on the App Store, where it scores 4.7 stars. With an Everyone content rating, a free download and offline play, it is an easy recommendation for anyone who wants a puzzle that calms rather than shouts — and that sharp recent surge is why it earns a review here this week.

The idea is deliberately simple. Every board is a grid of arrows, each pointing in one direction. Your job is to work out the order: find an arrow with a clear path to the edge of the board, tap it, and watch it slide off. That move opens a lane for the next arrow, and so on, until the board is empty. It sounds trivial until you try it — tap the wrong arrow first and its path is blocked, so you have to think two or three moves ahead, like a quiet game of chess played one arrow at a time.

Amaze GO! in its title art: red and blue arrows against a maze pattern — the whole game in one image.

How Amaze GO! plays

Each level starts with a full board and a small set of hearts, shown as drops at the top of the screen. Tap an arrow with a free route to the edge and it flies off the grid with a satisfying whoosh — the developer describes that release as ASMR-like, and it really is the hook of the game. A wrong tap costs a heart for that level, so careless tapping is punished gently but firmly. If a board has you stuck, a long-press brings up Guidance, which highlights directions to help you find the next safe move, and hints are there for the tightest grids. Clear the whole board and the level is done; clear it without losing a heart and you have earned a perfect run.

The boards themselves are part of the charm. They are not plain grids: the winding arrow paths form pictures. Level 10, for example, is shaped like a trophy, Level 27 like an anchor, and Level 40 like a dog, so every few levels you get a small visual surprise on top of the puzzle itself. The screenshots also carry the developer's own taglines — “Eye Comfort” and “Tap to Clear” — and one advertises “10,000+ levels”, so this is not a game you will finish in a weekend.

What makes it good

First, the calm is real. The design cuts visual noise: pale backgrounds, clean arrows, no flashing banners or shouting characters. The developer pitches it as a puzzle for “the ten minutes before a meeting starts, the metro ride too short for a podcast, the flight where your signal drops” — and because it works offline, that pitch actually holds up. You can play it on a plane, in a dead zone, or anywhere you would rather not burn mobile data.

Second, the difficulty curve is honest. Early boards teach the rule in seconds — find the free arrow, tap it, repeat — and later boards demand real sequencing: you must spot which arrow unblocks which, and hold the tempting move until its path is clear. It is a cognitive workout in the best sense, sharpening planning and focus without a timer breathing down your neck. There is no countdown forcing you to rush, which is rare in a genre full of ticking clocks.

Third, the controls are exactly as simple as they should be. Tap to move, long-press for Guidance, and two buttons at the bottom of the screen for the grid view and hints. Nothing else gets in the way. If you like puzzle games that respect your attention, this is the opposite of the noisy, ad-stuffed end of the genre — at least while you are actually playing.

The honest downsides

Pros

  • Simple to learn, genuinely tricky to master — real sequencing puzzles, not reflex tests
  • Fully playable offline, with a calm, low-noise design
  • Boards shaped like pictures (trophy, anchor, dog) keep levels visually fresh
  • Free, rated Everyone, and runs on Android 7.1 and higher
  • Guidance and hints when a board feels impossible

Cons

  • Contains ads, and some players report them as frequent and hard to skip
  • A wrong tap costs a heart, which can stall a level you were enjoying
  • The single mechanic can feel repetitive over very long sessions
  • No multiplayer or social features — this is a solo, quiet game

The ads deserve a straight word. Google Play lists Amaze GO! as containing ads, and player reviews back that up: several recent reviewers praise the puzzles but complain about ads after tasks, with one dropping a five-star review to one star over them. If you play in short bursts, they are a minor tax; if you binge for an hour, they will interrupt the calm the game works so hard to build. There is no subscription or paywall to dodge them that we could verify — the store data lists no in-app purchases — so going in with that expectation is the honest way to play.

Repetition is the other caveat. The core loop never really changes: it is always arrows, order, and a clear board. That is a strength for a wind-down game and a weakness for anyone who wants variety, story, or progression beyond new board shapes and harder grids. A small number of long-term players also say the daily grind eventually feels samey after hundreds of levels. Treat it as a ten-minute puzzle, not a main game, and it stays fresh much longer.

Pricing and practical details

Amaze GO! is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. No in-app purchases are listed in its store data, which keeps the deal simple: download it, play it, and put up with the ads. It needs Android 7.1 or higher, and because it works offline, it is a good pick for older phones and patchy connections alike. Google Play rates it Everyone, and its data-safety section says data is encrypted in transit and deletion can be requested.
