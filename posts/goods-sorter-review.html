Goods Sorter Review: Shelf Sorting (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Goods Sorter is a satisfying shelf-tidying puzzle with a simple idea done well: drag groceries onto matching shelves and clear the board one group at a time. It is free, Everyone-rated, and already past 100,000 installs in its first six weeks — but fiddly drag placement and frequent ads are real frustrations that keep it from a higher score.

Goods Sorter, from developer Gimica GmbH, has one of the clearest pitches on Google Play: "The shelves are full of mixed-up groceries. Can you put everything in the right place?" There is no story to follow and no reflex test to pass. Each level shows you a set of wooden shelves stacked with mixed groceries — milk cartons, cola cans, sugar bags, jam jars, avocados — and your job is to move products between shelves until identical items sit together, at which point they clear. It is the digital equivalent of tidying a pantry, and for players who like order, it is quietly addictive.

The numbers show a genuine breakout, not a slow burn. Goods Sorter has been on Google Play only since September 2026, yet it already carries 100,000+ installs on Play — AppBrain puts the total at about 120,000 installs, with roughly 99,000 of them in the last 30 days — and its current trending run includes about 45,000 installs in seven days, three times the week before. It ranks #67 in Top Free Casual in the United States and #54 in Canada on AppBrain's charts. The rating is solid: about 4.4 stars from roughly 6,800 ratings on Google Play. The app is about 122 MB at version 1.1.0, updated 30 September 2026, and it carries an Everyone content rating.

So how does a level actually work? You drag a grocery item from one shelf to another, aiming to group identical products together. The catch, and the whole puzzle, is space: shelves fill up, and moving the wrong item can block the very group you were building. The official description puts it plainly — "consider what each space will open before making your next move" — and that advice matters more with every level, as shelf layouts get more crowded and the number of product types grows. A careless early move can leave you shuffling the same jar back and forth while the board stays stubbornly full.

When you do get stuck, three tools can bail you out: a hint that reveals a move, a freeze that pauses the timer, and a shuffle that rearranges the remaining items. These are the difference between a relaxing wind-down game and a frustrating dead end, and learning when to spend them — rather than burning a hint on the first tricky board — is part of getting good at Goods Sorter. Levels are short enough for a quick session, and the game is designed to be played at your own pace, so it fits both a two-minute wait and a longer evening session.

Key features

  • Drag-and-drop shelf sorting. Move groceries between shelves with simple drag controls — easy to learn in seconds, with real planning needed as boards get crowded.
  • Match identical goods to clear them. Group the same products together and they clear from the board, one group at a time, until the shelves are empty.
  • Varied shelf arrangements. Different layouts and product mixes keep levels fresh — a tactic that clears one board will not automatically work on the next.
  • Hint, freeze and shuffle tools. Reveal a move when you are stuck, pause the timer when you need to think, or shuffle the remaining items for a fresh start.
  • Bright, grocery-themed visuals. Colourful, chunky product art — milk, jam, snacks, soda, avocados — makes it easy to spot matches at a glance.
  • Everyone-rated and free. Free to download on Google Play with an Everyone content rating, and the core sorting loop is playable without paying.

Pros and cons

What works: the core idea is genuinely satisfying — there is a small, real pleasure in watching a chaotic shelf resolve into neat rows of matching goods, and the game understands that, keeping levels short and the feedback immediate. The difficulty curve is fair: early boards teach the drag-and-match loop, later boards demand actual planning around free spaces. The presentation helps too: bright, readable grocery art means you are never squinting to tell products apart, and the Everyone rating plus free download make it an easy recommendation for all ages. And the growth figures back it up — 120,000 installs in six weeks with a 4.4-star average is a real breakout, not a flash in the pan.

What to watch: first, and most importantly, placement can be fiddly. This is the single most repeated complaint in player reviews: items sometimes refuse to sit on a shelf unless dropped in exactly the right spot, snapping back to where they started, and you must wait for one item to settle before moving another. On timed boards that precision requirement turns a relaxing puzzle into a frustrating one, so expect some retries. Second, ads: the listing confirms the game contains ads, and reviewers report them before, after and sometimes within levels — play in short bursts and you will meet them often. Third, a few players report sluggish movement and slow load times on older phones, so a 122 MB game on a low-end device may stutter. None of this breaks the game, but it is worth knowing before you install.

Pricing

Goods Sorter is free to download on Google Play, and the listing confirms it contains ads. The Play data we verified for this review did not show an in-app purchase price range, so we are not quoting one here — check the Play page's current details before spending anything in-game. The core sorting loop, all three helper tools in their basic form, and the full level rotation are playable without paying; ads are the main cost of the free version, and the helper tools are the thing most players will be tempted to spend on.

If you like sorting puzzles with a similar tidy-up satisfaction, our BlockFlow review covers another October 2026 breakout built around matching and clearing boards. BlockFlow is the faster, more arcade-flavoured of the two; Goods Sorter is the calmer one — a pantry to organise rather than a race to clear — and for players who unwind by putting things in order, that calm is exactly the point.

Download Goods Sorter on Google Play

The bottom line: Goods Sorter earns its trending spot with a simple, satisfying idea — drag, match, clear, repeat — executed with bright, readable charm. The fiddly placement and frequent ads are genuine annoyances, and the download is chunky for a casual puzzle. But as a free, Everyone-rated game that turns tidying shelves into a genuinely absorbing puzzle, it is one of the easiest recommendations in this week's trending charts: download it, take your time with the tricky boards, and enjoy the moment the last messy shelf finally falls into place.
