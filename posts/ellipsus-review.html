Ellipsus Review: Write Without Limits (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Ellipsus is a refreshingly old-fashioned writing app in the best sense: a clean, distraction-free editor for stories, novels and fan fiction, with real collaboration, draft versioning and offline writing — and a firm promise of no generative AI and no tracking of your words. It is still young on Android, so expect a few rough edges, but for writers who want their manuscript, not a chatbot, in the centre of the screen, it is already a serious option.

Ellipsus, from developer Ellipsus, arrived on Google Play on 22 September 2026 and has already reached the 10,000+ installs bracket, with a 4.8-star rating from 586 ratings on the Play header checked for this review. Its current version is 1.1.8, updated 21 October 2026, and it requires Android 10 and up. Google Play rates its content Everyone, with the interactive element Users Interact — which makes sense, because this is an app built around sharing your writing with other people. It sits in the Productivity category, and AppBrain's developer page puts its tracked downloads at around 40,000, with the app highly ranked in several countries in its first weeks. That is a fast start for a writing tool that does not shout about AI at all.

The pitch is simple, and it is stated right at the top of the store listing: write without limits, online or offline, with a collaborative writing tool built by writers, for writers. Whether you are working alone, swapping chapters with beta readers and editors, or co-authoring something longer, Ellipsus gives you one place for your documents, your drafts and your collaborators. There is no generative AI in the app, no creepy tracking, and, in the developer's words, no compromises on privacy. Your words stay yours. For writers who have watched every other writing tool bolt on an AI assistant, that stance alone is the headline.

How it works

Ellipsus is organised around documents, drafts and folders, with no artificial limits on how many of each you create. Start a document, write in a clean editor with the usual formatting controls, and when you want to try a different direction, spin off a draft instead of overwriting your good copy. Drafts can be compared and merged back into the main document when you are happy with them, which is exactly how novelists and fan-fiction writers actually work — try the scene two ways, keep the better one, and never lose the other version. The first store screenshot shows this in action: a Chapter 5 document with three drafts alongside it, including one marked as merged.

Writing on the go is a first-class feature, not an afterthought. You can write, edit and organise without an internet connection, and your changes sync when you are back online. That matters for a writing app: the best writing time is often on a train, on a plane, or in a café with unreliable Wi-Fi, and Ellipsus is built for exactly those moments. Built-in writing sprints and timers help you set a goal — a word count, a time block — and stay focused until you hit it, rather than drifting into another app.

Why it is trending

Ellipsus is trending because it answers a very current frustration. Writers are tired of tools that read, summarise or rewrite their work, and of editors that treat a manuscript as training data. Ellipsus takes the opposite position — no generative AI, no prying eyes — and puts that promise front and centre, alongside practical features that writers actually asked for: offline writing, version history, spellcheck, and a direct export route to AO3, the fan-fiction archive, which reviewers single out as a favourite feature. One reviewer writes that the no-AI stance was the main selling point, alongside the fonts, layout and colour themes; another says the app is incredibly easy to use for writing on the go.

The numbers back up the buzz. Reaching 10,000+ Play installs within about a month of launch, with a 4.8-star average, is a strong debut for a niche productivity app, and the AppBrain developer data showing around 40,000 tracked downloads suggests the Android app is only part of a wider audience that also uses Ellipsus in the browser. The app was also ranked among the top new free productivity apps in Sweden in October 2026. It is early days, but the trajectory is clearly upward.

Collaboration done properly

Where Ellipsus really earns its keep is collaboration. You can invite as many collaborators as you like, leave comments on a document, manage permissions, and track every change with version history. The third store screenshot shows a comment thread in action, with a collaborator asking whether a character's obsession is clear enough and suggesting an extra line — the kind of focused, chapter-level feedback that beta readers actually give. Permissions are granular: you choose a collaboration role for each person, so a beta reader does not get the same access as a co-author, and you can invite people by email or with a shareable link.

Publishing is handled with the same care. You can publish a piece with a Reader link, where readers can leave comments and give props, or share your fan fiction directly to AO3. You can also turn a favourite passage into a shareable, customisable Snippet — a styled image of your text, with a theme, image or GIF background — which is a neat way to tease a chapter on social media without pasting a wall of text. Documents stay private by default; you decide if, when and with whom to share them.

Key features

  • Unlimited documents, drafts and folders — no caps on your workspace, and unlimited collaborators.
  • Offline writing — write, edit and organise without a connection; changes sync when you are back online.
  • Real-time collaboration — comments, permissions and version history for beta readers, editors and co-authors.
  • Draft management with version control — try a scene a different way, then merge the winning draft back in.
  • Distraction-free editor — a clean, intuitive layout that keeps the focus on your words.
  • Colourful themes — customise your writing space to match your personality or your story's mood.
  • Import and export — import from .Docx or Markdown, export to PDF, and share Snippets of your favourite passages.
  • Writing sprints and timers — set goals and stay focused with built-in timers.
  • Reader links and AO3 export — publish for readers to comment on, or send fan fiction straight to AO3.
  • Privacy-first, no generative AI — no AI writing, no tracking of your work, documents private by default.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Strong debut: 4.8 stars from 586 ratings and 10,000+ installs in its first month on Play
  • Genuinely privacy-first: no generative AI, no tracking, documents private by default
  • Offline writing with sync — ideal for writing on trains, planes and patchy Wi-Fi
  • Thoughtful collaboration: granular permissions, comments, version history and draft merging
  • Writer-friendly extras: AO3 export, Reader links, Snippets, sprints and timers
Cons
  • Very young Android app (launched September 2026), so expect occasional rough edges
  • Some reviewers report lag and crashes on mobile, and no offline use in some flows, so results may vary by device
  • No chapter system yet — some writers want chapters rather than managing drafts of drafts
  • App size, ads status and in-app purchase details were not shown on the Play page checked for this review
  • Users Interact rating means collaboration features involve other people — check permissions before sharing a document

Pricing

Ellipsus is free to download on Google Play. The Play page checked for this review shows no in-app purchase price range and no contains-ads label, so the free tier is the only pricing the listing itself confirms — check any paid offer inside the app on its own merits before accepting it. Ellipsus also sells a paid Plus tier on its own website for writers who want unlimited documents and extra features, but that is separate from the Play listing, so treat the app's free version as the starting point and upgrade only if you hit its limits. For a writing tool with this much included free — unlimited documents, drafts, folders and collaborators — the free tier is already generous.

Download

Download Ellipsus on Google Play

If you write fiction, fan fiction or anything longer than a note, Ellipsus is an easy recommendation: a 4.8-star average, a privacy-first promise that actually means something, and collaboration tools that respect how writers really work.
