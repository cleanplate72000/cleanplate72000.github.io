iPhone Duo's Top Games Are Mostly Indies

Apple's editors have picked 20 standout games for the first folding iPhone, the iPhone Duo — and the list is dominated by indies and small studios, not the mobile industry's biggest publishers.

Why this matters: the early Duo games shelf shows who is actually building for a foldable iPhone right now: small teams and indie hits, while larger publishers appear to be waiting to see whether the device takes off.

iPhone Duo games: the key facts

  • The list: Apple's App Store editors picked 20 games for a "Great games on iPhone Duo" collection, calling them standouts on the first foldable iPhone. Only three of the 20 are Apple Arcade titles.
  • Smallest teams first: games published under individual developers' names include Tomasz Szykulski's Toastie, Klemens Strasser's Art of Flora: Cozy Jigsaw, Brian Corrieri's Battlefleet Duo, Hernan Rosario's FleetFold and Cromulent Labs' Marbles Unhinged: Folding Maze. The last three appear to have launched in the past few weeks.
  • Established indies: Playstack's Balatro, Playdigious's Planet of Lana, Blek, Pools, Phoenix 2, Waltz of the Wizard, Blackbox, Little Things Forever and No. 10: Full Confidence also made the list.
  • The big names: just three major mobile publishers feature — Gameloft's Asphalt Legends, Miniclip's 8 Ball Pool and Rovio's Angry Birds Dream Blast, all long-running mobile games.
  • Apple Arcade picks: Sneaky Sasquatch, Crossy Road Castle and Retrocade are the collection's three Arcade titles.
  • Prices vary: Art of Flora, Battlefleet Duo, FleetFold, Pools and Marbles Unhinged are free with in-app purchases, while Balatro costs £9.99, Planet of Lana £8.99, Blek £4.99 and Toastie £2.99, as reported.
  • The device: the iPhone Duo went on sale on October 23, from $1,999 (£1,999 in the UK).
  • Still to come: Hoyoverse's Zenless Zone Zero and Azra Games' UnGodly were demoed on the device at its reveal last month, suggesting larger publishers are watching rather than ignoring it.

Why it matters

For a brand-new, premium device, the games that are ready on day one shape what buyers actually do with it. Right now that job is being done largely by indies — from a grilled-cheese game to Balatro — rather than by the biggest free-to-play publishers. If the Duo sells, expect those bigger names to follow; if it stalls, this indie-led list may be all it gets for a while.

What to watch next

Watch for other publishers to announce Duo-optimised versions of their games, and for Apple to update its Duo collection as more titles arrive. For the publisher side of the story, see our Playdigious iPhone Duo games story, and browse the Top 100 apps page.
