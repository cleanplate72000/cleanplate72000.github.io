Sand Out: Color Puzzle Review (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Sand Out: Color Puzzle is a satisfying, good-looking sand puzzle with real momentum behind it: 380,000+ installs, 130,000 of them in the last 30 days, and a 4.2-star rating from more than 3,000 ratings. The picture-reveal boards are genuinely relaxing to clear, and the developer keeps updating it — version 1.7.1 landed on 21 October 2026. The catch is the advertising: several reviewers say ads pile up after the first few levels, and a few complain that later levels feel more like luck than logic. Worth downloading if you like calm sorting puzzles; less appealing if you hate ad breaks.

Sand Out: Color Puzzle, from developer CrazyLabs LTD, is one of the fastest-growing puzzle games on Google Play this month. It launched in June 2026, and independent tracker AppBrain — which pulls its figures from Google Play — rates it “Growing fast, Well rated”: 380,000 installs in total, 130,000 of them in the last 30 days, an average of about 4,300 new installs a day. That kind of growth for a four-month-old puzzle game is exactly why it earns a review here, and it is also why the review needs to be honest about the two sides of the game: a relaxing core idea, and an advertising model that some players clearly resent.

The developer pitches Sand Out as a physics puzzle: a “high-fidelity liquid simulation” where every grain of sand follows a gravity engine, and you carve paths, rotate portals and work around fans, teleporters and mixers to guide the flow into target buckets. The store screenshots tell a slightly more specific story about how a level actually looks. Each board is a pixel-art picture — a kiwi, a banana, a flamingo — buried under layers of coloured sand. Below the picture sits a queue of buckets, each holding one colour, and a conveyor showing how full each colour is. Your job is to send the right colour at the right time so the sand clears and the picture is revealed, piece by piece. It is less about frantic pouring and more about watching a hidden image emerge, which is where the relaxing, almost ASMR feel comes from.

The store art shows the idea at a glance: pixel-art pictures waiting to be uncovered, one colour at a time.

How it plays

Take Level 49, shown in the screenshots: the hidden picture is a kiwi, and the board is a field of green, orange and blue sand. The conveyor at the bottom shows one colour already at 58%, with the others at 8% and 0%, and a counter reading 5/5. The skill is in the order — pick the wrong bucket and you waste sand, stall the conveyor, or leave the one colour you need buried where you cannot reach it. Level 12 works the same way with a banana on a pink background, where a hand is shown tapping a yellow bucket into place, and Level 29 hides a pink flamingo under teal and pink sand, with one colour already at 91% and a counter at 3/5. The coin balance in the corner (1,025 coins on Level 12, 905 on Level 29, 3,125 on Level 49) shows the game’s soft currency ticking along as you play.

That structure — one picture per level, a limited bucket queue, a visible fill percentage for each colour — is what makes Sand Out different from a plain colour-sort game. You are not just matching colours; you are uncovering something, and the picture gives you a reason to finish a level that a bare progress bar never would. The developer promises hundreds of levels, new colour themes, and new mechanics over time, and the update history backs up the “kept fresh” claim: versions 1.5.0, 1.5.1, 1.6.1, 1.7.0 and 1.7.1 have all arrived since September 2026, with the latest on 21 October 2026.

Level 49: a kiwi emerges from the sand, with the conveyor showing one colour at 58% and a 5/5 counter.

Key features

  • Picture-reveal puzzle boards — clear coloured sand to uncover pixel-art images like a kiwi, a banana and a flamingo.
  • Physics-style sand flow, with the developer promising portals, fans, teleporters and mixers that change how each level behaves.
  • A bucket queue and conveyor system, so the order you pick colours matters as much as the colours themselves.
  • Hundreds of levels with new colour themes added in regular updates, most recently version 1.7.1 on 21 October 2026.
  • A calm, colourful presentation pitched as a relaxing, ASMR-style break rather than a high-pressure arcade game.
  • Coins earned as you play, shown in the corner of every board.

Level 12: a banana on a pink background, with a hand tapping the yellow bucket into the queue.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely satisfying reveal mechanic that is easy to understand in seconds; bright, varied picture boards; strong and recent momentum, with 130,000 installs in the last 30 days; frequent updates from an established developer, CrazyLabs; free to download and try.

Cons: reviewers repeatedly complain about advertising — one says the game switches to “an advertisement after every level” once the early levels are done, and another says ads are longer than the levels themselves; some players find later levels more luck-based than skill-based, with boards ending at 99% on the conveyor and no way to win; and at around 114 MB it is a fairly large download for a puzzle game.

Pricing and safety

Sand Out: Color Puzzle is free to download on Google Play, and the Play listing notes that it contains ads. AppBrain lists its content rating as Medium Maturity, so it is pitched at older players rather than young children, although the gameplay itself is a colourful sand puzzle with no gambling, adult or crypto-mining content. One honest flag from the Play listing’s data-safety section: the developer says the app may share financial information, app information and performance data, and device identifiers with third parties, and that data is encrypted in transit. Data practices can vary by region and age, and the developer may update them, so it is worth a glance at the store page before installing on a shared device.

On ratings, the picture is mixed in a useful way. The headline score is a healthy 4.2 stars from more than 3,000 ratings, and the five-star reviews praise exactly what you would hope for — “unique idea, beautiful graphics, fun for all ages” and “no ads and fun” from two players. The one- and two-star reviews, though, are consistent about the same two complaints: ads after almost every level, and levels that feel decided by luck. That split is the real story of this game — a strong, relaxing core, wrapped in an advertising model that will bother some players a lot and others barely at all.

Level 29: a pink flamingo under teal and pink sand, with one colour already at 91% on the conveyor.

Sand Out: Color Puzzle is worth a try if you like sorting and reveal puzzles and want something calm to play in short sessions — the picture boards are charming, the growth numbers show a lot of other players agree, and it costs nothing to test. Just go in knowing the trade-off: the ads are the most common complaint by a clear margin, and a few later levels may frustrate you more than they relax you.
