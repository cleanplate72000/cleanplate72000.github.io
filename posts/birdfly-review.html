BirdFly Review: Bird Nest Sorting (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. BirdFly: Sort Puzzle is a gentle, genuinely charming sorting game: spot the right birds in a mixed flock, send each species to its matching nest, and clear the board one nest at a time. It is easy to learn, relaxing to play, and free — just expect ads, a still-small rating base, and trickier layouts as the levels climb.

BirdFly: Sort Puzzle, from developer Casual Games For Fun, launched on Google Play in September 2026 and has already passed 50,000+ installs — with around 68,000 installs tracked in total, all in the last 30 days. On Google Play it holds 4.8 stars from about 1,600 ratings (4.75 in the latest AppBrain snapshot), and it is climbing the new-puzzle charts, reaching #4 Top New Free Puzzle in Iran with top-ten new-puzzle showings in Germany and France and a top-fifteen showing in Canada. For a game barely a month old, that is a fast start — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

Do not confuse this one with the older branch-sorting bird games on the store. BirdFly plays differently: there are no branches to stack birds on. Instead, each level shows you bird targets at the top of the screen — a species and the number you must collect — and a mixed flock below, held in bubbles. You find the matching species, select those birds, and send them to their nest. Fill a nest, and that part of the board clears, gradually opening up the whole flock. The developer's own pitch is simple: “Sort colorful birds into the right nests and clear every flock!”

How it plays

The loop is calm and readable. At the top of the screen, small nest icons show which species you need and how many of each — in the early levels, just a handful of pigeons or blackbirds. Below, the flock floats in bubbles of different sizes, sometimes blocked by locked or frozen areas. Tap the birds you need and they fly up to their nest; clear every target and the level ends. Later levels add many more species at once, plus special obstacles and blocked areas, so the same simple action starts to demand real observation: which birds do you clear first, and which blocked bubbles do you need to free before you can reach the birds behind them?

That “clear the right birds first” choice is the whole puzzle. Pick carelessly and the board stays clogged; pick well and the flock opens up in a satisfying cascade, nest by nest. There is no frantic timer in the screenshots, and the bright nature backgrounds — a lakeside branch, a sunny meadow — keep the mood relaxed even when a level gets busy. Level 120 in the store screenshots shows how far the idea scales: more than sixty birds on screen, three nest targets, and tools at the bottom including a magnifier, a clear option and a refresh option for when you are stuck.

Why it is trending

BirdFly is trending because it takes a familiar sorting idea and makes it instantly readable. Colour-sorting games are everywhere, but a game where you match whole birds to nests — with clear species, clear targets and a cute payoff every time a nest fills — reads in a single screenshot. Players seem to agree: one five-star reviewer calls it excellent for full relaxation and stress relief, while another praises how easily the birds can be told apart and says there are enough nests to keep coming back to. At roughly 3,200 installs a day over the last 30 days, it is growing the way only a genuinely easy-to-recommend puzzle game does.

It also helps that the game respects short sessions. Levels are quick, the goal is always visible at the top of the screen, and you can dip in for one level or a dozen. At about 99.5 MB, version 0.0.6 (updated 16 October 2026), it is a reasonable download for a polished puzzle game, and small enough to try on a whim.

Key features

  • Bird-and-nest sorting — match each species to its nest instead of stacking birds on branches.
  • Clear targets every level — the top bar shows exactly which birds you need and how many.
  • Cute, colourful bird characters — distinct species that are easy to tell apart at a glance.
  • Increasingly tricky layouts — more species, bigger flocks and blocked areas as you progress.
  • Special obstacles — locked and frozen areas that force you to plan which birds to clear first.
  • Helpful tools for stuck levels — later levels show magnifier, clear and refresh options.
  • Bright, relaxing nature settings — lakeside and meadow scenes with satisfying collection animations.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Fresh twist on bird sorting — nest targets instead of branch stacking, instantly easy to understand
  • Strong 4.8 stars from about 1,600 ratings, with all 68,000 tracked installs in the last 30 days
  • Distinct, readable birds and a clear goal every level — relaxing, not frantic
  • Real difficulty curve: early levels teach the loop, later levels demand genuine planning
  • Everyone content rating, and a quick level fits a coffee break or a commute
Cons
  • Contains ads, as expected from a free game — one reviewer complains ads arrive aggressively once certain bubble mechanics appear
  • Still a young game: about 1,600 ratings is a small base, so the 4.8 average may move as more players arrive
  • One game type done well, rather than a variety of modes — know what you are downloading
  • Later levels can feel genuinely hard, and blocked bubbles may frustrate players who prefer pure relaxation
  • About 99.5 MB — a fair size for a puzzle game, though not a tiny one

Pricing

BirdFly: Sort Puzzle is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The listing data checked for this review shows it as free with ads and no in-app purchase price range, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 0.0.6, updated 16 October 2026, and the download is about 99.5 MB — worth it if the bird-and-nest loop clicks for you.

Download

Download BirdFly on Google Play

If you like a sorting puzzle that rewards observation over speed, BirdFly is an easy recommendation: a fresh nest-matching twist, a 4.8-star average, and a loop — spot, send, clear — that stays satisfying well past the first few flocks.
