Cradle of Empires Review: Match 3 (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.2 / 5. Cradle of Empires: 3 in a Row is a match-3 game with a city-building twist: swap jewels to clear levels, then spend what you earn rebuilding an ancient Egyptian city, one wonder at a time. It is free, rated Everyone, and holds 4.6 stars from about 259,000 ratings — but the free ride comes with ads and in-app purchases that run up to $154.99, so treat the wallet warnings in this review as seriously as the praise.

Cradle of Empires: 3 in a Row, from developer Awem Games Limited, has been on Google Play for about a decade and is having a fresh surge in 2026. On its official Play listing it holds 4.6 stars from about 259,000 reviews, with Play showing the 5,000,000+ downloads bracket and an Everyone content rating. Independent tracker AppBrain puts total installs higher, at about 9.4 million, with roughly 35,000 of those in the last 7 days — about three times the week before — which is why it tops the trending puzzle charts this week. Its latest version on Play is 9.4.5, updated on 26 October 2026. That combination — a long-running game still growing fast, with a very large rating base — is why it earns a review this week.

All screenshots in this review are the developer's own promotional images from the official Google Play Store listing.

What Cradle of Empires does

You play as a wanderer helping Nimiru, a guide in an alternative ancient Egypt, lift a curse laid on the town by Menes, the evil priest of the demon Amrun. The loop has two halves that feed each other. First, the match-3 boards: swap neighbouring jewels to line up three or more, chain longer matches into bombs — a five-match in an angle makes a Fiery Ring bomb, a vertical five makes a Horizontal Lightning Bomb, a six-match makes a Superlightning Bomb, and a seven-match makes a Starry Ball Bomb — and clear each level's objectives. Second, the city: the rewards from those boards go into rebuilding a devastated civilisation, constructing iconic structures and, eventually, renovating seven wonders. There are 4,500+ levels across 6 different modes, so this is a game designed to be played for months, not finished in a weekend.

More than just matching

Three systems sit on top of the boards and give the game its staying power. Collections ask you to find hidden objects and artifacts in levels — Pharaoh's cups, genie lamps, evil spirits — and complete sets for rewards. Events rotate constantly, with names like Treasures of the Ancients, Jeweler's Dream, Genie's Chest and Portal of Worlds, plus a competitive Meows & Paws event; the newest update, which went live on 12 October, sends players to a Mayan jungle with new merge mechanics, 10 fresh collections and new quests. Guilds let you team up with other players or start your own guild with friends for exclusive event rewards. If you only ever play the boards, you will still have a solid match-3; the collections, events and guilds are what keep long-term players opening the app every day.

Key features

  • 4,500+ match-3 levels across 6 modes, with special bombs for 5, 6 and 7-chip matches
  • City building: rebuild an ancient Egyptian city and renovate seven wonders as you progress
  • Collections of hidden objects and artifacts, with rewards for completed sets
  • Rotating events, including Treasures of the Ancients, Jeweler's Dream and Genie's Chest
  • Guilds: team up with other players or create your own guild for exclusive rewards
  • A story campaign following Nimiru against the priest Menes and the demon Amrun
  • Regular updates — the latest, version 9.4.5, arrived on 26 October 2026

Pros and cons

Pros: a huge amount of content for a free download; the match-3 and city-building halves genuinely complement each other; a 4.6-star average across about 259,000 ratings; an Everyone rating; and a steady stream of events and updates after a decade on Play.

Cons: it contains ads, and in-app purchases run from $0.49 to $154.99 per item; reviewers report that event pop-ups get annoying and that the energy cost of levels does not always feel fair; and Play's data-safety section notes the app may collect personal and financial information, though it says no data is shared with third parties, data is encrypted in transit, and you can request deletion.

Pricing

Cradle of Empires is free to download on Google Play and contains ads. In-app purchases are optional and range from $0.49 to $154.99 per item — the top end is steep, so if you play with children around, it is worth checking that purchase approval is switched on for your device before handing it over. The core game, all 4,500+ levels included, is playable without paying; purchases mainly buy boosters, energy and event progress.

Download

Download Cradle of Empires on Google Play

If you like match-3 games but want something to build between boards, Cradle of Empires is an easy recommendation: a decade of content, a 4.6-star average, and a city that genuinely grows as you play. Go in expecting ads, event pop-ups and tempting purchases — set a spending boundary first — and you will find one of the most complete free puzzle games trending on Google Play right now. If you prefer a calmer, smaller puzzle with no city to manage, also read our Tiledom review, which trades empire-building for quiet tile matching.

Bottom line: a big, polished match-3 with a city-builder attached. The boards earn the download; the pricey in-app purchases are the thing to watch.
