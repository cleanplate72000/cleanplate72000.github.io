Dig Inc Review: Earn As You Dig (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.5 / 5. Dig Inc. is a tiny, instantly understandable digging game with a genuinely satisfying core loop: steer your excavator, smash ores, and push deeper for more cash and experience. It loses marks because the game around that loop is still thin — players report heavy ads, money that does not yet mean much, and a few glitches — so download it for quick digging sessions, not for a deep game.

Dig Inc., from developer KAYAC Inc., is a free arcade game with one of the simplest pitches on the Play Store: dig and earn. You drag to move your excavator through pixel-art earth, break ores to earn money and experience points, and aim to dig deeper into the ground. There is no story to follow and no complicated controls to learn — you point the drill, it digs, and the numbers go up.

Why review it now? Because it is climbing fast from a standing start. Dig Inc. launched on Google Play in October 2026, and tracking puts it at roughly 44,000 installs in total, all of them in the last 30 days — about 2,400 new players a day — with a best chart position of number 1 in Top New Free Arcade in the United States, plus number 1 spots in several other countries. The Play listing itself shows the 10,000+ installs bracket and a rating of about 4.0 stars from around 370 ratings. That mix — a brand-new game topping a chart while its players are clearly divided — is exactly worth an honest look before you install it.

What you actually do

Each run is a digging session measured in metres, minutes, and money. The screenshots show the whole loop at a glance: a timer ticking up top, your depth in metres, your drill level, and a cash counter that grows as you break things. Drag to steer the excavator, chew through soil and rock, and collect the ores you crack open. The deeper you go, the more interesting the ground gets — the screenshots show water pooling in the tunnels and a drill upgrade popping up as you pass 14 metres and reach drill level 2.

The appeal is the same one that makes digging games relaxing: there is always a little further to go, and every ore you break is a small reward. The pixel-art style helps too — the underground is dark, your drill is lit, and the tunnel you carve is the only bright path on screen, which makes each run feel like exploring rather than just grinding. It is the kind of game you can play for three minutes while waiting for something, which is probably why it has spread so quickly.

Key features

  • Drag-to-dig controls — steer the excavator anywhere underground; no buttons or complicated menus to learn.
  • Ore breaking for cash and experience — smash ores to earn in-game money and level up your drill.
  • Depth progression — dig deeper for tougher ground, new hazards like water, and better rewards.
  • Drill upgrades — level your drill up as you earn experience, shown in-game as Drill LV 2 and beyond.
  • Tiny download — about 22 MB, so it costs almost no storage and downloads in seconds.
  • Free to play — no upfront price; the game is free on Google Play, with ads inside.

The honest downsides

This is where the 4.0-star rating tells its real story, because the players are split. The most common complaint is ads: several reviewers call them forced or unskippable, and one says the game got noticeably more ad-heavy after an update. Others say the ads barely matter because you can play offline — so your experience may depend on how you play. The second complaint is thinness: more than one player says the game is, at the moment, just digging, with levelling up not changing much and the money not yet meaning anything. Players are asking for more levels, more maps down to the centre of the earth, a shop with more items, and a prestige system — all signs of a game people like but want more of.

There are glitches too. One player reports being launched to an absurd depth after water turned to obsidian, and another reports a run-ending glitch with no way to continue. One more note for the careful: the Play Store data safety section says the app may share location and financial information with third parties, that data is not encrypted, and that data cannot be deleted. That is the developer's own disclosure on the listing, and it is worth knowing before you install, even for a small free game.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Dead-simple, relaxing digging loop anyone can pick up in seconds
  • Chart-topping momentum: number 1 new free arcade game in the US
  • Tiny 22 MB download, free to play
  • Depth, drill upgrades and hazards give each run a small goal
  • Rated Everyone, so it is family-safe content
Cons
  • Many players report forced or unskippable ads
  • Progression feels thin: money and levels do not mean much yet
  • Very few levels and maps so far; players want much more content
  • Occasional glitches, including a run-ending one
  • Data safety flags: shares location and financial info, data not encrypted and cannot be deleted

Pricing

Dig Inc. is free on Google Play and contains ads. No in-app purchase prices are shown on the Play listing checked for this review, so the only cost of playing is your time — and your tolerance for ads. The current version checked is 007, updated on 26 October 2026, and the game needs about 22 MB of storage.

Who should download it?

If you like quick, casual arcade games where one more run is always tempting — the same crowd that enjoys Harbor Blast — Dig Inc. is an easy, free try: it is small, simple, rated Everyone, and genuinely relaxing for a few minutes at a time. If you hate ads, play it offline where you can, or wait: this is a young game, and its players are already asking the developer for more levels, a proper shop, and a reason for the money to matter. If those arrive, the score here goes up. For now, it is a fun hole to dig in — just do not expect a deep game yet.

Download Dig Inc. on Google Play

Bottom line: a tiny chart-topping digger with a satisfying core and a thin shell around it — great for short sessions, held back by ads and a lack of content, and worth watching as KAYAC Inc. builds it out.
