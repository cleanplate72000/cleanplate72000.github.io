Kettle Street Cosy Life Sim Out on Android

Kettle Street is out now on Android, bringing a cosy British life sim to phones — move into a bare flat in the small town of Kettleby, earn your keep with hands-on local jobs, and turn the place into a proper home.

Why this matters: the jobs are things you actually do — driving the parcel van, pulling pints, making butties — not buttons you press and wait on, and the town runs on the real calendar, so a start now lands straight in Halloween, Bonfire Night and Christmas.

Kettle Street: the key facts

  • Out now: on Android, as reported by Pocket Gamer on October 26, 2026.
  • The setup: you have just moved into a bare flat on Kettle Street in Kettleby, and Maureen at number 11 has already put the kettle on.
  • Work for real: drive the parcel van round town on the sat-nav, take the number 12 double-decker from stop to stop without jolting passengers, make butties at Café Rosa, pull pints at the Red Lion and serve poppadoms first at the Kettleby Tandoori.
  • Get better, earn more: do a job well and you move up from trainee, with better pay, perks and a reference for the letting agent.
  • Make it home: order furniture, repaint, change the floors and place every lamp and pot plant exactly where you want it, top up the electric at the corner shop and get broadband put in.
  • Seven neighbours: a street group chat links seven neighbours — help out and they warm to you, ignore them and they go a bit frosty.
  • Real calendar: Kettleby's year follows the actual date, so starting now brings Halloween pumpkins, Bonfire Night fireworks over the park, Christmas lights on the High Street and, Pocket Gamer jokes, a white Christmas.
  • Price model: free for your first week in Kettleby, then a single purchase unlocks the full game — Pocket Gamer does not state the purchase price, so this post does not guess one.

Why it matters

Life sims often let work fade into a timer while you wait. Kettle Street makes the working day the game: a badly driven bus or a badly poured pint is yours to own, and the payoff — a reference, a better wage, a flat that finally feels lived in — is earned rather than bought. That, plus a town that changes with the real seasons, gives a small Android launch more personality than many bigger releases.
