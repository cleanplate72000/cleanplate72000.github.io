Junk Cleaner Review: Storage Fix (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.0 / 5. Junk Cleaner is a simple, honest storage tidy-up tool: scan, review what it found, and delete only what you recognise. It will not magically double your phone's speed, but for a phone groaning under cache and duplicate photos, it is a genuinely useful free download — just check every item before you tap delete.

Junk Cleaner, from developer CardAccount, launched on Google Play in September 2026 and has already reached the 100,000+ install bracket. On Google Play it holds 4.3 stars from around 870 ratings, and independent tracker AppBrain reports roughly 400,000 installs in total — all of them in the last 30 days, at an average of about 16,000 installs a day. Its best chart positions so far are #2 in Top Free Tools in Canada and #3 in the United States, with top-ten Tools spots in Japan and Australia as well. For a plain-looking utility barely six weeks old, that is a remarkably fast rise — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The pitch is refreshingly modest. The developer describes it simply as a tool to “clean junk, remove unwanted files, and manage phone storage with ease” — no wild claims about boosting your processor, cooling your battery or making your phone “like new”. As you use your phone every day, cache data, temporary files and other unwanted items build up over time. Junk Cleaner scans your phone, shows you what it found, and lets you remove the items you no longer need. That review-before-you-delete step matters, and it is the first thing to praise about this app.

What it actually does

The home screen, shown in the app's own screenshots, puts the headline numbers front and centre: a big storage-used ring (54% in the promo shot), with storage and RAM usage underneath, and one large Clean button. Below that sit the extra tools: Network Traffic, Notification Cleaner, App Manager, Screenshot Cleaner, Duplicate Photo Cleaner and Video Cleaner. It is a sensible, uncluttered layout — you can see at a glance what is eating your storage, and you do not have to hunt through menus to reach the one tool you actually wanted.

The core Junk File Scan looks for cache files, temporary data and other unnecessary items, then presents the results for review before anything is deleted. That is the right order of operations for a cleaner: an app that silently deleted files would be dangerous, and one that only showed a scary “your phone is full!” warning without detail would be useless. Junk Cleaner sits in the middle — scan, review, clean selected files with a few taps.

The tools that earn their place

Large File Finder is arguably the most valuable feature here. Junk files are usually small and numerous; the real storage wins come from finding the handful of huge files — an old downloaded video, a forgotten offline map, a bloated game cache — and deciding what to keep. A finder that surfaces those big files quickly saves more space in two minutes than a dozen cache sweeps.

Duplicate Photo Cleaner is the second genuinely useful tool. The promo screenshot shows it grouping near-identical photos — two files at 1.1 MB, three at 1.9 MB, three more at 896.5 KB — with a “Keep Best” toggle and a running total (“Delete 9 (9.8 MB)”). Anyone who has burst-shot the same sunset twenty times knows how duplicates quietly eat storage, and a tool that groups them and lets you keep the best shot is a real time-saver. As always, glance through the group before deleting: near-duplicates are not always exact duplicates.

App Manager rounds out the set, listing installed apps by last used, size or install time — the screenshot shows Drive at 73.7 MB, Google TV at 48.6 MB and LINE at 269.7 MB — with an uninstall button at the bottom. Sorting by size is the quick way to spot the app you installed for one holiday and never opened again.

Why it is trending

Junk Cleaner is trending for the most boring, relatable reason possible: phones fill up. A 38 MB free tool that promises a quick tidy-up, with a clean interface and no account or setup fuss, is an easy install when the “storage almost full” warning appears. Its rise — 400,000 installs in about a month, #2 in Canada and #3 in the US for free Tools — looks like word of mouth plus chart momentum feeding each other: the higher it climbs, the more people try it.

It helps that the app is small and focused. At about 38 MB, version 1.2.0 (updated 13 October 2026), it does one job and does not pretend to be a security suite, a battery doctor and a VPN all at once. The Everyone content rating means it is an easy recommendation for a parent tidying a family phone, too.

Key features

  • Junk File Scan — finds cache files, temporary data and other unnecessary items for review.
  • Easy Cleanup — review scan results and clean selected files with just a few taps.
  • Storage Management — see what is taking up space and manage it more easily.
  • Large File Finder — locate the biggest files quickly and decide what to keep or delete.
  • App Overview — view installed apps and check storage usage in one place, with uninstall.
  • Duplicate Photo Cleaner — group near-identical photos, keep the best, delete the rest.
  • Screenshot and Video Cleaner — tidy screenshots and videos from the same home screen.
  • Simple design — a clean, user-friendly interface with no account or setup fuss.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Fast-growing hit: 4.3 stars, 100,000+ Play installs, #2 free Tools in Canada and #3 in the US
  • Review-before-you-delete flow — you see what will go before anything is removed
  • Large File Finder and Duplicate Photo Cleaner tackle the real storage hogs, not just cache
  • Small 38 MB download, free, with a clean and uncluttered interface
  • App Manager with size sorting makes uninstalling forgotten apps painless
  • Everyone content rating, and no account mentioned on the listing
Cons
  • Contains ads, so expect interruptions in a free utility
  • One reviewer reports the app left too many junk files behind — results will vary by phone
  • Cleaner apps cannot work miracles: cache rebuilds as you use apps, so gains are temporary
  • AppBrain lists 27 permissions — check what you are granting before your first scan
  • Small rating base (around 870 ratings), so the 4.3-star average is still early days
  • No in-app purchase prices are shown on the listing, so check for any paid offer in the app itself

Pricing

Junk Cleaner is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The Play listing shows a price of $0 and no in-app purchase price range, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 1.2.0, updated 13 October 2026, and the download is about 38 MB — small enough to grab even when storage is tight, which is rather the point.

Download

Download Junk Cleaner on Google Play

If your phone keeps warning you about storage, Junk Cleaner is a sensible first try: scan, review, delete only what you recognise, and use the Large File Finder before you obsess over cache. Do not expect it to make an old phone feel new — but as a free, focused tidy-up tool riding a genuine chart surge, it earns its place.
