City Transport Manager Review: Routes (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.3 / 5. City Transport Manager turns your own hometown into a playable transport network: place stops on real streets, run your first bus line, then grow it into buses, trams and metro. It is free, rated for everyone, and topping the new-simulation charts in Germany and the UK — but as a brand-new October 2026 release it still has rough edges, like stops occasionally landing on the wrong road.

City Transport Manager, from developer PeDePe GbR, asks a simple question: what would public transport in your city look like if you planned it yourself? Instead of a made-up map, you build on real streets, buildings and destinations — your hometown or somewhere new — connecting homes with workplaces, shops and leisure spots, one line at a time. It launched in October 2026 and is already moving fast: on Google Play it holds 4.4 stars from 648 reviews, and it has reached #1 in the Top New Free Simulation charts in Germany and the United Kingdom, with top showings in Spain, Poland, Austria, Switzerland and the Czech Republic. Tracked installs stand at roughly 23,000, all of them in the last 30 days — about 3,700 a day — which is exactly the kind of breakout a new simulation game needs to earn a review here.

The hook is personal. Most city builders hand you a blank grid; this one hands you the streets you already know. Your first job is humble — a single bus line with a handful of stops — but every decision matters: where the stops sit, how short the transfers are, and whether the bus you chose fits the demand. Get it right and passengers flow; get it wrong and they wait, crowd the vehicles and grumble. That loop — plan, watch, fix, expand — is the whole game, and it is surprisingly hard to put down.

How it plays

You start by founding your own transport company on a real map. Place stops, draw your first bus line and set it running, then watch real passengers use it: each one has a destination and will transfer between your lines to get there. Wait times, the routes they take and how full the vehicles get all feed into their satisfaction, so a line that looks good on paper can still fail if the buses are packed or the connections are slow.

Growth comes in layers. Your fleet starts with small minibuses and grows to articulated buses and double-deckers for the busy routes, while your depot expands alongside the company. Hit the milestones and you unlock trams and metro lines, which open up the high-demand corridors that buses alone cannot carry. Goals and stars feed research, new areas and upgrades, so there is always a next unlock pulling you forward.

Why it is trending

City Transport Manager is trending because it makes a niche fantasy instantly relatable: your streets, your buses, your decisions. Players are already recreating real networks — one reviewer writes about simulating the transport system in Pimpri-Chinchwad in India, and asks for dedicated bus lanes to match the real BRTS routes there. That is the sign of a simulation that has clicked: players stop playing a generic city and start rebuilding the one they live in.

The charts back it up. A game released in October 2026 does not reach #1 new simulation in Germany and the UK by accident, and the 4.4-star average across 648 reviews says the early players are mostly happy. The developer is also visibly listening: when a reviewer flagged stops landing on the wrong road, PeDePe GbR replied the same day asking for exact stop names so the bug could be checked. For a new release, that responsiveness matters as much as the feature list.

Key features

  • Real maps as the game world — start in your hometown or discover somewhere new, with real streets, buildings and destinations under your network.
  • Buses, trams and metro — begin with a single bus line and combine modes into one connected network as you unlock them.
  • A fleet that grows with demand — minibuses to articulated buses and double-deckers, with a depot that enlarges as your company grows.
  • Passengers with real destinations — they transfer between lines, and wait times, routes and full vehicles shape their satisfaction.
  • Run it like a business — set ticket prices, watch revenue against operating costs, choose operating hours, and balance an attractive service with a profitable operation.
  • Goals, milestones and stars — earn stars for research, new areas and upgrades, unlocking more vehicle categories and more of your city.
  • Automatic or tailored timetables — run the fleet automatically or tune it to the time of day.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • A genuinely fresh idea — your real city as the map, not a generic grid
  • Chart-topping debut: #1 Top New Free Simulation in Germany and the UK, 4.4 stars from 648 reviews
  • Satisfying growth curve from one bus line to a full multi-mode network
  • Business layer with ticket prices, costs and timetables adds real depth
  • Everyone content rating, and a responsive developer answering feedback the same day
Cons
  • New-release rough edges — reviewers report stops sometimes landing on the wrong road when streets run close together
  • No dedicated bus lanes yet, which frustrates players recreating real BRTS-style networks
  • At about 125 MB it is a bigger download than most casual games
  • Contains ads, and the in-app purchase details are light on the store page
  • One city at a time — deep rather than broad, so know what you are downloading

Pricing

City Transport Manager is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads with in-app purchases. The current version is 1.0.6, updated 22 October 2026, the download is about 125 MB, and it is also available on iOS. For a free simulation with this much depth, the price of entry is hard to argue with — just expect the odd ad between planning sessions.
