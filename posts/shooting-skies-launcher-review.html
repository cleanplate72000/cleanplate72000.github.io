Shooting Skies Launcher Review: Home (2026)

Quick verdict — 3.8 / 5. Shooting Skies Launcher is a fun, free way to give your Android home screen a combat-game makeover, with a playable shooter built right in — but it is a version 0.1 first release that takes over your home screen, so only install it if you are happy with that trade-off, and know how to switch back.

Shooting Skies Launcher, from developer Hero Fighting Games, is one of the fastest-rising new Tools apps on Google Play in October 2026. AppBrain's trending lists for 26 October 2026 put it at about 140,000 installs in total, all of them in the last 30 days — roughly 10,000 installs a day — with a 4.7-star rating from about 1,676 ratings on the live Play listing. It has reached #3 in the Top New Free Tools chart in the United States, #12 in the Top New Free chart in France and #10 in Brazil. That is a striking start for an app that only appeared on Google Play in October 2026, and it is easy to see why people are trying it: most launchers just change your wallpaper, while this one also gives you a game to play from the home screen itself.

The idea is simple. A launcher replaces the look and layout of your Android home screen — the wallpaper, the icons, the way you swipe between screens. Shooting Skies Launcher does that with a single, very specific theme: the world of its Shooting Skies combat game, all jets, soldiers, desert bases and dramatic skies. When you set it as your default home app, your phone's usual home screen is replaced by this themed one. The developer is clear about that in the Play description, and clear that you can return to your previous default home app at any time. That warning matters, because it is also the source of the app's most common complaints, which we cover honestly below.

Inside the launcher, the headline feature is the built-in Shooting Skies game, reached from the home screen. You take aim and bring down incoming missiles, drones, planes and other enemies with a variety of weapons, working through challenging levels across different combat environments, testing accuracy and reflexes as you go. It is not a separate download and it is not a demo tucked away in a menu — it is presented as part of the launcher experience, so the home screen itself becomes a place to play, not just a place to open other apps. For someone who already likes this kind of arcade shooter, that is a genuinely different reason to try a launcher.

Key features

  • Built-in Shooting Skies game: aim at missiles, drones, planes and other enemies with different weapons, across levels in varied combat environments.
  • Home screen wallpapers: choose from original wallpapers inspired by the Shooting Skies game, including 10 wallpapers highlighted in the version 0.1 update, from Home Settings or by long-pressing an empty space on the home screen.
  • Color themes: change the launcher's colour scheme from Home Settings to suit your own style, again reachable by long-pressing an empty home-screen space.
  • Icon shapes: customise the shape of your home screen icons so the whole screen matches the theme, rather than just the wallpaper.
  • Gestures: use swipe or tap gestures to open apps, reach settings or open the app drawer more quickly.
  • Easy return: the developer states you can go back to your previous default home app at any time — see the switching-back advice below before you install.

Customisation is where a launcher earns its keep, and on paper this one covers the basics well: wallpaper, colours, icon shapes and gestures are all in Home Settings, and the long-press shortcut for wallpaper and themes is the kind of small convenience that makes a launcher feel finished rather than fiddly. The wallpapers are the strongest part of the package. They are original game art, not generic stock images, and they carry the theme across the whole screen instead of sitting behind a mismatched set of icons. If you want your phone to look like it belongs in this game world, the launcher delivers that look immediately, without needing a separate wallpaper app, icon pack and theme app stitched together.

That said, this is still a first release, and it shows in three honest caveats. First, the theme is narrow by design. If you love the combat-game look, that is the point; if you want a calm, minimal or professional home screen, or a launcher with dozens of themes to switch between, this single-theme launcher will feel limiting very quickly. Second, it is a chunky download for a launcher at about 63.52 MB, and it contains ads, which is the usual price of a free themed app but worth knowing before you install. Third, AppBrain lists 26 permissions for the app, and the Play data-safety section says it may collect location, app activity and three other data types, shares no data with third parties, and encrypts data in transit. A launcher inevitably needs broad access to run your home screen, but if you are privacy-conscious, read that data-safety section on Play before you set it as your default.

The most important caveat is about the home-screen takeover itself. Some Play reviewers report home-menu errors after installing, and a few use strong words like "virus" because the app changed their home screen and they struggled to get their old layout back. Those are user reports, not a verified malware finding — the app is still listed on Google Play, and changing the home screen is exactly what a launcher is supposed to do — but the confusion is understandable, and it is a real risk for anyone who installs it without expecting it. Before you install, know how to switch back: on most Android phones, go to Settings, then Apps, then Default apps, then Home app, and pick your previous launcher. If you are not comfortable changing a default app, or you share the phone with someone who is not, do not set this as your default just to try the game.

Pros and cons

Pros: a genuinely different launcher idea, with a playable game built in; strong, original game-themed wallpapers; colour themes, icon shapes and gestures in one place; long-press shortcuts for wallpaper and themes; free, fast-growing and highly rated at 4.7 stars; the developer states you can return to your old home app at any time.

Cons: a single combat theme, so it will not suit everyone; a first release (version 0.1), so expect rough edges; replaces your home screen when set as default, and some users report trouble reverting; contains ads; a large 63.52 MB download for a launcher; broad permissions and location/app-activity collection to check in the data-safety section; combat theme and low-maturity rating mean it is for older children and up, not young kids.

Pricing

Shooting Skies Launcher is free to download on Google Play and contains ads. The Play listing data checked for this review does not highlight in-app purchases, so ads are the price of the free download. The current version is 0.1, updated 8 October 2026, the download is about 63.52 MB, and Google Play's data via AppBrain gives it a low-maturity content rating, meaning it is meant for older children and up rather than young children.

Download Shooting Skies Launcher on Google Play

So, should you download it? If you like arcade shooters and want your whole home screen to match that world, yes — this is a fun, free experiment, and the built-in game makes it more than another wallpaper app. Just install it with your eyes open: expect one theme, expect ads, check the data-safety section, and know the Settings route back to your old launcher before you set it as default. If you would rather customise your phone without changing your home screen at all, also read our Cool Clean review, a free Android tool that tidies your phone without taking over its home screen. For a bold first release with a clear idea and a few first-release rough edges, Shooting Skies Launcher earns its 3.8 out of 5.
