Knock Away Review: Cannon Smash Fun (2026)

Quick verdict — 4.5 / 5. Knock Away is a simple, satisfying physics puzzler: aim a cannon, fire a limited number of balls, and knock every crate, pot and barrel off its platform. It is relaxing, easy to pick up and free — just expect ads, and a challenge that grows quickly once iron barrels appear.

Knock Away, from developer Solid Games, launched on Google Play in August 2026 and has already passed 500,000+ installs on the store, with about 610,000 installs tracked in total and around 260,000 of those in the last 30 days. On Google Play it holds 4.6 stars from about 44,000 ratings, and it is climbing the puzzle charts, reaching #88 Top Free Puzzle in Russia with top-200 free-puzzle showings in Germany, Japan, Italy, India and several other countries. For a game barely two months old, that is a fast start — and the reason it earns a review here this week.

The idea could hardly be simpler, and that is the point. A cannon sits at the bottom of the screen. Above it, a platform is stacked with targets: light wooden crates at first, then heavier ceramic pots, then stubborn iron barrels. You have a limited number of cannonballs per level, shown in the corner of the screen. Knock every single target off the platform and the level is cleared. There is no timer, no rush and no complicated tutorial — just aim, angle and force, as the developer puts it: “Aim at the target and fire the shell. Experience ultimate stress relief!”

How it plays

Each level is a small physics puzzle disguised as a demolition game. Wooden crates fly off with a single well-placed shot, so early levels feel generous: one ball through the base of a stack can send the whole tower tumbling. Ceramic pots are heavier and need more hits, and iron barrels are the real test — sturdy, stubborn and best attacked at exactly the right point. As the levels climb, the stacks grow taller and more fortified, mixing materials on a single platform so you have to think about order as well as aim.

That order is where the game earns its puzzle label. A heavy barrel sitting on top of a wooden crate can crush the crate beneath it if you hit the right spot — or stay put and waste your shot if you do not. Sometimes a single ball in the perfect place triggers a chain reaction that clears half the platform in one glorious collapse. Other times you have to chip away piece by piece, spending your limited balls carefully. With no timer breathing down your neck, you can study the stack, line up the shot and only fire when you are ready, which is exactly why players describe it as relaxing rather than frantic.

Why it is trending

Knock Away is trending because its fun is visible in a single screenshot: a cannon, a wobbling tower and the promise of a satisfying crash. AppBrain's trending puzzle list for late October 2026 puts it at about 83,000 installs in seven days, up about 79% on the week before, and roughly 8,800 installs a day over the last 30 days. Players seem to agree with the numbers: one five-star reviewer calls it fun and relaxing and says it helps with accuracy of aim, while another praises how a stylus phone helps with precise shots. At about 76 MB, version 0.1.5 (updated 29 September 2026), it is a modest download for a polished physics game.

It also helps that the game respects short sessions. A level takes a minute or two, the goal is always the same — clear the platform — and you can stop after one level or play for an hour. The bright desert backdrops, with palm trees and a blue sky behind the platforms, keep the mood light even when a tower refuses to fall.

Key features

  • Cannon-and-target physics — aim, fire and watch crates, pots and barrels tumble off the platform.
  • Three materials, three challenges — light wood, heavier ceramic and stubborn iron, each needing a different approach.
  • Limited balls per level — every shot counts, so placement and order matter more than speed.
  • Chain reactions — one well-placed hit can topple half a stack, rewarding careful aiming.
  • No timer, no pressure — study each stack and fire only when you are ready.
  • Dozens of levels — wooden villages, ceramic workshops and iron fortresses, with taller and trickier stacks as you progress.
  • Cannon upgrades and new ammo — improve your cannon and unlock new ammunition types as you advance.
  • Everyone rating — Google Play rates the app's content as suitable for everyone.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Instantly understandable: aim, fire, knock everything off — no tutorial wall to climb
  • Strong 4.6 stars from about 44,000 ratings, with around 260,000 installs in the last 30 days
  • Real physics thinking: weight, stacking order and chain reactions, not just tapping
  • Relaxing pace with no timer — a level fits a coffee break or a commute
  • Everyone content rating, and a modest 76 MB download
Cons
  • Contains ads, as expected from a free game
  • Limited balls per level can frustrate players who prefer to experiment freely
  • One reviewer notes there is no option to turn off the end-of-level jingle
  • One game type done well, rather than a variety of modes — know what you are downloading
  • Later iron-barrel towers can feel punishing if you waste early shots

Pricing

Knock Away is free to download on Google Play, and it contains ads. The listing data checked for this review shows it as free with ads and no in-app purchase price range, so treat any paid offer you meet inside the app on its own merits and check it before accepting. The current version is 0.1.5, updated 29 September 2026, and the download is about 76 MB — worth it if the cannon-and-collapse loop clicks for you.
